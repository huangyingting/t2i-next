Aviary Noon frames three Chinese women in their sixties—retirees and lifelong pigeon fanciers—within the stark geometry of their rooftop coop in Beijing. Liqin, with her short, practical gray hair and deeply lined face, kneels on the sun-bleached concrete, her back to the lens, her wide-brimmed straw hat casting a sharp shadow across her loose, pale-blue shirt as she carefully slides a fresh water dish into a wooden coop. Her companion, Meixiu, stands beside the coop, her posture relaxed and watchful; her own hat shields her eyes as she looks up, her expression one of quiet, expert assessment. Beyond them, the third woman, Fang, leans against a weathered railing, her gaze following the precise, swirling flight of dozens of homing pigeons, their bodies catching the zenith sun as a silvery, kinetic cloud against a vast, saturated cyan sky. The composition is a deep, layered tableau, using the harsh noon light to carve every texture: the grain of the bleached wood, the weave of the cotton shirts, the leathery texture of the women’s hands and necks, and the brilliant white highlights on the pigeons’ wings. The finish is a contact-printed reversal film, its dense, charcoal blacks and milky, shoulder-less highlights compressing the tonal range into a graphic, nostalgic clarity, with a subtle, archival selenium toning lending a cool, metallic sheen to the concrete shadows and the women's silver hair.

The image was captured on large-format color negative, then printed via a dye-transfer process that intensifies the separation between the ultramarine, vermilion, and gold into almost lithographic purity, while leaving the concrete and fabric textures dense and granular.
