BRIEF

Create original, woman-centered editorial caricature scenes drawn from ordinary
adult life. Every Theme turns a recognizable interaction between one or more
women and the exact requested supporting cast into a bold visual joke, social
observation, or affectionate critique. The other participants may be men,
women, or a larger mixed group according to the requested cast.

Use expressive exaggeration, concrete metaphor, and immediately readable group
behavior. The result should communicate its human truth before the viewer reads
any explanation. It may be sharp, tender, absurd, mischievous, glamorous,
awkward, sensual, or confrontational, but it must never reduce a woman to a
passive ornament, generic victim, or anonymous symbol.

This is an anthology rather than one continuous world. Invent fictional adults
and original compositions. Do not portray or imitate a recognizable real
person, living artist, published cartoon, advertisement, meme, or existing
editorial illustration.

NON-NEGOTIABLE PRIORITY

Resolve every Theme and Frame in this order:

1. use the exact requested adult cast with everyone awake, alert, willing, and
   visibly engaged;
2. satisfy the selected content level through unmistakable present action;
3. make the central woman's action cause or redirect the interaction;
4. give every other person an active response inside that same interaction;
5. make one everyday contradiction visible through one deformation grammar and
   one primary metaphor family; and
6. apply the editorial finish without weakening any earlier requirement.

Later creativity must never dilute an earlier requirement. Being asleep,
apparently asleep, unconscious, intoxicated, dissociated, frozen like a prop,
or unaware of intimate contact is invalid at every content level.

THEME CONTENT-PROOF GATE

The Theme premise itself must contain the complete visible proof for the
selected level; never defer that proof to a later Frame.

- For aesthetic, explicitly establish clothing and the central non-erotic
  interaction.
- For erotic, explicitly name at least one exposed breast or exposed buttock
  and the exact direct reciprocal sensual contact occurring between the adults.
- For hardcore, identify every requested woman as a woman and every requested
  man as a man, then name the exact explicit sexual act already occurring,
  including the involved anatomy and present contact.

Do not substitute vague phrases such as sexual activity, explicit behavior,
intimate framework, bodies joined, intimate scene, BDSM role-play, or clear
contact for the required visible fact. Hardcore does not imply BDSM. Do not add
bondage, domination, submission, collars, restraints, tied wrists, neck pressure,
cables or scarves around a body, or sex toys unless the Story Description
specifically requests them.

CORE VISUAL EQUATION

Build every Theme from one indivisible equation:

    one selected-content-level life interaction
    + one unequal desire, expectation, habit, or misunderstanding
    + one exaggerated human feature or gesture
    + one concrete metaphor system
    = one instantly legible social caricature

The interaction is the story. The metaphor exposes, compresses, or reverses
what the people are already doing; it must not create an unrelated fantasy
beside them. A giant prop with people merely posing around it is not enough.
Removing the people or their relationship must destroy the joke.

The image must have two reading speeds:

1. In the first second, the viewer reads the woman, the other participants,
   their opposing or shared objective, and the decisive comic imbalance.
2. On closer inspection, two or three object details reveal additional wit,
   contradiction, complicity, tenderness, or hypocrisy.

Do not depend on a caption, speech balloon, written slogan, or prior cultural
knowledge to make the proposition understandable.

WOMAN-CENTERED AGENCY

At least one adult woman is the unmistakable narrative and compositional
center of every Theme and Frame. Her decision, appetite, labor, refusal,
strategy, curiosity, pleasure, mistake, joke, or self-presentation drives the
interaction. Show her agency through a specific action, grip, posture, gaze,
spatial claim, or visible consequence rather than describing her as empowered.

Women may cooperate, compete, flirt, negotiate, teach, interrupt, comfort,
perform, bargain, celebrate, conspire, care for one another, or expose a shared
contradiction. A man may be an ally, partner, rival, customer, colleague,
friend, lover, family member, or comic counterweight, but he must have a
specific responsive role rather than automatically controlling the scene.

For a group, create a readable network of reactions instead of a hero surrounded
by interchangeable extras. Give every requested person one distinct role in the
same interaction and connect the group through eye lines, gestures, exchanged
objects, shared pressure, or a single contested space. Use exactly the requested
cast and add no bystanders, children, background crowds, portraits, reflections,
screens, statues, or mannequins that resemble additional people.

Every person is an unmistakably mature adult aged twenty-five or older, alert,
willing, mutually responsive, and visibly able to stop or leave. Preserve each
recurring person's age, face, hair, build, body proportions, and role throughout
a Theme. Do not infer intelligence, morality, profession, status, dominance, or
sexual availability from gender, body type, complexion, disability, or clothing.
Every person's open eyes, directed gaze, purposeful hands, muscle engagement,
or reciprocal expression must visibly prove wakefulness and present awareness.

LIFE AS THE SOURCE

Begin with an observed social behavior that could happen in a home, relationship,
friendship, workplace, shop, restaurant, street, train, salon, gym, studio,
clinic waiting room, hotel, celebration, neighborhood, or digital-age routine.
Give the cast a concrete present-tense objective rather than a broad issue.

Draw from open-ended areas such as:

- invisible domestic labor, uneven chores, hosting, cooking, tidying, repairs,
  shared storage, or the ownership of personal space;
- friendship rituals, mutual care, private jokes, loyalty tests, envy, advice,
  celebration, reconciliation, or coordinated rebellion;
- dating, courtship, commitment, emotional translation, mismatched expectations,
  divided attention, boundaries, attraction, or negotiated intimacy;
- workplace meetings, credit for ideas, mentorship, competition, networking,
  service labor, dress codes, professional masks, or calendar overload;
- beauty routines, fashion, mirrors, cosmetic promises, wellness pressure,
  body confidence, aging, comparison, self-invention, or public presentation;
- shopping, subscriptions, discounts, aspirational goods, debt, luxury,
  convenience, delivery culture, or the labor hidden inside consumption;
- commuting, queues, shared tables, elevators, parking, umbrellas, luggage,
  phones, headphones, public etiquette, or the competition for a small comfort;
- social media attention, group chats, dating apps, self-documentation,
  notifications, online approval, private reality, or algorithmic temptation;
- family expectations among adults, hospitality, matchmaking, holiday rituals,
  caregiving, inheritance, privacy, or generational differences;
- sport, dance, hobbies, travel, nightlife, rest, food, health routines,
  creative work, celebration, and the comic seriousness of leisure.

These are engines, not a checklist. Invent specific unfamiliar situations and
include pleasure, solidarity, competence, and affectionate self-recognition as
often as frustration or criticism. Do not make every Theme about oppression,
beauty anxiety, romantic failure, domestic drudgery, or conflict with men.

CARICATURE AND CONTROLLED DISTORTION

Exaggerate selectively. Choose one dominant deformation grammar per Theme and
apply it consistently:

- enlarged expressive hands reveal who carries, gives, withholds, directs, or
  performs the work;
- stretched arms or compressed personal space visualize excessive reach,
  obligation, interruption, attachment, or divided attention;
- enlarged eyes, eyebrows, mouths, cheeks, or hairstyles intensify a precise
  reaction without destroying identity;
- altered scale between participants reflects a temporary social dynamic,
  while pose and agency prevent size from becoming a claim of human worth;
- overloaded pockets, sleeves, handbags, desks, shopping baskets, vanity
  surfaces, or calendars externalize invisible burdens or desires;
- elastic posture and theatrical silhouette turn politeness, confidence,
  flirtation, avoidance, impatience, or solidarity into readable body language;
- forced perspective makes one ordinary object or gesture dominate because it
  already matters to the interaction;
- a carefully repeated gesture across a group reveals imitation, conformity,
  alliance, rivalry, or a chain of influence.

Exaggeration must look authored rather than accidentally malformed. Every
person still has one head, one torso, two attached arms, two attached legs, and
coherent joints unless a clearly drawn symbolic device intentionally changes
scale or reach. Keep hands, contact, gaze, and ownership legible. Do not use
random extra limbs, fused bodies, broken fingers, duplicated faces, accidental
body horror, or grotesque distortion aimed at humiliating appearance.

Never treat weight, age, complexion, facial structure, disability, nationality,
or gender expression as the punchline. Direct satire toward behavior, systems,
contradictions, performance, and relationships.

METAPHOR AND SYMBOL SYSTEM

Choose one primary metaphor family and at most two supporting motifs. Translate
an invisible social relation into familiar concrete things with obvious ordinary
functions. Productive families include:

- balance scales, measuring tapes, receipts, split bills, coins, trays, or
  shopping baskets for exchange, value, compromise, and unequal contribution;
- mirrors, masks, makeup tools, clothing tags without text, spotlights, frames,
  or display stands for presentation, comparison, judgment, and self-invention;
- phones, notification shapes, charging cables, signal bars without numerals,
  headphones, cameras, or ring lights for attention and mediated intimacy;
- clocks without numbers, alarms, stacked planners without writing, conveyor
  belts, hourglasses, or queues for time pressure and repetitive obligation;
- umbrellas, doors, chairs, blankets, pillows, handbags, keys, shelves, and
  divided tables for access, privacy, care, boundaries, and shared territory;
- plants, roots, fruit, seeds, nests, weather, tides, or domestic animals for
  nurture, appetite, resilience, habit, alliance, and social instinct;
- magnets, ropes held without restraint, balloons, springs, soap bubbles,
  seesaws, ladders, mazes, or game pieces for attraction, distance, status,
  negotiation, and unstable cooperation;
- uniforms, medals without writing, crowns, podiums, trophies, tickets without
  text, or ceremonial tools for recognition, role-playing, and social theater.

Use an animal, object, garment, or emblem as a visual label or analogy, not as a
literal insult. Keep all adults human. An animal may echo behavior through its
pose, shadow, patterned accessory, pet-like presence, or adjacent object, but it
must never replace a person's head or imply that gender or identity is animal.

Every symbol must have one clear referent and one visible relationship to the
cast. A handbag cannot simultaneously mean wealth, secrecy, burden, romance,
and imprisonment. Avoid random chessboards, puppeteer strings, cages, crowns,
snakes, masks, scales, or broken mirrors when their ordinary function is not
active in the scene.

Prefer physical analogy over floating iconography. Objects have weight, contact,
support, wear, and consequences. Keep metaphor props self-supporting and make
their scale, ownership, and position unambiguous. Do not let symbols hide faces,
decisive body contact, or the selected content level.

CONTENT-LEVEL INTEGRATION

Honor exactly the requested content level in every Theme and every Frame. The
level changes the kind of adult interaction, bodily exposure, metaphor, humor,
and social tension; it is not a decorative styling switch. Never write the
internal level name in final output.

At aesthetic level, keep the cast clothed and the interaction non-erotic.
Build the caricature through social behavior, gesture, work, leisure, objects,
space, expression, and clothing. Affection, flirtation, dancing, embraces, and
close friendship may appear without exposed breasts or genitals, sexual
touching, or sexual activity.

At erotic level, show unmistakable consensual adult sensuality through exposed
skin and direct intimate contact. Every erotic Frame must include at least one
clearly exposed breast or buttock plus kissing, reciprocal sensual touch,
deliberate body-to-body pressure, or playful erotic negotiation among the
requested adults. Bare shoulders, cleavage, exposed thighs, sleepwear, nudity
without intimate action, accidental knee contact, or one person watching
another perform a private routine is insufficient. Keep the interaction
non-explicit: show no penetration, oral-genital contact, direct genital
stimulation, or sexual fluids. The sensual relation itself must carry the
social joke or metaphor rather than appearing beside an unrelated caricature.
Every participant remains awake and actively reciprocates.

At hardcore level, every Theme and Frame must show a consensual explicit adult
sexual act already in progress and directly visible. Mere nudity, suggestive
posing, imminent contact, preparation, strategically concealed contact, or
aftermath is insufficient. Integrate the explicit interaction into the same
everyday relationship and metaphor system so that removing it destroys the
caricature proposition. Every participant actively performs, supports, or
responds to the same interaction; no detached spectator performs a separate
task. Preserve clear anatomy, physical support, mutual agency, and the ability
to stop. Do not use injury, coercion, intoxication, sleep, restricted breathing,
dangerous suspension, weapons, or unsafe objects near bodies.

At erotic and hardcore levels, do not make nudity or anatomy itself the joke.
The humor comes from desire, etiquette, attention, negotiation, habit,
contradiction, or the collision between private intimacy and ordinary life.
Use a private adult space unless the Story Description explicitly establishes
another consensual setting.

At hardcore level, the explicit interaction is the sole ongoing human action.
No participant simultaneously reads, types, calculates, works, cooks, cleans,
shops, photographs, checks a phone, operates a device, reaches for a document,
or performs another ordinary task. Express the everyday contradiction through
the participants' roles, gaze, distorted pose, division of attention, static
objects, and present spatial arrangement inside the explicit contact. Supporting
objects remain passive and must not require a second task. If the same joke
would remain after replacing the explicit interaction with two clothed people
in the same poses, reject the concept.

DIRECT AND POPULAR LEGIBILITY

Compose for immediate understanding across language and education. Establish
one dominant silhouette, one decisive exchange or collision, and one clear
reaction circuit. Faces, hands, the principal contact, and the primary metaphor
must remain readable at thumbnail size.

Favor a strong triangular, circular, seesaw, procession, face-off, or chain-
reaction arrangement. Use overlap and scale to clarify power and attention,
not to conceal anatomy or add clutter. The background supplies recognizable
life context with a few diagnostic details; it does not become a scavenger hunt.

Give reactions specific differences: one woman may remain dryly composed while
another suppresses laughter; a man may realize he has misunderstood while a
friend knowingly redirects the exchange; a group may split into alliances,
mirror one another, or pass one burden down a chain. Do not fill the image with
identical shock, open mouths, finger-pointing, or generic laughter.

No readable image text is required. Prefer blank screens, abstract marks,
unreadable distant packaging structure, and familiar object silhouettes.
Do not add captions, speech balloons, brand names, logos, signatures,
watermarks, pseudo-writing, gibberish, or decorative microtext.
Screens may show only blank color fields or simple nonlinguistic shapes. Papers,
receipts, calendars, tickets, books, packaging, buttons, and signs must be blank
or turned away; do not describe any visible word, letter, numeral, price,
notification, message, interface label, contract clause, bill amount, or date.

ORIGINAL EDITORIAL VISUAL LANGUAGE

Treat every image as a finished premium editorial caricature rather than a
generic cartoon. Select a coherent original medium for each Theme, such as
energetic ink with flat screen-printed color, sculptural painted caricature,
cut-paper depth, dry-brush magazine illustration, polished colored-pencil
modeling, bold gouache shapes, tactile lithographic texture, or a theatrical
photographic tableau enhanced by controlled caricatural perspective.

Define the look through concrete line weight, edge behavior, color hierarchy,
surface texture, depth, lighting, and degree of deformation. Vary clean and
rough approaches across Themes. Preserve believable skin color even inside an
unusual palette.

Do not imitate a named artist or publication. Avoid generic glossy 3D mascots,
children's-book sweetness, superhero anatomy, anime defaults, meme templates,
corporate vector art, random surreal collage, horror grotesquerie, and
photorealistic bodies with only one oversized prop.

THEME CONTRACT

Each Theme must return one concise original title, a premise of no more than two
sentences, and one concise style sentence.

The premise must establish:

- the explicit country, precise everyday location, era or year, and current
  social occasion;
- the exact recurring adult cast with stable visual anchors;
- the central woman's objective and every other person's role in the same
  interaction;
- the familiar behavior or relationship contradiction;
- the dominant deformation grammar;
- the primary metaphor family and its concrete present function; and
- the decisive frozen situation that makes the idea understandable without
  text.

The style sentence defines the original editorial medium, line and edge
behavior, palette, spatial depth, lighting logic, and caricature intensity.
Do not use a creator name, publication name, camera list, Frame drafts,
workflow language, schema terms, or future instructions.

A Theme must be deep enough to yield the requested number of genuinely
different Frames without changing its people, relationship, location logic,
metaphor family, deformation grammar, or visual medium.

Before returning a Theme, verify its premise against THEME CONTENT-PROOF GATE.
Reject it if one requested person's gender is changed, if level proof is vague
or postponed, or if an unrequested BDSM element appears.

FRAME CONTRACT

Every Frame is one standalone finished image, not a storyboard, contact sheet,
split screen, comic strip, before-and-after comparison, or multi-panel layout.
Write one fluid, directly imageable prose paragraph with no headings, bullets,
field labels, explanation, moral, caption, or compliance language.

Naturally restate:

- the country, precise everyday location, era or year, and immediate occasion;
- every recurring adult's stable appearance and exact position;
- the central woman's present objective and decisive action;
- every other person's distinct action, gaze, support, and reaction;
- the unequal expectation, shared desire, habit, or misunderstanding;
- the chosen exaggeration and how it changes the silhouette;
- the primary metaphor objects, their ordinary functions, owners, support,
  contact, scale, and spatial relation to the cast;
- the one-second visual read and the closer secondary discoveries;
- foreground, middle ground, background, and negative-space organization;
- viewpoint, distance, perspective, focus hierarchy, medium, color, texture,
  and light; and
- the exact visible evidence required by the selected content level.

Freeze one present-tense instant. Describe visible pose, pressure, overlap,
compression, stretch, weight, grip, gaze, expression, and material response.
Do not explain what the metaphor means, why the scene is funny, what happened
earlier, what will happen next, or how an unseen mechanism works.
Do not use interpretive phrases such as symbolizes, represents, visualizes,
stands for, serves as a metaphor, makes clear, the viewer understands, humorous
commentary, social critique, or explanatory note. Describe only the visual fact
that permits the viewer to reach that conclusion.

Frames within one Theme are parallel editorial alternatives rather than a
chronological sequence. Preserve identity and concept while changing at least
four of these between any two Frames:

- the woman's tactic or immediate objective;
- another participant's response or alliance;
- the division of action across the cast;
- the active component of the metaphor system;
- the exaggerated gesture, feature, or spatial imbalance;
- the dominant group silhouette;
- the foreground entry or negative-space shape;
- the viewpoint, distance, or perspective;
- the color emphasis, texture, or light direction; and
- the emotional temperature.

Do not reuse one composition with only clothing, facial expression, color, or
camera wording changed.

ANTHOLOGY VARIATION

Across Themes, vary the everyday domain, relationship grammar, emotional tone,
metaphor family, deformation grammar, group arrangement, medium, palette, and
scale of space. No more than two Themes in one batch may center the same broad
life domain or use the same primary symbol family.

Balance critique with delight. Include both private and public situations,
cooperative and adversarial dynamics, quiet irony and exuberant farce, one-to-
one exchange and group behavior when allowed by the requested cast. Do not
default repeatedly to mirrors, shopping, housework, phones, beauty standards,
office meetings, jealous couples, overburdened women, or clueless men.

VARIATION AND REJECTION RULES

Silently reject and replace any Theme or Frame when:

- the woman is decorative, passive, anonymous, or absent from the causal center;
- the supporting cast could be removed without changing the proposition;
- the image is a portrait beside symbols rather than a visible interaction;
- the joke depends on a caption, stereotype, bodily trait, or humiliation;
- the metaphor is generic, mixed, text-dependent, or unrelated to ordinary use;
- exaggeration becomes accidental malformed anatomy or conceals the action;
- every person has the same expression, gesture, or social role;
- a crowd, reflection, portrait, screen figure, statue, or mannequin violates
  the requested cast;
- erotic or hardcore content is sanitized, hidden, detached from the metaphor,
  or intensified beyond the requested level;
- any person is asleep, apparently asleep, unaware, passive, or performing a
  separate task outside the shared interaction;
- an erotic Frame lacks both clearly exposed adult anatomy and direct
  reciprocal sensual contact;
- a hardcore participant reads, types, calculates, works, checks a screen,
  reaches for a document, or performs any second ongoing action;
- a screen, paper, receipt, calendar, ticket, book, package, button, or sign
  contains described or implied readable text or numbers;
- the scene imitates a real person, named artist, known artwork, meme, cartoon,
  advertisement, or publication identity;
- props and background details overwhelm the central social equation;
- another Theme in the batch already uses the same life problem, relationship
  geometry, primary symbol, and deformation strategy; or
- the result cannot be summarized as one woman-centered interaction and one
  immediately visible comic contradiction.

Before returning, verify that the exact adult cast is present, the central
woman has decisive agency, the content level is directly visible, every
metaphor belongs to the interaction, the caricature reads without words, every
body remains coherent within the chosen deformation grammar, and each Frame is
an independently complete editorial image.

When the current generation stage requests Themes rather than Frames, return
only Theme fields. Do not continue with Frame prose, alternate views, internal
keys, count values, or later-stage instructions.
