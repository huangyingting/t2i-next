WOMAN-CENTERED ACTION-METAPHOR EQUATION

Build every concept from one indivisible equation:

    one selected-level everyday interaction
    + one unequal desire, expectation, habit, or misunderstanding
    + one woman-driven group action
    + one visible force chain
    + one concrete metaphor system
    = one instantly legible social caricature

At least one woman is the narrative and compositional center. Her decision,
appetite, labor, refusal, strategy, curiosity, pleasure, mistake, joke, or
self-presentation drives the event. Show agency through action, grip, posture,
gaze, spatial claim, or visible consequence, not through the word empowered.

Give every adult a distinct role in the same action. Connect the group through
eye lines, reciprocal gestures, bodily pressure, shared weight, or contested
space. The supporting cast must be load-bearing: removing anyone changes the
proposition.

Create one closed causal force chain:

1. The central woman's movement applies one visible directional force.
2. Another participant receives, resists, redirects, or amplifies that force.
3. Their bodies load, tilt, stretch, compress, open, close, turn, or balance the
   monumental primary metaphor.
4. The changed mechanism visibly redirects pressure or consequence into every
   remaining participant.

At least two adults directly touch, grip, brace, block, pull, push, load, or
counterweight the same primary structure or one another. The primary metaphor
must visibly change mechanical state because of the bodies. It is not scenery,
a nearby giant prop, or an explanation of an unrelated action. At erotic and
hardcore levels, the intimate action itself supplies the force; removing it must
destroy both the mechanism and the joke.

Use one blunt spatial opposition such as push versus pull, high versus low,
inside versus outside, weight versus lift, approach versus retreat, appetite
versus scarcity, or command versus resistance. The composition must reveal who
wants what, who benefits, who carries the cost, and how the others respond.

MONTAGE CARICATURE, STYLING, AND ANATOMY

The first read is a forceful photographic caricature, not a normal lifestyle
scene with one large prop. The signature technique is deliberate scale mismatch:
cut, resize, reposition, and overlap photographed body regions so selected parts
are visibly out of proportion. Give the central woman the dominant distortion
and every other adult one different secondary distortion. Choose one coherent
distortion design per person:

- an enlarged or compressed photographic head creating an unequal head-to-body
  ratio;
- one enlarged, compressed, widened, or narrowed facial anchor such as the
  brows, eyes, nose, jaw, cheeks, or mouth;
- an expanded or pinched torso contrasted against normal limbs;
- one arm, hand, leg, or foot enlarged, shortened, or elongated against the
  rest of the body;
- a deliberately mismatched upper-body and lower-body scale; or
- the entire head-to-foot figure scaled against the other adults.

Use visible hard photographic cut seams, overlaps, and scale jumps where the
selected regions meet. The distortion must look deliberately assembled, not
caused by lens perspective, and must change the silhouette at thumbnail size.
Keep one recognizable person with one head, one torso, two arms, two hands, two
legs, and two feet. Every resized part remains visibly connected by purposeful
paper overlap. Do not add or duplicate anatomy, float body parts, fuse people,
break fingers, melt flesh, expose gore, or create accidental body horror.

Use distortion to amplify performed pettiness, hypocrisy, vanity, greed,
smugness, cowardice, status hunger, or another socially ugly behavior until it
becomes humorous and biting. Make the distorted feature participate in the
person's action: a grasping person may have an enormous working hand, a vain
person an inflated chest and tiny supporting legs, or a hypocrite a rigidly
wide smile that conflicts with narrowed eyes. Do not distort ethnic features,
disability, age, complexion, body weight, gender expression, or other inherent
traits as the joke.

Do not exaggerate breasts, buttocks, genitals, or tongue. Do not use sexual
anatomy as a visual punchline. Avoid cute doll proportions, sparkling anime
eyes, school-age styling, or any face that stops looking mature and photographic.

Separate from the one montage distortion, give every adult one
unified amplified personal look containing all three elements:

- a bold outfit silhouette or dramatic garment construction;
- a sculptural, unusually voluminous, or sharply graphic hairstyle; and
- one oversized worn accessory such as eyewear, earrings, necklace, brooch,
  belt, or footwear, with no display, writing, numbers, or device interface.

Coordinate outfit, hair, and accessory with the person's social role, action,
and primary metaphor. They must read at thumbnail size as one authored look,
not three unrelated gimmicks. Styling does not count as an extra anatomical
distortion or supporting symbol. At erotic or hardcore level, displaced
garments retain their exaggerated visual identity and the accessory stays
securely attached without obscuring contact or tangling bodies.
