BRIEF

Create original, woman-centered editorial caricature scenes drawn from ordinary
adult life. Every Theme turns a recognizable interaction between one or more
women and the exact requested supporting cast into a bold visual joke, social
observation, or affectionate critique. The other participants may be men,
women, or a larger mixed group according to the requested cast.

Use expressive exaggeration, concrete metaphor, and immediately readable group
behavior. The result should communicate its human truth before the viewer reads
any explanation. It may be sharp, tender, absurd, mischievous, glamorous,
awkward, sensual, or confrontational, but it must never reduce a woman to a
passive ornament, generic victim, or anonymous symbol.

This is an anthology rather than one continuous world. Invent fictional adults
and original compositions. Do not portray or imitate a recognizable real
person, living artist, published cartoon, advertisement, meme, or existing
editorial illustration.

ENGLISH OUTPUT AND REAL-PERSON PHOTOMONTAGE LOCK

Write every Theme title, premise, style field, and complete Frame in English,
regardless of the requested output language. English is required because the
final prose is sent directly to an image model and must preserve the exact
caricature, symbol, and flat real-person photomontage vocabulary.

Use English-only ASCII characters. Transliterate names and places with
unaccented Latin letters. Before returning either stage, replace curly quotes
with straight ASCII quotes, replace long dashes with a plain hyphen, and rewrite
any remaining non-ASCII character. Write personal names in normal Title Case,
never all uppercase; reserve uppercase words exclusively for exact visible copy
declared at the end of a Frame.

Begin every Frame with this exact ASCII phrase:

Flat satirical photomontage assembled from photographs of real adult performers:

Continue immediately with the complete dominant visual icon. This opening is
part of the image description, not a heading. The first sentence must establish
the impossible body silhouettes and monumental metaphor before location,
clothing, anatomy detail, atmosphere, or supporting props.

NON-NEGOTIABLE PRIORITY

Resolve every Theme and Frame in this order:

1. use the exact requested adult cast with everyone awake, alert, willing, and
   visibly engaged;
2. satisfy the selected content level through unmistakable present action;
3. make the central woman's action cause or redirect the interaction;
4. give every other person an active response inside that same interaction;
5. make the contradiction dominate the outer silhouette through unmistakably
   impossible caricatural deformation;
6. assign opposing social roles concrete animal, object, or emblem labels and
   bind them into one monumental metaphor system; and
7. plan one exact, readable English label pair for the two opposing social
   forces and eliminate all unplanned writing; and
8. apply the editorial finish without weakening any earlier requirement.

Later creativity must never dilute an earlier requirement. Being asleep,
apparently asleep, unconscious, intoxicated, dissociated, frozen like a prop,
or unaware of intimate contact is invalid at every content level.

PLANNED ENGLISH LABEL SYSTEM

Apply this gate before inventing a Theme. Every Theme must choose one meaningful
pair of opposed English labels that names the two social forces already visible
in the caricature, such as WORK and CREDIT, MINE and OURS, GIVE and TAKE, SEEN
and UNSEEN, or WAIT and NOW. These examples demonstrate brevity and opposition;
invent a pair specific to the Theme rather than copying them by default.

Each label contains one or two short words using only uppercase ASCII letters A
through Z and spaces. The pair must be grammatical, correctly spelled, easy to
read, and conceptually parallel. Do not use names, digits, dates, prices,
punctuation, abbreviations, brands, logos, slogans longer than two words, or
wordplay that depends on ambiguous spelling.

Lock the exact pair during Theme generation. End the Theme premise with exactly
this ASCII pattern, replacing the placeholders with the selected copy:
`Locked image labels: FIRST LABEL | SECOND LABEL.` Do not spell either label
anywhere else in the premise or style. The Frame must preserve this exact pair
without synonym, tense, number, spelling, or order changes.

Give each label one large, simple physical carrier integrated into the primary
metaphor: opposite scale pans, two halves of an umbrella, two plain badges, two
large garment panels, two blank blocks, two sides of a door, or another pair of
clean matte surfaces. Each carrier contains only its one declared label. Keep
it free of borders, seals, icons, decorative marks, secondary lines, textures,
grids, handwriting, fine print, numbers, or nearby glyph-like details.

Text is part of the visual power map, not an explanation placed beneath it.
Position each label directly on the force it names. The bodies, deformation,
symbols, and metaphor must still communicate the contradiction without words,
while the English pair makes the opposition faster to recognize.

No other readable or pseudo-readable content may appear. If the scene includes
a screen, receipt, bill, form, ledger, document, newspaper, magazine, notebook,
book, menu, calendar, ticket, tag, stamp, certificate, map, clock, measuring
tool, package, or garment that would normally contain writing, remove that
object and replace it with a non-textual physical symbol. The two clean label
carriers are the only text-bearing surfaces permitted. Never add a button word,
interface, document layout, printed record, serial number, packaging panel,
barcode, QR code, fine print, or third label.

CORE VISUAL EQUATION

Build every Theme from one indivisible equation:

    one selected-content-level life interaction
    + one unequal desire, expectation, habit, or misunderstanding
    + one exaggerated human feature or gesture
    + one concrete metaphor system
    = one instantly legible social caricature

The interaction is the story. The metaphor exposes, compresses, or reverses
what the people are already doing; it must not create an unrelated fantasy
beside them. A giant prop with people merely posing around it is not enough.
Removing the people or their relationship must destroy the joke.

The image must have two reading speeds:

1. In the first second, the viewer reads the woman, the other participants,
   their opposing or shared objective, and the decisive comic imbalance.
2. On closer inspection, two or three object details reveal additional wit,
   contradiction, complicity, tenderness, or hypocrisy.

Do not depend on a caption, speech balloon, written slogan, or prior cultural
knowledge to make the proposition understandable.

SATIRICAL CARICATURE HARD GATE

The first read must be a forceful real-person photographic caricature, not a
naturalistic lifestyle photograph or drawn cartoon with one slightly enlarged
hand. Every Theme and Frame must contain all of the following:

1. The central woman's real photographed face is visibly caricatured through
   expression, hairstyle, and selective photographic enlargement or compression
   of at least two adult features such as brows, cheeks, mouth, jaw, or nose.
2. One of her hands, feet, arms, legs, torso silhouettes, garments, or carried
   burdens is roughly two to four times its ordinary visual scale as an enlarged
   photographic cutout joined cleanly to the full-body photo, changing the
   silhouette without using foreground perspective.
3. At least one interacting adult receives a different equally obvious
   deformation that reveals that person's role or response.
4. One impossible spatial exaggeration compresses the whole relationship:
   extreme size imbalance, elastic distance, seesaw balance, concentric crowding,
   a body folded by obligation, or another instantly readable distortion.
5. One monumental metaphor structure occupies roughly one third of the image
   and physically organizes the people rather than sitting beside them.
6. At least two smaller symbols from the same metaphor family reinforce the
   primary comparison without introducing a second idea.

Describe these six facts explicitly in every Theme premise and every Frame.
Words such as exaggerated, enlarged, stretched, cartoonish, or symbolic do not
satisfy the gate by themselves: state what is enlarged, by how much it dominates
the silhouette, what the paired deformation is, and how the metaphor structure
positions the cast.

The deformation is intentionally impossible but photographically assembled.
Enlarge, compress, repeat, or stretch selected fragments of genuine adult
photographs while preserving skin texture, recognizable faces, correct limb
attachment, pose readability, contact, and the selected content-level action.
Show crisp cut edges or subtle physical collage seams so the flat construction
is deliberate. A single long arm, a mildly large hand, ordinary bodies beside
an oversized prop, rubbery digital warping, or visible silicone prosthetics is
an automatic rejection.

All faces must remain unmistakably mature. Build adult caricature from prominent
brows, noses, jaws, cheek tension, expression lines, narrowed or asymmetrical
eyes, compressed mouths, and forceful posture. Do not enlarge every head and
both eyes into cute rounded proportions. Reject smooth doll faces, huge sparkling
eyes, tiny noses, childlike head-to-body ratios, school-age styling, innocent
chibi expressions, painted faces, or any figure who could be mistaken for a
teenager.

DRAW FIRST, DESCRIBE SECOND

Never invent the picture while writing the final prose. Privately construct and
judge the complete real-person photomontage first; only after the assembled
picture works, translate that fixed picture into words. Do not output the
private construction process.

For every Theme, silently draw at least three radically different thumbnail
compositions using only black shapes and one accent color. At this stage:

1. place the central woman's caricatured head and full-body silhouette;
2. place every other requested adult as a different readable silhouette;
3. exaggerate each side's face and body before adding clothing or anatomy detail;
4. turn the primary metaphor into the floor, furniture, enclosure, balance,
   machine, creature, weather system, or other structure holding the composition;
5. attach each side's visual label and exactly two supporting symbols;
6. block one decisive gesture and one closed circuit of reactions; and
7. reserve only a few simple shapes for the everyday setting.

Choose the thumbnail whose social relation remains clearest when viewed small,
in silhouette, without color, text, facial detail, or explanation. Discard a
thumbnail if removing the monumental metaphor leaves an ordinary scene, if the
bodies return to normal proportion, or if the joke lives only in a small prop.
Measure the primary metaphor against the canvas, not against a tabletop object:
it must fill thirty-five to fifty percent of the entire image area, cross or
support the principal bodies, and remain the first or second largest shape.

For every Frame, silently assemble one complete final photomontage from
separately photographed real adult performers before drafting any sentence.
Fix every photographic cutout, enlarged body fragment, hard edge, physical

Do not add a new idea, object, gesture, expression, or meaning while writing.

The first Frame sentence must show the whole visual icon at once: caricatured
cast, impossible group silhouette, selected-content-level interaction, and
monumental metaphor in one composition. Later sentences move inward to faces,
visual labels, supporting symbols, material contact, and finally the compact
print treatment. If a sentence must explain what a symbol means, stop, redesign
the silent picture so the comparison becomes visible, and only then rewrite.

VISUAL LABEL AND POWER MAP

Treat the people as opposing or cooperating forces in a compact power map. Give
each side a concrete visual label: an animal analogy, familiar object, garment,
tool, emblem, material, or repeated shape attached to that person's role. The
labels must be visible in silhouette or color before close details are read.

Animal comparison may appear as an expressive animal companion, cast shadow,
mask-like facial echo, headdress, patterned garment, hybridized caricatural
feature, or object shaped like the animal. It may sharpen appetite, caution,
loyalty, vanity, patience, opportunism, territorial behavior, mimicry, or group
instinct. Preserve the adult's recognizable human identity and agency, and aim
the comparison at behavior rather than gender or appearance.

The primary symbol system must act like a stage mechanism: a balance carries the
people at unequal heights; a magnet bends bodies and possessions toward it; a
nest envelops one alliance while excluding another; a crown becomes a shared
seat; a handbag becomes a bottomless labor pit; an hourglass compresses the
group into opposing chambers; an umbrella becomes a protective wall. These
illustrate mechanisms, not reusable scene templates. Invent an equally direct
original system.

If the scene can be mistaken for an ordinary lifestyle illustration at
thumbnail size, reject it. If the viewer needs prose to know who wants what,
who benefits, who carries the cost, or why the arrangement is contradictory,
strengthen the visual labels, size hierarchy, and metaphor geometry.

Use one blunt visual contest that survives without context: push against pull,
weight against lift, appetite against scarcity, spotlight against shadow,
shield against intrusion, hoarding against sharing, command against resistance,
or another equally direct opposition. The primary metaphor must perform that
contest physically. Subtle hesitation, private thoughts, deferred assistance,
polite ambiguity, or a small symbolic object on an ordinary table is not enough.

WOMAN-CENTERED AGENCY

At least one adult woman is the unmistakable narrative and compositional
center of every Theme and Frame. Her decision, appetite, labor, refusal,
strategy, curiosity, pleasure, mistake, joke, or self-presentation drives the
interaction. Show her agency through a specific action, grip, posture, gaze,
spatial claim, or visible consequence rather than describing her as empowered.

Women may cooperate, compete, flirt, negotiate, teach, interrupt, comfort,
perform, bargain, celebrate, conspire, care for one another, or expose a shared
contradiction. A man may be an ally, partner, rival, customer, colleague,
friend, lover, family member, or comic counterweight, but he must have a
specific responsive role rather than automatically controlling the scene.

For a group, create a readable network of reactions instead of a hero surrounded
by interchangeable extras. Give every requested person one distinct role in the
same interaction and connect the group through eye lines, gestures, exchanged
objects, shared pressure, or a single contested space. Use exactly the requested
cast and add no bystanders, children, background crowds, portraits, reflections,
screens, statues, or mannequins that resemble additional people.

Every person is an unmistakably mature adult aged twenty-five or older, alert,
willing, mutually responsive, and visibly able to stop or leave. Preserve each
recurring person's age, face, hair, build, body proportions, and role throughout
a Theme. Do not infer intelligence, morality, profession, status, dominance, or
sexual availability from gender, body type, complexion, disability, or clothing.
Every person's open eyes, directed gaze, purposeful hands, muscle engagement,
or reciprocal expression must visibly prove wakefulness and present awareness.

CARICATURE AND CONTROLLED DISTORTION

Exaggerate decisively. Choose one dominant deformation grammar per Theme and
apply it consistently:

- enlarged expressive hands reveal who carries, gives, withholds, directs, or
  performs the work;
- stretched arms or compressed personal space visualize excessive reach,
  obligation, interruption, attachment, or divided attention;
- enlarged eyes, eyebrows, mouths, cheeks, or hairstyles intensify a precise
  reaction without destroying identity;
- altered scale between participants reflects a temporary social dynamic,
  while pose and agency prevent size from becoming a claim of human worth;
- overloaded pockets, sleeves, handbags, desks, shopping baskets, vanity
  surfaces, or stacked containers externalize invisible burdens or desires;
- elastic posture and theatrical silhouette turn politeness, confidence,
  flirtation, avoidance, impatience, or solidarity into readable body language;
- forced perspective makes one ordinary object or gesture dominate because it
  already matters to the interaction;
- a carefully repeated gesture across a group reveals imitation, conformity,
  alliance, rivalry, or a chain of influence.

Exaggeration must look authored rather than accidentally malformed. Every
person still has one identifiable head and torso with attached limbs, but scale,
length, volume, facial proportion, and perspective should be boldly impossible.
Keep hands, contact, gaze, and ownership legible. Do not use random extra limbs,
fused bodies, broken fingers, duplicated faces, accidental body horror, or
grotesque distortion aimed at humiliating appearance.

Never treat weight, age, complexion, facial structure, disability, nationality,
or gender expression as the punchline. Direct satire toward behavior, systems,
contradictions, performance, and relationships.

Use an animal, object, garment, or emblem as a visual label or analogy, not as a

decisive body contact, or the selected content level.

CONTENT-LEVEL INTEGRATION

Honor exactly the requested content level in every Theme and every Frame. The
level changes the kind of adult interaction, bodily exposure, metaphor, humor,
and social tension; it is not a decorative styling switch. Never write the
internal level name in final output.

At aesthetic level, keep the cast clothed and the interaction non-erotic.
Build the caricature through social behavior, gesture, work, leisure, objects,
space, expression, and clothing. Affection, flirtation, dancing, embraces, and
close friendship may appear without exposed breasts or genitals, sexual
touching, or sexual activity.

At erotic level, show unmistakable consensual adult sensuality through exposed
skin and direct intimate contact. Every erotic Frame must include at least one
clearly exposed breast or buttock plus kissing, reciprocal sensual touch,
deliberate body-to-body pressure, or playful erotic negotiation among the
requested adults. Bare shoulders, cleavage, exposed thighs, sleepwear, nudity
without intimate action, accidental knee contact, or one person watching
another perform a private routine is insufficient. Keep the interaction
non-explicit: show no penetration, oral-genital contact, direct genital
stimulation, or sexual fluids. The sensual relation itself must carry the
social joke or metaphor rather than appearing beside an unrelated caricature.
Every participant remains awake and actively reciprocates.

At hardcore level, every Theme and Frame must show a consensual explicit adult
sexual act already in progress and directly visible. Mere nudity, suggestive
posing, imminent contact, preparation, strategically concealed contact, or
aftermath is insufficient. Integrate the explicit interaction into the same
everyday relationship and metaphor system so that removing it destroys the
caricature proposition. Every participant actively performs, supports, or
responds to the same interaction; no detached spectator performs a separate
task. Preserve clear anatomy, physical support, mutual agency, and the ability
to stop. Do not use injury, coercion, intoxication, sleep, restricted breathing,
dangerous suspension, weapons, or unsafe objects near bodies.

At erotic and hardcore levels, do not make nudity or anatomy itself the joke.
The humor comes from desire, etiquette, attention, negotiation, habit,
contradiction, or the collision between private intimacy and ordinary life.
Use a private adult space unless the Story Description explicitly establishes
another consensual setting.

At hardcore level, the explicit interaction is the sole ongoing human action.
No participant simultaneously reads, types, calculates, works, cooks, cleans,
shops, photographs, checks a phone, operates a device, reaches for a document,
or performs another ordinary task. Express the everyday contradiction through
the participants' roles, gaze, distorted pose, division of attention, static
objects, and present spatial arrangement inside the explicit contact. Supporting
objects remain passive and must not require a second task. If the same joke
would remain after replacing the explicit interaction with two clothed people
in the same poses, reject the concept.

DIRECT AND POPULAR LEGIBILITY

Compose for immediate understanding across language and education. Establish
one dominant silhouette, one decisive exchange or collision, and one clear
reaction circuit. Faces, hands, the principal contact, and the primary metaphor
must remain readable at thumbnail size.

Use a poster-like hierarchy: caricatured faces first, impossible body silhouette
second, monumental metaphor third, two supporting symbols fourth, sparse life
context last. The central figures and metaphor occupy at least four fifths of
the composition. Reduce the background to two or three diagnostic objects and
one broad color field. Do not spend visual attention on realistic toiletries,
furniture inventory, skin highlights, atmospheric steam, decorative clutter,
or cinematic set dressing.

Photograph each real performer frontally or in a shallow side pose with the same
eye-level viewpoint, neutral focal scale, crisp focus, and uniform shadowless
studio light. Assemble every cutout onto one exact orthographic poster plane;
there is no foreground, middle distance, room corner, floor recession, or
background architecture. Use overlap and relative cutout size only. Do not use
overhead, high-angle, low-angle, wide-angle, selfie, or strong foreshortening.
Do not enlarge a hand or foot by placing it close to the lens; enlarge its
photographic cutout while keeping it on the same poster plane as the face.

Favor a strong triangular, circular, seesaw, procession, face-off, or chain-
reaction arrangement. Use overlap and scale to clarify power and attention,
not to conceal anatomy or add clutter. The background supplies recognizable
life context with a few diagnostic details; it does not become a scavenger hunt.

Give reactions specific differences: one woman may remain dryly composed while
another suppresses laughter; a man may realize he has misunderstood while a
friend knowingly redirects the exchange; a group may split into alliances,
mirror one another, or pass one burden down a chain. Do not fill the image with
identical shock, open mouths, finger-pointing, or generic laughter.

IMAGE-TEXT GATE

Every Frame must visibly include the Theme's exact pair of English labels and
no other text. Each label contains one or two short words using only uppercase
ASCII letters A through Z and spaces. Render both horizontally in bold,
high-contrast block capitals. Every letter must be at least one twentieth of
the image height, and each complete label must occupy at least one eighth of the
image width. Both labels sit in the same focal plane as the people and remain
fully unobstructed.

Before the final image-text passage, never spell either locked label and never
write any other all-uppercase word. Refer only to the first plain label carrier
and second plain label carrier without quoting their copy. The exact two strings
appear for the first and only time after the final two ASCII colons.

Quarantine every exact visible string until one dedicated image-text passage at
the absolute end of the Frame. Write exactly two entries. For each entry, state
its physical carrier, exact image location, minimum letter height, horizontal
orientation, type weight, text color, and plain background color, add an ASCII
colon, write the exact uppercase English copy, and end with an ASCII semicolon.
Use the literal characters `: ` before the copy and `;` after it. Never enclose
copy in quotation marks or repeat either string anywhere else in the Frame.
Do not add an IMAGE-TEXT heading, Text label, Copy label, or any other prefix.
Begin directly with the first carrier description. The Frame's final character
must be the second entry's ASCII semicolon.

Every other surface must contain zero letters, words, digits, pseudo-letters,
glyph-like marks, fine print, barcodes, or QR codes. Do not ask the image model
to invent any environmental copy. If a text-bearing object is present, its
entire visible typographic content must be one of the two declared labels;
otherwise simplify it into a plain, mark-free shape.

Visible copy confirms a relation already obvious from bodies, symbols, and
composition. If removing the words makes the satire unintelligible, redesign
the picture rather than adding more text.

REAL-PERSON PHOTOMONTAGE LANGUAGE

Treat every image as a premium physical editorial photomontage assembled from
studio photographs of real adult performers. Each adult is photographed
separately against the same neutral light, then cut out with a crisp continuous
edge and mounted onto one matte poster surface. Enlarged hands, faces, limbs,
objects, and symbols are resized photographic cutouts, not illustrations,
prosthetics, or perspective tricks.

Use four dominant colors for clothing, object cutouts, and the flat background
while preserving full natural color and photographic tonal variation in human
skin and hair. Separate silhouettes with contrast, narrow physical cut-paper
seams, one-to-three-millimeter contact shadows, and empty space rather than
drawn outlines. Pores, fine hair, fabric weave, object texture, and pressure at
contact points remain photographic, while the assembled world has zero
atmosphere, floor depth, wall depth, or lens recession.

Do not imitate a named artist or publication. Avoid generic glossy 3D mascots,
children's-book sweetness, superhero anatomy, anime defaults, meme templates,
corporate vector art, random surreal collage, horror grotesquerie, illustrated
people, painted skin, silicone body extensions, deep cinematic locations,
glossy fashion imagery, and ordinary lifestyle photographs with only one
oversized prop.

THEME CONTRACT

Each Theme must return one concise original title, a premise of no more than two
sentences, and one concise style sentence.

Complete the private thumbnail selection before writing the title or premise.
The premise records the already selected visual solution; it must not brainstorm
alternatives, explain intentions, or propose what a later Frame could contain.

The premise must establish:

- the explicit country, precise everyday location, era or year, and current
  social occasion;
- the exact recurring adult cast with stable visual anchors;
- the central woman's objective and every other person's role in the same
  interaction;
- the familiar behavior or relationship contradiction;
- both adults' different dominant deformations and the impossible full-group
  silhouette they create;
- each side's concrete visual label;
- the monumental primary metaphor, its two supporting symbols, and their
  concrete present functions, including that the primary structure fills
