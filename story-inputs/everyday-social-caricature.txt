BRIEF

Create original, woman-centered editorial caricatures from recognizable adult
life. Turn one familiar social interaction into a bold visual joke, observation,
or affectionate critique. The image must communicate through people, action,
exaggerated anatomy, and physical metaphor before any words are read.
This is an anthology of fictional adults and original compositions. Never copy
a real person, artist, published cartoon, advertisement, or existing image.
PRIORITY
Resolve every Theme and Frame in this order:
1. exact script-supplied cast, East Asian identity, adulthood, and consent;
2. the central woman's decisive action and every other adult's active response;
3. forceful real-person photographic caricature and impossible silhouette;
4. one monumental metaphor physically joined to the interaction;
5. flat editorial photomontage with no environmental depth;
6. exactly two readable English labels and no other writing.
Reject and redesign rather than weakening an earlier priority.
EAST ASIAN CAST LOCK
Every visible person is a fictional adult aged twenty-five or older and must be
one of these identities only:
- mainland Chinese;
- Taiwanese;
- Hong Kong Chinese;
- Japanese;
- South Korean; or
- Singaporean Chinese.
Set each Theme in mainland China, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Japan, South Korea, or
Singapore. Use plausible local names written in ASCII Latin letters. State each
person's allowed identity in the Theme premise and preserve it in every Frame.
Never make nationality, ethnicity, accent, or physical appearance the joke.
Cast quantity comes only from the generation request. Use exactly the requested
female count, male count, and total. Never infer, default, demonstrate, or
hard-code a cast quantity in this brief. Omit a zero-count gender phrase.
Add no bystander, reflection, portrait, mannequin, child, background silhouette,
or human-like figure.
ENGLISH OUTPUT
Write every Theme field and complete Frame in English regardless of requested
language. Use only ASCII U+0020 through U+007E plus line breaks. Replace curly
quotes, accented letters, and long dashes before returning. Write personal names
in normal Title Case; reserve all-uppercase words for the two visible labels.
Begin every Frame exactly:

VISUAL EQUATION
Build one indivisible picture from:
- one current everyday interaction;
- one unequal desire, expectation, habit, burden, or misunderstanding;
- one decisive action led by a woman;
- two or more visibly caricatured human silhouettes;
- one familiar object transformed into a monumental metaphor; and
- one pair of opposed social forces.
The people are the story. The metaphor exposes, compresses, reverses, carries,
or obstructs what their bodies are already doing. Removing either the human
interaction or the metaphor must destroy the joke.
The central woman's action applies a visible force. Another adult receives,
resists, assists, redirects, or amplifies it. Their bodies physically change the
metaphor, and its changed state produces the next reaction. Do not compose an
intimate tableau beside an unrelated giant prop.
ACTION-OBJECT COUPLING GATE
Every requested adult belongs to one closed mechanical circuit around the same
primary object. Each person's hand, limb, torso, weight, or received pressure
directly touches that object or another body already loading it.
For every adult, describe in one sentence the directional action, exact contact
point, and immediate visible change it causes in the object's position, angle,
compression, opening, tension, balance, or shape. The changed object must return
force into the next adult and alter that person's pose or reaction.
No one performs a separate task, handles a supporting symbol, or gestures beside the mechanism. Supporting symbols stay attached to the mechanism and remain
passive. Reject any composition if removing one adult's action leaves the
object's state and every other pose unchanged.
SATIRICAL CARICATURE HARD GATE
The first read must be an unmistakable photographic caricature, not ordinary
lifestyle photography and not a drawn cartoon.
Every Theme and Frame includes:
1. the central woman's photographed face caricatured through mature expression,
   hairstyle, and selective enlargement or compression of at least two facial
   features such as brows, cheeks, mouth, jaw, or nose;
2. one attached hand, foot, arm, leg, torso silhouette, garment, hairstyle, or
   carried burden at roughly two to four times ordinary visual scale;
3. at least one interacting adult with a different, equally obvious deformation;
4. one impossible group arrangement such as extreme size imbalance, elastic
   distance, seesaw balance, concentric crowding, or a body bent by obligation;
5. one monumental metaphor occupying thirty-five to fifty percent of the canvas
   and physically organizing the people; and
6. exactly two smaller symbols from the same metaphor family.
State what is enlarged, compressed, repeated, or stretched and how it changes
the outer silhouette. Words such as exaggerated or cartoonish are insufficient.
Do not use lens proximity, foreshortening, a mildly large hand, rubbery digital
warping, or an oversized object beside normal bodies as substitutes.
Describe scale through silhouette comparison: a giant hand is as wide as its
owner's torso, a stretched arm crosses another complete body, or an enlarged
head is wider than its shoulders while remaining on the same flat plane.
Keep every adult recognizable and anatomically connected. Preserve real skin,
hair, joints, hands, face identity, and readable contact. Never create detached
organs, floating body parts, duplicated limbs, melted anatomy, body horror, or
prosthetic-looking pieces. Do not use breasts, buttocks, genitals, or isolated
sexual anatomy as the exaggerated feature.
Faces remain unmistakably mature. Avoid giant sparkling eyes, tiny noses,
rounded childlike heads, anime proportions, doll faces, and youthful styling.
DRAW FIRST, DESCRIBE SECOND
Before writing each Theme, privately compose at least three radically different
black-shape thumbnails with one accent color. Place the central woman, every
other requested adult, the deformed silhouettes, the monumental metaphor, two
supporting symbols, and one reaction circuit before adding surface detail.
Choose the thumbnail whose relationship remains clearest when viewed small,
without text, color, or explanation. Reject it if normalizing the bodies,
removing the metaphor, or removing one requested adult leaves the same idea.
Before writing a Frame, privately assemble the finished photomontage from
separately photographed real adults. Fix all cutouts, resized fragments,
overlaps, grips, contact points, metaphor parts, label carriers, matte fields,
and empty shapes. Then describe only that finished image in visual scan order.
WOMAN-CENTERED AGENCY
At least one adult woman is the narrative and compositional center. Her decision,
labor, appetite, refusal, strategy, pleasure, mistake, or joke causes the visible
change. Show agency through action, grip, weight, gaze, posture, spatial claim,
or consequence rather than calling her empowered.
Every other requested adult has a distinct objective, physical action, gaze,
support, and reaction inside the same exchange. Nobody merely watches, waits,
poses, points, or decorates the scene. Aim satire at behavior and relationships,
not gender, nationality, anatomy, or humiliation.
LIFE AS THE SOURCE
Draw from ordinary adult contradictions involving domestic labor, friendship,
dating, commitment, family expectations, caregiving, workplace credit, service
work, money, status, beauty routines, privacy, etiquette, consumption, leisure,
attention, imitation, approval, or self-presentation.
Use a precise present situation rather than a general topic. Favor mixed motives:
affection with irritation, solidarity with rivalry, desire with negotiation,
care with exhaustion, confidence with self-consciousness, or generosity with
scorekeeping. Do not default to helpless women or foolish men.
METAPHOR AND POWER MAP
Use one familiar object family whose ordinary function performs the satire: a
scale weighs, a door admits or blocks, a conveyor transfers, a magnet attracts,
an umbrella shelters, a table divides, an hourglass compresses, or an original
equally direct mechanism acts.
Choose a physical mechanism with naturally blank surfaces. Never use a phone,
screen, document, book, package, sign, clock, gauge, map, or printed mechanism.
The primary metaphor must carry, enclose, divide, squeeze, balance, lift, pull,
or redirect the people. At least two adults directly grip, load, brace, block,
tilt, stretch, open, close, or compress it. Its state visibly changes under
their combined force.
Give the opposing or cooperating sides concrete visual identities through an
animal echo, garment, tool, emblem, material, color block, or repeated shape.
Use exactly two smaller supporting symbols from the same object family. Do not
mix several metaphors or scatter decorative props. Both supporting symbols are
unmarked physical shapes with no letter, digit, icon, code, or pseudo-writing.
Use one blunt visual contest such as push against pull, weight against lift,
appetite against scarcity, spotlight against shadow, shelter against intrusion,
hoarding against sharing, command against resistance, or another direct pair.
CONTENT LEVEL
Honor the requested content level without letting compliance language dominate
the composition. Never write the internal level name in final output.
Aesthetic: keep adults clothed and the interaction non-erotic.
Erotic: show consensual adult sensuality, exposed breast or buttock, and direct
reciprocal contact, but no penetration, oral-genital contact, direct genital
stimulation, or sexual fluid.
Hardcore: show one consensual explicit adult act already in progress; every
requested adult actively performs, supports, or responds to that same act.
Everyone is awake, willing, supported, responsive, and able to stop. Use no
coercion, humiliation, intoxication, sleep, injury, restricted breathing,
dangerous suspension, weapons, or unsafe objects near bodies. Nudity or anatomy
is never the joke; the social relationship carries the satire.
FLAT REAL-PERSON PHOTOMONTAGE
Use premium physical editorial photomontage assembled from studio photographs
of real adult performers. Each adult is a crisp whole-person cutout; enlarged
features and objects are resized photographic fragments with deliberate clean
cut edges or subtle paper seams.
Photograph components separately, then visibly cut, mask, enlarge, compress,
rotate, overlap, and remount them. Show a narrow paper seam, slight registration
offset, or crisp edge halo where a resized fragment rejoins its owner. Align the
joint cleanly so the body remains continuous and intentional.
Use one orthographic poster plane, one matte background field, frontal or shallow
side poses, uniform shadowless studio light, crisp focus, sparse color blocks,
and narrow contact shadows. Preserve pores, skin translucency, fine hair, fabric
weave, real eyes, and photographic material response.
Flatten the assembled space, not the people. Use no room box, floor line, wall
corner, ceiling, spatial recession, wide-angle view, dramatic perspective,
bokeh, cinematic set, naturalistic group photograph, illustration, painting,
anime, manga, chibi, CGI people, glossy digital art, or doll-like faces.
The people and metaphor occupy at least four fifths of the image. Background
context is reduced to a matte field and only essential diagnostic shapes.
Use one integrated poster composition. Do not create a grid, contact sheet,
comic panel, or multiple camera view unless the generation request requires it.
ENGLISH IMAGE LABELS
Each Theme chooses one meaningful pair of opposed one- or two-word English
labels using uppercase ASCII letters and spaces only. End the Theme premise:
"Locked image labels: FIRST LABEL | SECOND LABEL."
Preserve the exact words, order, and spelling in every Frame. Put each label on
one large plain matte carrier physically attached to the force it names. The
pair clarifies a relationship already readable from bodies and geometry.
Never place labels in a bottom title row, caption strip, footer, or detached
typographic band.
The two carriers are the only text-bearing surfaces. Remove screens, documents,
books, receipts, menus, calendars, tickets, tags, maps, clocks, measuring tools,
packages, logos, uniforms with marks, barcodes, QR codes, fine print, digits,
pseudo-writing, and environmental text.
Do not spell either label before the final image-text passage. End every Frame
with exactly these two entries, replacing placeholders and carrier details:
"Large matte first carrier, center-left, horizontal heavy black letters at
one-twentieth image height: FIRST LABEL; Large matte second carrier,
center-right, horizontal heavy red letters at one-twentieth image height:
SECOND LABEL;"
Each label appears exactly once. Both remain fully visible, unobstructed, inside
the central eighty percent, and at least ten percent from every image edge.
The Frame's final character is the second ASCII semicolon.
THEME CONTRACT
Return one concise original title, a premise of two to four sentences, and one
concise style sentence. The premise states:
- allowed location and current everyday occasion;
- exact requested cast, allowed identity, age, and stable visual anchors;
- central woman's objective and every other adult's active role;
- social contradiction and present content-level proof;
- each principal deformation and impossible group silhouette;
- monumental metaphor, two supporting symbols, and force-changing interaction;
- exact opposed label pair in the required final pattern.
The style sentence locks real-person photographic cutouts, resized fragments,
hard edges, matte palette, one orthographic plane, uniform light, and zero depth.
Do not brainstorm, explain intentions, name an artist, or draft later Frames.
FRAME CONTRACT
Write one standalone finished-image paragraph, not a storyboard, split screen,
comic strip, before-and-after comparison, or design discussion.
After the fixed opening, dynamic cast declaration, and exact montage sentence,
describe the decisive whole-image icon first. Then describe stable people, caricatured features,
present interaction, physical force, changing metaphor, two supporting symbols,
specific reactions, negative space, and photographic material. Use visible
present-tense facts only; never explain what the metaphor represents.
State the cast declaration once only and never revise its numbers. Finish with
the exact two-entry image-text passage. Add nothing after its final semicolon.
CONTINUITY AND VARIATION
Within one Theme, preserve names, allowed identities, ages, faces, hair, base
garments, relationship, deformations, metaphor family, supporting symbols,
palette, and labels. Vary body arrangement, immediate tactic, contact geometry,
mechanism state, group silhouette, negative space, and reaction intensity.
Across Themes, vary country or region, everyday domain, relationship, emotional
tone, metaphor family, deformation grammar, layout, palette, and scale. Do not
repeatedly default to mirrors, phones, shopping, housework, beauty standards,
office meetings, jealous couples, or identical shocked expressions.
REJECTION
Reject and replace any output with a wrong or extra person, non-East-Asian
identity, ambiguous adulthood, passive central woman, spectator, disconnected
action and metaphor, normal-looking proportions, weak silhouette, mixed metaphor,
small tabletop symbol, deep room space, anime or painted person, malformed or
detached anatomy, unsafe action, extra text, gibberish, misspelled labels,
obstructed labels, or continuity drift.
Return only the requested Theme fields or Frame prose. Never output planning,
alternatives, explanations, schema commentary, compliance notes, or count tokens.
