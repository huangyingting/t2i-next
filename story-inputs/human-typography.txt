BRIEF

Create a complete visual alphabet of varied, polished, hyper-realistic adult
photographs. Each Theme centers on one uppercase English letter from A through
Z, formed entirely by the minimum letter-specific adult cast using complete
bodies, coordinated poses, body-to-body spacing, and negative space.

This is human-pose typography, not word spelling and not conventional text.
Every Frame contains one letter only. The human bodies themselves must make
that letter unmistakably readable at thumbnail size.

ALPHABET COVERAGE

A complete alphabet edition contains one Theme for each of the 26 uppercase
letters A through Z. Plan the whole batch before writing any Theme.

- When the batch contains 26 Themes, return the Themes in strict alphabetical
  order: first A, then B, then C, continuing without gaps through final Z.
  Every letter must appear exactly once. Use this exact assignment:
  T001=A, T002=B, T003=C, T004=D, T005=E, T006=F, T007=G, T008=H,
  T009=I, T010=J, T011=K, T012=L, T013=M, T014=N, T015=O, T016=P,
  T017=Q, T018=R, T019=S, T020=T, T021=U, T022=V, T023=W, T024=X,
  T025=Y, T026=Z.
- When the batch contains fewer than 26 Themes, use distinct letters without
  repetition and maximize structural variety across straight, diagonal,
  branched, curved, enclosed, and open letters.
- When the batch contains more than 26 Themes, exhaust all 26 letters before
  repeating one, then give repeated letters substantially different pose,
  mood, wardrobe, lighting, and environment systems.

Do not mention Theme IDs or alphabet indices in titles, premises, styles, or
Frames. Privately track the ordered coverage and reject accidental duplicates,
missing letters, or out-of-order letters before returning the batch.

TYPOGRAPHY-FIRST PRIORITY

The priorities for every Theme and Frame are, in this exact order:

1. the single intended uppercase letter is readable at thumbnail size;
2. every selected adult is visibly indispensable to that letter;
3. torso, arms, legs, contact, and negative space match the letter topology;
4. the number of complete adults matches the letter-specific stroke budget;
5. anatomy, support, adulthood, and physical safety remain clear;
6. expression, fashion, light, and atmosphere give the letter an original
   campaign identity.

If styling weakens recognition, simplify the styling. A beautiful group
portrait that does not read as the assigned letter is invalid.

CAST CONTRACT

This brief requires the female and male totals to remain unspecified for the
whole batch. Determine the cast independently for each letter from the
letter-specific body-count table below. Do not impose one shared cast size or
gender ratio across A through Z.

Every selected person must be an unmistakably mature adult aged 25 or older
with an original identity. When a letter uses two or more people, include at
least one woman and at least one man. Across three- and four-person letters,
vary between woman-majority, man-majority, and balanced groups so the alphabet
does not repeat one gender arrangement.

Use exactly the table's adult total for each letter, with no additional
humans, duplicates, reflections, portraits, screens, silhouettes, or
background figures. Every person appears exactly once at full body length and
supplies exactly one indispensable major stem, bar, diagonal, smooth arc, or
tail. Arms and legs may refine that person's assigned stroke but must not
create a second spatially separate stroke.

Never use a participant as an observer or decorative extra. No person may be
duplicated, occupy two incompatible locations, become a detached body part, or
disappear behind another participant.

- E: 4 adults — vertical stem and three horizontal bars.
- F: 3 adults — vertical stem, upper bar, middle bar.
- G: 3 adults — upper arc, lower arc, inward terminal.
- H: 3 adults — two vertical stems and center crossbar.
- I: 1 adult — one vertical stem.
- J: 2 adults — upper bar and hooked descending stroke.
- K: 3 adults — vertical stem and two diagonals.
- L: 2 adults — vertical stem and lower bar.
- M: 4 adults — four connected long strokes.
- N: 3 adults — two vertical stems and one diagonal.
- O: 2 adults — left continuous arc and right continuous arc.
- P: 2 adults — vertical stem and one continuous bowl arc.
- Q: 3 adults — two oval arcs and one short diagonal tail.
- R: 3 adults — vertical stem, upper bowl arc, diagonal leg.
- S: 2 adults — upper opposing arc and lower opposing arc.
- T: 2 adults — upper bar and vertical stem.
- U: 3 adults — left stem, lower rounded arc, right stem.
- V: 2 adults — left diagonal and right diagonal.

- X: 4 adults — four half-diagonals meeting around one empty center.

Do not reduce a count by asking one adult to occupy two strokes. Do not

LETTER TOPOLOGY — A THROUGH Z

Use the following topology as the non-negotiable visual skeleton. Human poses
may soften the geometry, but every named stroke, curve, junction, aperture,
counter, and terminal must remain visible.

A — two rising outer diagonals meet at one apex, with one clear horizontal
crossbar and one open triangular counter.

B — one straight vertical stem supports two distinct right-facing bowls,
smaller above and larger below, with two readable counters.

C — one broad open curve faces right, with clearly separated upper and lower
terminals and no closing stroke.

D — one straight vertical stem joins a single large right-facing outer curve,
creating one open interior counter.

E — one vertical stem carries three right-facing horizontal bars: long upper,
shorter middle, and long lower.

F — one vertical stem carries a long upper bar and shorter middle bar, with no
lower bar.

G — one broad right-open curve includes a short inward horizontal terminal at
the lower-right opening.

H — two parallel vertical stems connect through one centered horizontal
crossbar.

I — one narrow central vertical stem, with optional short aligned head and foot
terminals only when bodies can create them without ambiguity.

J — one long descending stem bends into a lower-left hook, with a short upper
horizontal terminal.

K — one vertical stem meets two right-facing diagonals at one clear center
junction.

L — one vertical stem meets one lower horizontal bar at a clean 90-degree
corner.

M — two outer verticals connect through two inward diagonals meeting at a
center valley near mid-height.

N — two parallel vertical stems connect through one diagonal running from
upper left to lower right.

O — one continuous closed oval with one large central counter and no opening.

P — one vertical stem supports one closed upper-right bowl, leaving the lower
half open.

Q — one closed oval plus one short diagonal tail exiting at the lower right.

R — one vertical stem, one closed upper-right bowl, and one diagonal leg
departing from the bowl junction toward the lower right.

S — one continuous opposing double curve, with an upper-right bulge and
lower-left bulge connected through a narrow center.

T — one long upper horizontal bar meets one centered vertical stem.

U — two parallel vertical sides connect through one broad rounded lower curve,
with an open top.

V — two long diagonals descend to one narrow lower point. Each of the two
complete straight bodies is one diagonal: the left adult's head is the
upper-left endpoint and their joined feet end at the lower-center vertex; the
right adult's head is the upper-right endpoint and their joined feet end at
the same lower-center vertex. The two pairs of feet nearly touch with a small
visible gap. Both adults keep both straight arms pressed alongside their
torsos and both legs joined. No hand, finger, elbow, shoulder, or head may form
the lower vertex. Do not extend arms overhead or outward, spread legs, cross
bodies, touch torsos, or create an X or star pose.

W — four diagonals create two lower points and one central upper junction.

X — four half-diagonals converge around one small empty center diamond. Use
exactly four complete straight adults, one for each ray. The upper-left
adult's head is the outer upper-left endpoint and their joined feet stop just
before center; the upper-right adult mirrors that path. The lower-left and
lower-right adults place their heads at the two outer lower endpoints and
their joined feet stop just before center. All four pairs of feet surround,
but do not enter, one small diamond of empty floor. Every adult keeps both arms
pressed alongside the torso and both legs joined. No body crosses the center,
overlaps another body, spreads its legs, extends its arms outward, or forms a
star pose.

Y — two upper diagonals meet at one center junction and continue as one lower
vertical stem.

Z — one upper horizontal, one descending diagonal, and one lower horizontal
connect through two clean corners. Use exactly three complete straight adults,
one per stroke. The first lies horizontally with head at the upper-left
endpoint and joined feet at the upper-right corner. The second lies diagonally
with head beside the first person's feet and joined feet at the lower-left
corner. The third lies horizontally with head beside the second person's feet
and joined feet at the lower-right endpoint. Every person keeps both arms
pressed alongside their torso and both legs joined. Use small visible gaps at
both corners. Never bend one person across two strokes, detach a limb, create a
partial fourth body, or place an isolated pair of legs or arms along an edge.

HUMAN POSE CONSTRUCTION

Translate each topology into anatomically credible adult poses. Use torso and
joined legs for strong stems; straight arms for bars and diagonals; bent
elbows, bent knees, side curls, arched arm pairs, and coordinated body arcs for
bowls and curves; deliberate body-to-body gaps for counters and apertures.

Identify the one major stroke or smooth arc supplied by each participant. Keep
every limb traceable from shoulder or hip to hand or foot. Arms and legs must
remain visually subordinate to, and continuous with, that one assigned stroke.

Every participant's contribution must form one spatially continuous anatomical
path through physically adjoining body parts. Never assign one person to two
major strokes, even when they meet at a shoulder, elbow, hip, or knee. Never
assign one person's torso to an upper or left stroke and then reuse one of
their limbs as a remote lower or right stroke separated from that torso. Never
describe a hand, arm, foot, leg, pelvis, or partial torso at a location the
complete owner cannot physically occupy.

At junctions, hands, feet, shoulders, or safe outer limbs may nearly meet or
make light intentional contact. No participant may stand, kneel, sit, or place
hard pressure on another person's head, neck, spine, chest, abdomen, pelvis,
or unsupported joint. No body may lie on top of another.

Curved letters may use several coordinated bodies arranged as a smooth arc,
but the curve must have a clear opening or counter matching the topology.
Never replace a difficult curve with a printed outline, rope, ribbon, prop,
shadow, smoke trail, or caption.

Reject vague prose such as "suggests," "evokes," "resembles," "hints at," or
"approximately forms." Name the exact torso axis, arm direction, leg
direction, bend, junction, endpoint, and negative space that completes the
letter.

CAMERA AND READING FIELD

Use a true 90-degree orthographic overhead camera for floor-based letters. The
optical axis must be exactly perpendicular to the floor and centered on the
glyph. Upright wall-based silhouettes may instead use a perfectly centered
frontal camera with parallel verticals. Do not use oblique, three-quarter,
fisheye, or strongly perspective-distorted views.

Use a normal or moderate telephoto lens from sufficient distance. Keep the
entire letter and every complete head, hand, and foot inside the frame with
even breathing room. Align the image top with the top of the letter so it does
not read sideways, mirrored, or backward.

Use one seamless matte reading field with a solid base color or extremely
smooth tonal gradient. Do not place yoga mats, rugs, floorboards, tiles,
furniture, platforms, architectural lines, text, logos, or props beneath the
bodies. No rectangular object may outline an individual person.

Create strong figure-ground contrast with coordinated opaque close-fitting
full-body fashion. Gloves and socks must match each garment so hands and feet
remain connected to the silhouette. Keep hair controlled within the intended
letter boundary.

SEMANTIC ART DIRECTION

Give every letter one original emotional and visual interpretation. The
letter's phonetic associations, familiar symbolism, topology, or kinetic
character may inspire the concept, but visible conventional words and captions
remain forbidden.

Vary emotional direction across the alphabet: assertive, buoyant, curious,
dramatic, electric, fierce, graceful, harmonious, iconic, jubilant, kinetic,
luminous, monumental, nocturnal, open, playful, questioning, rebellious,
sensual, theatrical, uplifting, victorious, wild, confrontational, yearning,
or another concrete tone. Do not mechanically bind these examples to matching
initial letters.

Give each adult a distinct but coordinated expression and gaze: restrained
smile, lifted brow, calm concentration, defiant direct gaze, delighted open
gaze, inward focus, or another precise performance. Preserve the head position
required by the glyph.

Vary wardrobe material by Theme: lacquered stretch fabric, velvet, metallic
knit, satin, matte neoprene, fine sequins, translucent overlay above an opaque
base, or another silhouette-safe textile. Choose one coherent hue family for
the cast and maintain strong contrast with the reading field.

Build lighting from soft frontal fill plus one complementary colored rim or
edge accent. Possibilities include crimson with cyan edges, cobalt with amber,
emerald with gold, violet with acid green, or another deliberate contrast.
Keep shadows compact and never let a shadow become a missing letter stroke.

PERIPHERAL ATMOSPHERE AND MICRO-DYNAMIC

Decorative atmosphere is permitted only outside the letter's outer bounding
box, in perimeter and corner negative space. Suitable elements include sparse
metallic confetti, flower petals, feathers, leaves, colored haze, tiny
reflective flakes, or a soft projected color glow without lines or text.

Add exactly one restrained micro-dynamic that reinforces the Theme without
changing the letter geometry: synchronized pointed toes, tensed fingers,
visible garment tension from a shared breath, contained hair tips moved by a
controlled breeze, or perimeter particles drifting along one clear vector.

Do not add anatomical motion blur, casual limb movement, large fabric trails,
powder, water, smoke, ropes, ribbons, or props through the letter. Remove any
effect that enters a counter, closes an aperture, creates a false stroke, or
weakens the silhouette.

THEME CONTRACT

Every Theme defines one complete human-letter system:

- one exact uppercase letter;
- the exact letter-specific adult total from the body-count table, with every
  participant assigned one indispensable major stroke or arc;
- the complete letter topology and body-to-stroke mapping;
- one safe support and contact system;
- one seamless high-contrast reading field;
- one coordinated fashion material and palette;
- one orthographic overhead or perfectly frontal camera;
- one expression and gaze choreography;
- one fill and complementary rim-light system;
- one perimeter-only atmospheric treatment; and
- one restrained micro-dynamic.

Give the Theme a concise original title that is not merely the letter and does
not appear in the image. In no more than two premise sentences, name the
letter, exact adult total, gender composition, body geometry, junctions,
counters, and support. The style must define emotional direction, wardrobe
material, palette, camera, light, perimeter atmosphere, and micro-dynamic.

Frames within one Theme are parallel alternatives of the same letter, not
steps in drawing it. Preserve the letter and recurring adult identities while
varying pose distribution, expression, wardrobe treatment, light direction,
peripheral material, or crop without weakening topology.

FRAME CONTRACT

Every Frame is one standalone full-frame photograph containing one uppercase
letter only. Write one unbroken natural-language paragraph in the requested
language.

Begin every final Frame with this structural declaration, translated only when
the requested output language is not English:

    A true orthographic photograph shows exactly [TOTAL] complete adult bodies
    collectively forming the single uppercase letter [LETTER] on a seamless
    high-contrast field; each adult supplies exactly one indispensable major
    stroke or smooth arc of the glyph.

Replace both bracketed fields with exact values. Immediately describe the
letter topology, then map each participant's torso, left arm, right arm, left
leg, right leg, support, contact, and negative-space role. Complete all
geometry before describing identity, expression, wardrobe, floor, lighting,
atmosphere, lens, and finish.

Do not render the target as ordinary typography anywhere in the image. Do not
include an alphabet label, caption, cell border, diagram, guide line, arrow,
logo, watermark, Theme title, or visible written character. The only readable
letter must be the human formation.

End every Frame by stating that the seamless field contains no printed,
painted, projected, illuminated, glowing, architectural, shadow, outline, or
prop version of the target letter; only the complete human bodies define the
glyph. This exclusion must remain explicit in final prose rather than being
left as an internal instruction.

FRAME ACCEPTANCE GATE

Before accepting a Frame, verify all of the following:

1. exactly one uppercase letter is present;
2. its topology matches the corresponding A-through-Z definition;
3. the complete adult total exactly matches the letter-specific body-count
   table;
4. every participant is necessary to a named stroke, curve, junction, counter,
   terminal, or negative-space boundary;
5. every participant supplies exactly one major stroke or smooth arc;
6. every limb is connected, traceable, and assigned one compatible task;
7. all support and contact are anatomically credible and safe;
8. the camera is orthographic overhead or perfectly centered frontal;
9. the reading field contains no mats, patterns, props, or conventional text;
10. fashion and light preserve strong figure-ground contrast;
11. decoration remains outside the letter bounding box;
12. exactly one micro-dynamic is present without altering the glyph; and
13. the letter remains recognizable as a flat silhouette at thumbnail size.

For a two-adult V, additionally verify that both heads are the two upper
endpoints, both pairs of joined feet nearly meet at the sole lower vertex, all
four arms remain pressed against the two torsos, and no fingertips, hands,
heads, or crossed bodies create the vertex.

For a three-adult Z, additionally verify that three different complete straight
bodies form the upper bar, diagonal, and lower bar respectively. Reject the
Frame if one person spans two strokes, any limb is detached, or a partial
fourth body is implied.

For a four-adult X, additionally verify that four different complete straight
bodies form the four half-diagonals around one empty central diamond. Reject
the Frame if any torso crosses the center, any two bodies overlap, any arms or
legs spread outward, or fewer than four complete people are shown.

Reject and rewrite any Theme or Frame containing the wrong letter, more than
one letter, an extra or missing person, decorative observer, incomplete stroke,
missing counter, closed aperture, ordinary yoga pose without typographic
function, vague limb placement, unsafe overlap, detached anatomy, oblique
camera, yoga mat, patterned floor, prop-built stroke, shadow-built stroke,
printed letter, or unsupported claim of readability.
