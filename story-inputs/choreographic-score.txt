BRIEF

Create refined photographic Themes that translate an abstract choreographic
score into one image of the requested adult cast. The score must be visible
through bodies, floor geometry, movable objects, and light, without printed
notation or repeated exposures.

WOMAN CAST GATE

Every Theme and every Frame must include at least one unmistakably adult woman
whose complete, clearly visible body carries the choreographic score. Give her a
specific adult age, stable appearance, grounded position, gaze, and necessary
action. A silhouette, reflection, portrait, or implied dancer does not count.
Additional CLI-requested adults may participate, but she must retain a distinct
spatial function. This brief is incompatible with a zero-woman cast.

SCORE DESIGN

For each Theme invent one rule using direction, distance, level, pause, counter-
weight, orbit, symmetry, interruption, or accumulation. Use one operation and
express it through one arrested instant. Make that operation readable in three
ways: the cast's body geometry, its relationship to one existing floor seam,
and its relationship to one movable object. A shaped pool of light isolates the
action without becoming another rule. Every person must have
a distinct spatial function and stable support; no acrobatics, impossible
joints, suspended bodies, or motion trails.

THEME CONTRACT

The premise names the country, exact performance or rehearsal site, adult cast,
the single score operation, movable object, floor seam, shaped pool of light,
and the pressure or choice visible at its most revealing instant.
The style sentence states medium, floor treatment, palette, and lighting
geometry.

FRAME CONTRACT

Each Frame is a standalone interpretation of the same score, not the next dance
step. Restate every person's orientation, weight-bearing support, gaze, limb
paths, and relationship to the shared geometry. Use tape-free floor seams,
shadows, the named movable object, and the named shaped pool of light to expose
the score. Explain concretely how the same operation appears in body, seam, and
object. One dominant
diagonal, arc, grid, compression, or void should organize the image.

The pose must be directly imageable: each hand and foot has one job, every reach
is within range, silhouettes remain separable, and the camera preserves the
designed spacing. Wardrobe must respond to the chosen movement vocabulary and
site while remaining consistent within the Theme.

VARIETY GATE

Across Themes vary score logic, social relationship, architecture, body level,
camera axis, and emotional temperature. Reject ordinary dance photography,
groups evenly spaced in a row, decorative floor graphics unrelated to bodies,
or prose that narrates several successive movements.
