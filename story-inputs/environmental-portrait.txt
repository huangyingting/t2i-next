BRIEF

Create psychologically precise environmental portraits in which a working space
reveals the requested adult cast through current use rather than biography,
status symbols, or explanatory text. Each Theme must find drama in a real task
performed at its decisive quiet instant.

WOMAN CAST GATE

Every Theme and every Frame must include at least one unmistakably adult woman
as a skilled, active subject in the workspace. Give her a specific adult age,
stable appearance, clearly visible face and body, position, gaze, and necessary
task. A silhouette, portrait, reflection, or off-camera worker does not count.
Additional CLI-requested adults may share the work, but she must not become
architectural decoration. This brief is incompatible with a zero-woman cast.

SUBJECT AND SPACE

Select a different skilled practice, domestic responsibility, field activity,
or civic task for every Theme. The place must contain only tools and traces that
belong to that activity. Do not infer prestigious jobs, invent awards, or rely
on exotic hardship. The cast may share, divide, inspect, repair, teach, or hand
off the task, but every requested person must have a visible role.

THEME CONTRACT

The premise names the country, time, exact workspace, adult cast identities,
current task, one material problem, and the relationship expressed by how they
handle it. Name the material problem as a concrete damaged, blocked, misaligned,
contaminated, unfinished, or unstable physical state; an emotional tension,
desired outcome, or abstract difficulty does not count. The style sentence
defines a restrained editorial photographic language, palette, lens character,
and motivated light.

FRAME CONTRACT

Each Frame is an independent portrait from the same work session. Establish the
person-space relationship first, then describe one static action, one expression
or eyeline, the active tool, the material being changed, and the direct result.
Preserve stable faces, hair, clothing logic, workspace, and core task while
varying camera distance, responsibility, and the problem's visible state.

Use three to seven coherent objects, clear hand assignments, plausible protective
clothing, honest wear, and enough negative space to read the environment. Avoid
heroic posing, everyone facing camera, immaculate unused tools, floating dust for
automatic atmosphere, and generic workshop clutter.

Reject any Frame that could be moved to a blank studio without losing meaning,
that describes a profession but no current material change, or that turns the
cast into anonymous decoration for architecture.
