BRIEF

Create ambitious living-map Themes in which a real landscape or interior is
organized to resemble a map while remaining fully usable at human scale. The
requested adult cast navigates one current decision through paths, boundaries,
landmarks, and terrain made from actual site materials.

WOMAN CAST GATE

Every Theme and every Frame must include at least one unmistakably adult woman
physically navigating the mapped world. Give her a specific adult age, stable
appearance, clearly visible body, exact map position, gaze, and current route
choice. A tiny anonymous marker, silhouette, portrait, reflection, or off-camera
traveler does not count. Additional CLI-requested adults may navigate with her,
but she must remain essential to the decision. This brief is incompatible with
a zero-woman cast.

MAP-WORLD RULE

Choose a different map purpose for every Theme: water distribution, memory,
harvest, evacuation, migration of animals, neighborhood exchange, weather, or
another nonviolent system. Roads, contours, borders, and landmarks must be
formed by credible features such as paths, hedges, channels, rugs, shelving,
tables, light, crops, or stone. Do not simply print a map on the floor.

THEME CONTRACT

The premise names country, exact site, adult cast, mapped system, orientation
logic, one blocked route or competing destination, and the cast's current
decision. The style sentence defines elevated photography, scale cues, palette,
weather, and surface detail.

FRAME CONTRACT

Each Frame is one oblique or high view that reads both as a functional place and
as a map. Establish north or another orientation through a natural environmental
cue rather than a letter. Describe at least two routes, one boundary, three
landmarks, the cast's exact position, and a visible choice between paths.

Keep routes continuous, slopes traversable, water downhill, doors connected,
and scale consistent. Do not add labels, pins, legends, arrows, UI, floating
terrain, tiny anonymous crowds, or satellite imagery. The cast must physically
use the mapped world rather than point at it from outside.

Reject decorative labyrinths, landscapes that only resemble maps by coincidence,
and map concepts whose human decision cannot be understood from position and
direction alone.
