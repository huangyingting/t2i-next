BRIEF

Create cinematic archival-reconstruction Themes that turn one disputed historical
moment into a meticulously staged present-day photograph. The result must feel
like evidence rebuilt by production design, not a generic costume scene and not
an imitation of a surviving famous photograph.

WOMAN CAST GATE

Every Theme and every Frame must include at least one unmistakably adult woman
as an active, clearly visible subject. Give her a specific adult age, stable
appearance, era-coherent clothing, position, gaze, and decisive reconstruction
action. Never replace her with a man, silhouette, portrait, or off-camera
historical figure. Additional CLI-requested adults may appear, but the woman
must remain integral to the evidence. This brief is incompatible with a
zero-woman cast.

RECONSTRUCTION METHOD

Each Theme selects a different country, era, ordinary location, positive
material clue, and one present human action the clue makes meaningful. The clue must be an existing,
visible object or mark such as a torn matching edge, stain, altered fastener,
tool impression, folded document, or repaired seam; a missing object, empty
space, unsupported claim, or absence does not count. Use only historically coherent architecture,
tools, textiles, lighting, transport, etiquette, and written media. If a fact is
uncertain, choose a credible generic form rather than inventing a brand, exact
date, rank, or celebrated person.

The requested cast are clearly identifiable adults playing original people from
the chosen era. Do not depict celebrities, public figures, war atrocities, or
real victims. Additional human figures are forbidden unless the CLI cast requires
them.

THEME CONTRACT

The premise must establish the era, country, exact site, cast identities, one
visible archival clue, and the single present action staged around it. Do not
build the premise around an unseen crime or unverifiable historical claim.
The style sentence must identify a coherent capture process and restrained
period-informed color treatment without claiming the image is authentic history.

FRAME CONTRACT

Each Frame is an independent reconstruction of the action named in the premise,
not a chronological sequence. Freeze the action while it is visibly underway,
never just before or after it. Make hand positions, eyelines, object placement,
and physical consequences legible. Choose a camera axis that shows the positive
clue unobstructed in a natural location, along with at least
three era-specific environmental facts, but do not turn the scene into a catalog.

Use documentary composition, believable wear, imperfect materials, motivated
light, and an optical character consistent with the declared reconstruction
method. Visible text may appear only when essential to the clue; keep it short,
quoted exactly in the prose, attached to a named carrier, and appropriate to the
place and era.

FINAL GATE

Reject nostalgia without a visible material purpose, decorative costume portraiture, modern
objects hidden under vague language, melodramatic crime evidence, or any Frame
that needs a caption to explain what changed.
