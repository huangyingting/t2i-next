BRIEF

Create varied, publication-ready portrait magazine-cover Themes and finished
front-cover Frames. Every Theme invents one original magazine identity and one
specific issue concept; every Frame is a parallel cover proposal for that same
issue. The result must look like a professionally art-directed magazine sold on
a newsstand, not a film poster, social-media post, advertisement, title card,
editorial spread, moodboard, contact sheet, or photograph with arbitrary text
placed over it.

Every cover combines one decisive editorial photograph with an integrated
masthead, one dominant feature line, a restrained set of secondary cover lines,
and compact issue information. Image, typography, negative space, crop, color,
and hierarchy must work as one designed object.

MAGAZINE, NOT POSTER

The cover must communicate a recurring publication, a particular issue, and an
editorial package at first glance.

Mandatory magazine signals:

- one original recurring masthead at the top;
- one issue-specific hero subject or interaction;
- one dominant feature line tied to that hero;
- exactly two short secondary cover lines suggesting other issue contents;
- one compact alphabetic season marker;
- deliberate cover-line placement around the hero silhouette; and
- a flat, front-facing portrait trim with visible cover edges.

Do not use movie-poster signals: no film title treatment, tagline, theatrical
billing block, cast credits, studio mark, release date, review stars, festival
laurels, streaming badge, one-sheet metaphor, or cinematic key-art campaign.

Do not use social-post signals: no username, avatar, reaction count, comment,
share icon, interface chrome, carousel dots, square feed crop, device frame, or
platform branding.

Do not show a physical mockup. The image is the finished front cover itself,
not a magazine lying on a table, held by hands, displayed on a phone, wrapped
around a 3D object, opened to a spread, stacked with other issues, or shown with
a spine or back cover.

EXAMPLES ARE OPEN-ENDED

Every list below is open-ended inspiration, not a menu, enum, preset sequence,
or assignment by Theme ID. Combine compatible approaches or invent new ones.
Only statements using "must," "every," "exact," or "do not" are mandatory.

CAST, ADULTHOOD, AND CONTINUITY

Use the exact requested human cast and no additional people. Every depicted
person must be an unmistakably mature Chinese adult aged 25 or older, alert,
willing, responsive, and able to stop. Preserve each person's age, face, hair,
body proportions, garments, body marks, and other identity anchors throughout
one Theme.

Every requested adult must appear clearly in the hero photograph of every
Frame. Do not replace anyone with a silhouette, reflection, shadow, illustration,
typography, cropped body fragment, inset portrait, or off-cover implication.
Background crowds, staff, passersby, printed portraits, screen figures, statues,
and reflected people count as additional people and must not appear.

Repeated cover text, drop shadows, printed patterns, and abstract graphics must
not create a second face or body. Use one visible body per requested person and
keep anatomy, hands, gaze, support, and physical contact coherent.

EDITORIAL CONCEPT FIRST

Every Theme defines a real editorial proposition rather than a generic portrait
plus fashionable words. Establish:

- one original publication name and audience;
- one issue category and editorial mission;
- one exact hero subject, activity, relationship, or event;
- one specific Chinese location, era or year, season, and social context;
- one issue-level question, tension, discovery, celebration, or practical stake;
- one visual thesis connecting the hero photograph to the cover hierarchy; and
- one publication voice expressed through concise cover-line language.

The hero image must visibly support the main feature line. If the headline could
be exchanged for an unrelated subject without changing the photograph, the
cover concept is too generic. Do not depend on prose outside the image to
explain why this person, action, object, or place leads the issue.

Invent fictional editorial subjects and original publication identities. Do
not reproduce or closely imitate an existing magazine name, masthead, famous
cover, celebrity portrait, brand campaign, copyrighted character, recognizable
artwork, or established publication grid.

THEME CONTRACT

Use the Theme schema deliberately.

The Theme title is the magazine's exact recurring English masthead. It contains
one to three short words made only of uppercase ASCII letters and spaces, is
grammatical or intentionally name-like, and remains identical across every
Frame in that Theme. Do not translate it, decorate it with issue information,
or use an existing publication name.

The premise uses no more than two sentences. Sentence one establishes the
publication's category, audience, precise issue concept, complete adult cast,
hero action, Chinese setting, and content-level visibility anchor. Sentence two
states the editorial question or promise, the main feature angle, and the
essential magazine hierarchy that every cover must preserve.

The style sentence defines one original publication system through masthead
character, typography class, palette, photographic treatment, background
discipline, spacing, print finish, and editorial tone. It does not prescribe one
fixed Frame layout and does not name an existing magazine or designer.

During Theme generation, return only the masthead title, shared issue premise,
and shared publication style. Do not write Frame drafts, alternate covers,
visible-copy inventories, schema keys, count values, or future instructions.

FRAME CONTRACT

Every Frame is one standalone finished cover and one parallel design proposal
for the same issue, never a chronological story beat or another page in the
magazine. Preserve the exact masthead, issue concept, adult cast, identity
anchors, hero subject, content-level anchor, audience, and publication DNA.

Each Frame may invent a different exact main feature line and secondary cover
line package when they remain faithful to the same issue. Vary crop, pose,
negative space, masthead interaction, typography placement, palette emphasis,
background treatment, eye path, and print finish substantially. Do not vary
only color, camera angle, or wording.

Every Frame must independently describe:

- the complete hero photograph and exact adult cast;
- current action, expression, gaze, body support, and interaction;
- magazine category, issue subject, and intended audience;
- front-cover crop, trim, safe margins, and visual hierarchy;
- exact masthead placement and masthead-to-subject overlap;
- one exact main feature line and exactly two exact secondary cover lines;
- one exact alphabetic season marker;
- typography families, weights, scale, alignment, leading, and color;
- background, palette, source light, photographic treatment, and print finish;
- protected areas where text must not cover critical faces, hands, products, or
  content-level contact; and
- no other visible text or graphic interface.

Restate each cover completely. Do not use "same," "still," "again," "another
version," or any continuity shortcut.

FRONT-COVER FORMAT

Build one flat, full-bleed portrait 3:4 front cover with clean visible trim
edges. Do not create an open spread, gatefold, interior page, contents page,
poster, diptych, contact sheet, tiled grid, collage of cover alternatives, or
multiple magazines.

Keep important text and faces inside a consistent inner safe margin of roughly
four to six percent of the cover width. Background color, texture, photograph,
or intentional white space may bleed to the edges. Nothing should look
accidentally clipped.

Use one dominant hero image occupying roughly sixty to eighty percent of the
cover. The masthead and face establish the first read; the main feature line is
the second read; the current action or relationship is the third read;
secondary cover lines and issue metadata form the final read.

At thumbnail size, the viewer must recognize:

1. a magazine masthead;
2. the hero person or group;
3. one main editorial subject;
4. a deliberate family of cover lines; and
5. a coherent publication identity.

MASTHEAD CONTRACT

The exact Theme title is rendered once as the masthead in large, custom,
high-contrast English lettering across the top eight to sixteen percent of the
cover. Preserve its spelling exactly.

The masthead may sit fully in front of the hero, fully behind the head, or use
one controlled foreground-background weave. If the head overlaps it, keep at
least seventy percent of every letter legible and preserve the complete first
and last letters. Do not scatter, rotate, repeat, stack into unrelated pieces,
or turn the masthead into a movie title.

Make the masthead feel recurring and publication-specific through proportion,
spacing, terminals, stroke contrast, case, and color. It remains consistent
across one Theme while Frames explore different image relationships around it.
Do not add a separate logo, trademark symbol, registered mark, superscript,
badge, seal, or tiny characters beside the masthead.

COVER-LINE PACKAGE

Every Frame includes exactly:

- one masthead of one to three words;
- one main feature line of two to five words;
- exactly two secondary cover lines of two to four words each;
- one season marker chosen from SPRING, SUMMER, AUTUMN, WINTER, or SPECIAL.

Keep total visible copy under twenty-four English words. Do not fill the cover
with paragraphs, decks, captions, biographies, article summaries, pull quotes,
credits, slogans, hashtags, web addresses, or placeholder text.

The main feature line must be the largest text after the masthead. Place it near
the hero's strongest negative space or align it with the decisive gesture,
object, garment edge, architecture, or gaze. It may overlap low-information
background but must not cross eyes, mouth, hands, decisive products, or required
physical contact.

Secondary cover lines form one controlled system inside a single aligned
information block shared with the season marker. Place that block in one clear
side or lower area with generous line spacing. Vary weight without scattering
small type around empty gaps, and do not make every line compete with the main
feature.

The season marker belongs near the masthead or in one upper corner. It must be
exactly one allowed season word, never a date, month, year, volume, edition,
number word, or spelled-out number. Do not render a barcode, QR code, ISBN,
ISSN, SKU, price, currency symbol, serial number, issue number, web address,
copyright line, certification, retail sticker, numeric code, vertical bars, or
small retail box anywhere on the cover. Leave lower corners as clean
photography, color, or intentional negative space.

NO MICROTEXT

Every text element must be deliberately readable at thumbnail size. The
smallest secondary cover line or season marker uses letters at least roughly
four percent of the cover height. Do not create tiny captions, fine print,
legal copy, credits, footnotes, labels, decorative pseudo-type, rows of digits,
or any cluster of marks that an image model may interpret as miniature writing.
If there is not enough clean space, remove a secondary cover line rather than
shrinking it.

ENGLISH-ONLY IMAGE TEXT GATE

Every readable string inside the cover must be exact grammatical English using
only uppercase ASCII letters A through Z and spaces. Do not use lowercase
letters, digits, punctuation, symbols, or accented letters in visible copy. The
descriptive prompt follows the requested output language, but that language
must never leak into the rendered cover.

Every exact string must match `[A-Z]+( [A-Z]+)*`: uppercase words separated by
single spaces, with no apostrophe, quotation mark, hyphen, colon, ampersand,
period, or other mark inside the string. Each word contains two to twelve
letters. Never use `&`; write `AND` instead. Silently replace any string that
fails this pattern.

The dedicated image-text passage inside the Frame prose is the sole source of
visible copy. It contains exactly five entries in this order: masthead, main
feature line, first secondary line, second secondary line, and season marker.
For each entry, first state its exact cover location and printed role, add a
colon, then write the exact uppercase text, ending the entry with a semicolon.
Never surround visible text with quotation marks, brackets, boxes, code
formatting, or decorative marks. Text following the colon is printed on the
cover; location and role wording is descriptive only.

Outside that dedicated passage, do not repeat visible copy, translate it,
paraphrase it, or imply additional words. If a string is not declared after a
colon in that passage, it must not be readable anywhere in the image.
Before that passage, refer only to generic roles such as masthead, main feature
line, secondary lines, and season marker; never spell any planned cover string.

Render zero Chinese characters, Japanese kana, Korean hangul, Cyrillic,
accented Latin letters, invented glyphs, pseudo-letters, lorem ipsum, or
gibberish. Chinese people, places, clothing, documents, architecture, and
products do not authorize non-English image text.

Environmental signs, labels, packaging, screens, books, newspapers, garments,
equipment, and props must be blank, turned away, cropped, abstracted, or too
soft to read. They must not compete with the controlled cover-copy package.

Typography must be correctly spelled, horizontal, high contrast, and large
enough to read. Use no more than two type families and three weights. Keep
kerning, leading, baselines, punctuation, and alignment consistent. Do not
simulate editorial richness through tiny unreadable text.

TEXT-LAYER ISOLATION LOCK

Frame prose must use English throughout, regardless of the requested output
language. This is a render-safety requirement: non-English descriptive prose
can leak into the generated cover as unintended writing. Transliterate Chinese
names and places with unaccented ASCII letters, or identify adults by role when
a clean transliteration is unavailable. Prefer a generic English location such
as a bridge in Xinjiang over pasting an original Chinese proper name. Every
character in the complete Frame must be ASCII code point 0 through 127. A
single non-ASCII character invalidates the Frame and requires a complete
rewrite.

Begin every Frame with this exact sentence: "The cover contains exactly five
readable English strings and zero other letters, words, numbers, symbols,
pseudo-letters, or glyph-like marks." Follow it immediately with the hero
photograph description.

Use exactly three controlled typography zones:

1. the masthead alone across the top;
2. the main feature line alone in the strongest negative-space area; and
3. one aligned information block containing both secondary lines and the season
   marker with generous spacing.

All five strings run horizontally from left to right. Do not use vertical,
rotated, arched, circular, fragmented, repeated, mirrored, or perspective text.
Keep the information block in one location rather than distributing small type
across several corners. Do not add decorative initials, letter fragments,
column ornaments, faux editorial notation, or linear marks that resemble tiny
characters.

Immediately before the dedicated image-text passage, write this exact sentence:
"The five declared English strings are the complete typographic layer; all
remaining cover areas are pure photography, uninterrupted color, or blank
negative space." The dedicated passage must then be the final text in the
Frame.

Quarantine all exact visible copy until that final passage. Before the passage,
use only generic lowercase role phrases such as the masthead, the main feature
line, the information block, and the season marker. Do not write the Theme
title, candidate headline, secondary line, season word, or any all-uppercase
sequence of two or more letters anywhere earlier in the Frame. Each of the five
exact strings appears once and only once, after its assigned colon in the final
passage. The Frame's final character is the semicolon ending the season-marker
entry.

HERO PHOTOGRAPH

The hero photograph is one coherent exposure, not a montage of unrelated
images. It must look commissioned for this issue rather than selected from
generic stock.

Choose the crop according to the editorial subject:

- a close portrait may emphasize face, hair, makeup, jewelry, or psychological
  expression while retaining enough shoulders and posture to avoid a floating
  head;
- a three-quarter fashion or culture portrait may emphasize garment silhouette,
  body line, object, craft, or environment;
- a wider group portrait must keep every requested adult recognizable and make
  their relationship legible;
- an action cover must freeze one decisive movement with coherent anatomy and
  protected negative space; and
- an environmental cover may make the cast smaller only when location scale is
  the main editorial fact and every face remains identifiable.

Maintain realistic skin, pores, hair, fabric, contact shadows, reflections, and
material response. Keep hands anatomically correct, limbs traceable, and body
weight supported. Do not duplicate the cast through mirrors, screens, shadows,
cutouts, thumbnails, or inset photographs.

Direct the gaze intentionally. Eye contact with the reader creates authority or
intimacy; off-camera gaze must point toward a visible person, object, or action.
Do not leave every face vacant, closed-eyed, or looking in unrelated directions.

Protect the face. The masthead may overlap hair, but no cover line crosses the
eyes, nose, mouth, or defining facial contour. At erotic and hardcore levels,
typography must not conceal required anatomy, decisive contact, or active
participation.

CONTENT-LEVEL DIRECTION

Apply only the branch matching the requested content level. The selected level
controls the visible hero photograph, not merely copy, mood, wardrobe, or
publication category.

At aesthetic level, create a non-explicit editorial cover. Use fashion,
portraiture, culture, design, travel, food, sport, science, art, or another
publication-specific subject. Do not add sexual activity.

At erotic level, the hero photograph must visibly show adult nudity,
non-explicit intimate contact, sensual body tension, or another unmistakably
erotic adult subject. Every requested adult remains alert, willing, responsive,
and able to stop. Do not hide the erotic anchor behind the masthead or cover
lines, and do not introduce explicit sexual activity.

At hardcore level, the hero photograph must visibly show one explicit,
consensual adult sexual interaction already in progress. Name the involved
anatomy and exact current contact in the Frame prose. Every requested adult has
an active visible role and remains alert, willing, responsive, and able to
stop. Mere nudity, suggestive copy, preparation, obscured contact, or a tiny
inset image does not qualify.

At every level, the cover remains a designed magazine cover rather than a plain
photograph. Typography supports the editorial proposition but never replaces
the required visible content.

MAGAZINE CATEGORIES

Vary publication categories freely. Possibilities include high fashion,
independent style, contemporary art, architecture, interiors, industrial
design, photography, cinema culture, music, performance, literature, ideas,
science, ecology, technology, food, travel, urban life, craft, sport, movement,
wellness, collecting, social observation, or an original hybrid with a clear
audience.

The category must influence the photograph and cover system:

- fashion may use sculptural styling, controlled gaze, fabric movement, and
  severe typography;
- art and culture may use conceptual portraiture, unusual negative space, and
  intellectually precise cover lines;
- architecture and design may use grids, material detail, spatial rhythm, and
  disciplined alignments;
- science and technology may use clear diagrams or abstract non-text graphics
  without becoming an interface;
- food may use tactile ingredients, table geometry, and human appetite without
  resembling packaging;
- travel and ecology may use environmental scale and place-specific light
  without tourism clichés;
- music and performance may use gesture, stage energy, and rhythmic typography
  without becoming an album cover; and
- sport and movement may use decisive action and bold hierarchy without
  becoming an event poster.

These are directional examples, not fixed templates.

COVER DESIGN DIRECTIONS

Choose one dominant design direction per Frame:

- clean fashion authority: large masthead, direct gaze, restrained cover lines,
  immaculate background, and exact garment silhouette;
- dense newsstand energy: bold feature line, controlled side stacks, crisp
  color contrast, and energetic but ordered typography;
- independent culture journal: generous negative space, unusual crop, quiet
  masthead, and intellectually spare cover lines;
- special monochrome issue: black-and-white hero, one spot color, strong paper
  texture, and highly controlled type;
- color-field portrait: one saturated background, sharply separated skin and
  wardrobe, and minimal high-contrast typography;
- environmental issue: smaller cast within one powerful location while the
  masthead and feature line remain dominant;
- tactile print edition: uncoated stock, restrained halftone, embossed masthead,
  foil accent, overprint, or subtle registration character;
- photographic intervention: one controlled crop, shadow, translucent color
  block, hand-drawn non-text mark, or die-cut illusion tied to the issue; or
- typographic framing: masthead, feature line, and margin copy create a precise
  frame around the hero without turning the cover into a poster.

Use one direction coherently. Do not stack every effect, create random sticker
clutter, or imitate a known cover.

COLOR, LIGHT, AND PRINT FINISH

Choose one dominant palette relationship specific to the publication. Maintain
clear separation among skin, hair, wardrobe, background, masthead, feature line,
and secondary copy.

Use editorially motivated light: broad studio key, hard beauty flash, luminous
window light, crisp daylight, restrained stage light, environmental practicals,
or another coherent setup. Preserve facial catchlights and readable skin.
Avoid generic cinematic teal-orange grading, muddy shadows, uncontrolled bloom,
or effects that reduce text contrast.

Print finish may use coated gloss, uncoated matte, soft-touch laminate, subtle
paper tooth, restrained foil, spot varnish, embossing, debossing, fluorescent
ink, metallic ink, halftone, or slight registration character. Apply each finish
to a named cover element and state its visible effect. Do not cover the whole
image in arbitrary distress, scratches, torn paper, or aged-film damage unless
the publication concept specifically requires it.

VARIETY ACROSS THEMES

Plan the requested Theme batch as a set. Generate more candidate publications
than required, compare them, and select the strongest distinct identities.

Across Themes, vary:

- publication category and target reader;
- masthead name, proportions, case, and type character;
- issue question and main editorial promise;
- hero activity, relationship, crop, and gaze;
- Chinese location, era, season, and social context;
- cover density from minimal to newsstand-rich;
- palette, background, light, and photographic treatment;
- text alignment, side balance, and masthead overlap;
- print stock and finishing accent; and
- emotional temperature.

Do not assign categories or layouts by Theme ID. Reject a batch dominated by
fashion portraits, blank studio backgrounds, centered faces, red mastheads,
identical side stacks, luxury language, monochrome minimalism, or generic
celebrity-cover expressions.
