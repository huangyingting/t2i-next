BRIEF

Create varied, award-caliber, hyper-realistic adult campaign stills in an
original theatrical pop-surreal visual language. Each Theme invents a complete
human-scale staged tableau in which a familiar consumer object, service,
ceremony, or social ritual becomes an extravagant practical set with an
immediately legible visual contradiction.

The result should feel like an extravagant editorial production photographed
on a real soundstage: saturated color, sculptural bodies, elaborate practical
sets, glossy commercial surfaces, irreverent symbolic collisions, and one
decisive frozen action. Do not name, quote, recreate, or imitate any living
artist or any identifiable existing artwork, campaign, photograph, costume,
set, or composition. Build a new visual identity from the concrete principles
in this brief.

CORE CAMPAIGN EQUATION

Build every Theme from this equation:

    ordinary desire or social ritual
    + monumental consumer ecology
    + one morally or physically ironic reversal
    = one original pop-surreal campaign proposition

The consumer ecology is more than one enlarged object. Use its recognizable
packaging, mechanisms, ingredients, waste, tools, byproducts, service roles,
and material states as parts of one coherent tableau. The cast must actively
operate, resist, celebrate, perform, or transform that system.

Scale alone is not a concept. A giant object with people standing beside it is
invalid. The object must acquire a new social function, and the cast's action
must visibly expose the reversal. Favor propositions that can be understood
without captions, logos, or prior knowledge.

TABLEAU FIRST, NOT MINIATURE ENGINEERING

The cast remains ordinary human scale unless the Story Description explicitly
requires another scale. Build a full-scale theatrical set around them. Do not
default to tiny people living inside packaging, machines, appliances, ducts,
containers, food, cosmetics, or anatomical cavities.

The set is a symbolic stage, not a miniature survival simulation. Reject
shelters, civic utilities, maintenance societies, valve-control jobs, cable
transport, improvised workshops, pressure systems, domestic infrastructure,
and people operating giant mechanisms when those logistics become the main
idea. Machinery may appear only as one visually subordinate part of a larger
human ritual.

Every image needs a tableau readable first as one bold icon: a human desire,
public ritual, glamorous catastrophe, artificial paradise, or moral collision.
Small mechanisms and world-building details support that icon; they never
replace it.

Examples of productive reversals include luxury becoming public labor,
cleanliness producing visible excess, convenience requiring an absurd ritual,
devotion functioning as customer service, celebration becoming maintenance,
beauty manufacturing its own weather, abundance creating a precise shortage,
or private desire becoming civic infrastructure. These are mechanisms, not a
fixed subject list; invent unrelated propositions rather than paraphrasing the
examples.

ANTHOLOGY DIVERSITY

This is an anthology, not one continuous universe. Generate exactly the Theme
count and Frames-per-Theme count requested by the CLI.

Every Theme must differ from all others in:

- hero ecology and its ordinary real-world function;
- human objective and social ritual;
- ironic reversal;
- location type and architectural silhouette;
- dominant palette and secondary accent;
- material behavior;
- lighting design;
- emotional temperature; and
- camera grammar.

Do not let several Themes collapse into the same nightclub, chapel, beauty
salon, supermarket, swimming pool, banquet, factory, bedroom, or ruin. Do not
repeatedly rely on giant food, giant flowers, liquid baths, fabric mazes,
religious poses, miniature civilizations, mechanical interiors, or people
merely climbing an object.

Before returning a Theme, silently compare it with the other Themes in the
current batch. Replace it if the same proposition would survive after swapping
only the hero object, palette, or wardrobe.

THEME-STAGE CONTRACT

Each Theme must establish one campaign proposition capable of producing the
requested number of visually distinct Frames.

The title is concise, vivid, and original. It contains no artist name, content
level, camera term, workflow term, or explanation.

The premise contains no more than two sentences:

1. Sentence one establishes the explicit country, precise location, era or
   year, recurring adult cast, monumental hero ecology, and the cast's shared
   objective.
2. Sentence two states the single ironic operating law, the decisive physical
   mechanism that makes it visible, and the cost, desire, or contradiction that
   binds the cast to it.

The premise must give every recurring person a stable compact identity:
nationality, unmistakably adult age of twenty-five or older, complexion,
build, face structure, and hair. Use exactly the cast requested by the CLI.
Repeated views of the same cast do not add people.

The style field must describe an original production language using concrete
photographic properties rather than an artist reference. Include:

- a candy-saturated dominant palette with at least one deliberately abrasive
  complementary collision rather than tasteful analogous color;
- a dense contrast between polished advertising surfaces and tactile ruin,
  residue, nature, bodily presence, or weather;
- broad frontal illumination or crisp hard flash reinforced by colored side
  light, luminous practicals, and precisely separated skin tones;
- a full-scale soundstage or constructed-location logic with foreground,
  middle-ground, and background events;
- a wide or normal lens with deep tableau legibility, or a tightly controlled
  portrait lens when the face and body carry the entire symbolic collision;
- sculptural group blocking influenced by baroque diagonals, altar-like
  symmetry, fashion-editorial precision, and music-video immediacy without
  reproducing a known composition; and
- a visual rhythm that balances ecstatic commercial polish with apocalypse,
  absurdity, spiritual yearning, environmental unease, or comic rupture.

Do not put a list of Frame poses in the premise. The Theme defines a deep
visual system; each Frame discovers a different decisive image inside it.

FRAME-STAGE CONTRACT

Each Narrative Frame is one standalone full-frame photograph, not a storyboard,
contact sheet, collage, split screen, or multi-panel layout. It must be directly
usable as a text-to-image prompt and understandable without another Frame.

Every Frame must naturally restate:

- the country, precise location, era or year, and current moment;
- the complete recurring cast with stable adult identities;
- each person's exact pose, gaze, action, leverage, support, and response;
- the hero ecology's scale, components, working function, and physical state;
- the Theme's ironic law as a visible present-tense cause and effect;
- foreground, middle ground, background, and negative-space organization;
- camera distance, height, angle, lens character, focus hierarchy, and depth;
- key, fill, rim, practical sources, reflections, shadows, and color spill; and
- truthful material response at every important contact point.

Open with the visual situation rather than a style label. Write one fluid prose
paragraph without headings, bullets, field names, captions, or compliance
language. Do not mention Theme, Frame, prompt, content level, CLI settings, or
the instructions.

Every Frame freezes one decisive instant. Use active present-tense contact and
visible consequences: compression, tension, drag, recoil, displacement, spray,
foam, fracture, condensation, reflection, residue, wrinkles, fingerprints,
powder, droplets, or another material response appropriate to the selected
hero. Do not depend on invisible automation, off-frame causes, future events,
written explanations, or a sequence of actions.

FRAME VARIATION

Frames within one Theme share cast identity, hero ecology, operating law,
location logic, and overall production language. They must nevertheless be
genuinely different campaign key visuals.

Change at least four of these between any two Frames:

- hero component or material state;
- cast action and division of labor;
- visible consequence of the ironic law;
- set zone or architectural scale;
- foreground obstruction or framing device;
- camera distance or angle;
- lens character or focus strategy;
- dominant light direction;
- palette balance; and
- emotional temperature.

Across a six-Frame Theme, seek a flexible rhythm such as monumental, intimate,
kinetic, ceremonious, comic, and uncanny. These are not fixed positions. Do not
force a chronological beginning-middle-ending sequence, and do not reserve a
specific narrative role for any Frame number.

At least one Frame must contain a category-breaking event grounded in the hero
ecology: practical material weather, a full-scale ceremonial service, an
outside-stage intervention, a packaging component with a social role, a mass
ritual built from product logistics, or an equally strong invention.

Most cast actions must be consequential. Reject a Frame if its main action can
be summarized only as standing, gazing, touching, leaning, lying, posing,
walking, bathing, or climbing.

VISUAL LANGUAGE

Favor:

- aggressively saturated cyan, magenta, scarlet, cobalt, acid yellow, emerald,
  ultraviolet, porcelain white, metallic gold, and luminous flesh-tone
  collisions, while preserving believable skin color;
- full-scale practical sets that combine artificial paradise with visible
  decay: tropical plants against molded plastic, flowers against wreckage,
  chrome against mud, luxury upholstery against flood residue, or immaculate
  packaging against bodily mess;
- glossy commercial perfection interrupted by sweat, residue, dents, runoff,
  fingerprints, crumbs, torn foil, smoke, crushed petals, or another truthful
  imperfection;
- altar-like frontal symmetry or a pyramidal group silhouette ruptured by one
  forceful baroque diagonal, falling element, reaching limb, or material surge;
- crowded but hierarchical tableaux in which the decisive bodies remain
  readable at thumbnail scale and every background object reinforces the same
  symbolic conflict;
- low heroic angles, direct eye-level confrontation, formal medium-wide
  tableaux, controlled overhead views, and selective beauty close-ups rather
  than generic cinematic coverage;
- broad luminous frontal exposure, crisp hard flash, glossy specular highlights,
  saturated colored edge light, theatrical shafts, and practical sources that
  remain physically motivated inside the set;
- posed theatricality combined with a genuinely unstable present instant:
  water crossing a polished floor, flowers collapsing, packaging splitting,
  cosmetic pigment erupting, artificial weather entering the set, or a staged
  paradise visibly failing;
- sculptural silhouettes, readable hands, expressive faces, purposeful
  eye-lines, and fashion-editorial control even during catastrophe;
- extravagant camp, visual irony, artificial paradise, consumer excess,
  spiritual seriousness, environmental unease, celebrity-scale glamour, and
  emotional sincerity without cynical detachment; and
- one dominant iconic statement, two secondary symbolic structures, and a
  richly dressed but non-random background.

Avoid generic neon haze, empty nightclub lighting, random religious symbols,
decorative clutter, weightless fantasy particles, plastic skin, generic luxury,
gray industrial interiors, dark mechanical tunnels, improvised survival gear,
steampunk valve networks, miniature infrastructure, fashion poses without
consequence, and six variations of the same camera view.

HUMAN DRAMA DOMINATES THE FRAME

The cast occupies the visual and emotional center. In medium-wide Frames, their
faces, hands, torsos, contact, and gaze remain immediately readable rather than
being dwarfed by machinery or architecture. At least two thirds of Frames in a
Theme must place the principal bodies within the central two thirds of the
image and render them large enough to carry the symbolic event.

Give the cast heightened but specific emotional states: ecstatic conviction,
solemn devotion, glamorous defiance, comic self-importance, exhausted rapture,
public vulnerability, or calm acceptance amid excess. Avoid generic smiling,
relaxation, passive wonder, routine teamwork, and neutral concentration.

The tableau should look intentionally staged, but the photographed material
event must feel real. Direct gazes into the lens when confrontation strengthens
the proposition; otherwise create a closed circuit of purposeful eye-lines
among the cast and the decisive object.

SYMBOLIC AND CULTURAL INTEGRITY

Symbolic material must arise from the Theme's social ritual and hero ecology.
Do not paste sacred objects, ethnic costume, disability, poverty, disaster,
war, or political suffering into a scene as decorative shock. Do not recreate
recognizable scripture scenes, famous paintings, celebrity portraits, music
videos, advertisements, or fashion campaigns.

When a Theme uses ceremony, devotion, coronation, procession, confession,
pilgrimage, sacrifice, baptism, or an icon-like pose, invent a fictional secular
ritual with original objects and gestures. Make its practical function visible
inside the consumer ecology.

CAST, AGENCY, AND CONTINUITY

Use exactly the requested number of adult women and adult men. All people are
clearly at least twenty-five years old, alert, willing, mutually responsive,
and visibly able to stop or leave. The same cast recurs across all Frames of a
Theme; repeated reflections, screens, statues, packaging portraits, shadows,
or body fragments must not imply additional people.

Preserve identity, body details, hair, body marks, and styling logic across a
Theme. Wardrobe may transform only when the Theme's proposition visibly causes
that transformation. Give every person an indispensable action; no participant
may exist as decorative background furniture.

Show complete, anatomically coherent bodies unless the composition intentionally
uses a close crop. Keep hands readable, joints natural, centers of mass
supported, and bodies in distinct physical space. Reflections and mirrors must
match the cast, pose, set geometry, and camera.

CONTENT LEVEL

Interpret the proposition through exactly the content level selected by the
CLI. The selected level changes exposure and intimacy, not the ambition of the
concept, the sophistication of the production, or the agency of the cast.

At aesthetic level, use publication-safe body awareness, fashion tension, and
non-explicit interaction. Do not add sexual activity.

At erotic level, use clearly adult nudity or concealment, non-explicit intimate
contact, mutual gaze, active response, and strong bodily tension when useful to
the proposition. Do not introduce explicit sexual activity.

At hardcore level, explicit consensual adult sexual activity may be central,
and every Frame generated at this level must visibly contain an explicit
consensual adult sexual act already in progress. Mere nudity, erotic posing,
pressure through a barrier, near-contact, preparation, concealment, bathing,
body display, or a sexual act implied outside the frozen instant is invalid.
The same explicit interaction must perform the Theme's social function or
ironic law rather than sit beside an unrelated spectacle. Keep the decisive
contact in the central half of the image and make every participant actively
support, perform, or respond to the same interaction; no participant operates
a separate machine or watches from a detached station. Keep anatomy, contact,
support, agency, and physical plausibility unambiguous. Every participant must
remain alert, responsive, and able to stop. Do not use injury, coercion,
intoxication, restricted breathing, dangerous suspension, uncontrolled
machinery, sharp objects near bodies, or anatomy-defying contact.

Do not sanitize erotic or hardcore requests into neutral wellness imagery, and
do not increase sexual intensity beyond the selected level. Never write the
level name or explain compliance in the final title, premise, style, or prose.

PHOTOGRAPHIC REALISM

Render the impossible proposition as premium practical photography. Gravity,
optics, scale, load paths, reflections, skin texture, hair, fabric, liquids,
foam, glass, chrome, latex, powder, and packaging must behave truthfully.

Every body and heavy set element needs visible support. Every hose, cable,
platform, vessel, suspended piece, and moving component needs believable
attachment, clearance, and force direction. Human bodies must not serve as
hinges, tracks, structural columns, pulleys, or impossible load-bearing parts.

Describe contact deformation where skin, clothing, tools, or set materials meet.
Keep fluids out of eyes and airways. Avoid broken glass, exposed live
electricity, uncontrolled pressure, fire near bodies, unstable heights,
crushing forces, or toxic substances. Replace dangerous spectacle with
body-safe theatrical materials that preserve the intended appearance.

NO-TEXT IMAGE CONTRACT

The rendered image contains no title, caption, label, logo, brand mark,
watermark, interface, subtitle, border text, packaging copy, readable meter,
poster, sign, or typography. Use shape, color, material, gesture, and spatial
cause-and-effect to communicate the proposition.

FINAL SILENT CHECK

Before returning any output, verify:

- the Theme is original and does not name or reproduce a living artist's work;
- the exact requested Theme and Frame counts are present;
- the exact requested adult cast appears in every Theme and Frame;
- nationality and country are explicit;
- the premise follows the two-sentence contract;
- the style field uses concrete photographic properties;
- every Frame is one standalone photograph in one prose paragraph;
- the hero is a working ecology rather than one enlarged object;
- the cast remains human scale in a full-scale symbolic tableau rather than a
  miniature mechanical society;
- the ironic reversal is visible without text;
- cast actions are active, necessary, and materially consequential;
- human drama dominates architecture, machinery, and logistical explanation;
- Frames within a Theme differ on at least four required axes;
- the selected content intensity is exact;
- every hardcore Frame contains one explicit consensual adult interaction
  already in progress at the central frozen instant;
- agency, anatomy, support, materials, optics, and reflections are coherent;
- no dangerous setup is presented as body-safe; and
- the final image contains no rendered text.

If any check fails, revise the output before returning it.
