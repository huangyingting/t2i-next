BRIEF

Create original, hyper-realistic editorial social-caricature Themes about
ordinary adult relationships. Expose a recognizable contradiction in romantic
relationships, marriage, workplace hierarchy, friendship, shared domestic
labor, family expectations, status, attention, credit, care, or petty daily
friction.

Distort faces, expressions, gestures, and body proportions to enlarge the
participants' performed flaws, defenses, desires, or emotional reactions. The
result must be funny at first glance and uncomfortably truthful on reflection.
Satirize conduct and relationship patterns, never gender, nationality,
ethnicity, age, disability, body type, or identity itself.

Every image uses a clean, minimal stage, one dominant interaction, and a small
amount of exact English copy that states the central opposition. Do not build a
cluttered naturalistic room or scatter many symbolic props.

CAST AUTHORITY

Use exactly the requested number of adult women and exactly the requested
number of adult men. Never infer, default, hard-code, add, omit, merge, clone,
or replace a person. Add no child, bystander, assistant, crowd, portrait,
reflection, screen figure, statue, mannequin, shadow person, or detached body
fragment resembling another human.

Before inventing the relationship, derive one immutable cast declaration from
cast_constraints. Begin every Theme premise and every Frame with this exact
dynamic sentence:

    Exactly [TOTAL IN WORDS] Chinese [ADULT/ADULTS] fill the image:
    [FEMALE PHRASE], [MALE PHRASE], and no other human figures.

Replace each field from the request. Use "one adult woman", "two adult women",
"one adult man", and "two adult men" with correct grammar; omit a gender
phrase when its requested count is zero. Never copy a cast declaration from an
example or another Theme. When the requested male count is zero, the Theme and
Frame must contain no man, male role, husband, boyfriend, father, male pronoun,
or masculine name. When the requested female count is zero, apply the
equivalent exclusion to women. Restate the same total and gender composition
near the end of every Frame.

Every participant must be a fictional, unmistakably mature adult aged 25 or
older. Unless the Story Description explicitly states otherwise, make every
person Chinese and set the Theme in China. Give each adult a distinct original
face, age, build, hairstyle, content-level-appropriate body-coverage state,
social role, current action, exaggeration, and response.

Show every requested person prominently from head to foot. Every person has
exactly one head, one continuous torso, two attached arms, two hands, two
attached legs, and two feet. Keep limbs traceable, ownership unambiguous,
contacts reachable, and support physically coherent despite deliberate
caricatural scale changes.

CAST-TO-RELATIONSHIP SELECTION

Choose a relationship premise compatible with the actual requested cast. The
following are priorities, not fixed plots:

- one adult: the person's relationship with domestic labor, self-image,
  social performance, attention, consumption, perfectionism, procrastination,
  or the pressure to handle everything alone;
- two women and no men: supervisor and subordinate, colleagues dividing labor
  and credit, sisters, friends, roommates, neighbors, mentor and protege, or
  two women refusing an externally imposed rivalry;
- one woman and one man: spouses, long-term partners, dates, colleagues,
  neighbors, siblings, or friends caught in unequal care, selective listening,
  weaponized incompetence, emotional scorekeeping, status performance, or a
  petty dispute that conceals a deeper need;
- two women and one man: manufactured romantic competition, workplace
  triangulation, alliance against manipulation, unequal credit, family
  mediation, friendship under pressure, or one participant using the other two
  to validate status;
- one woman and two men: emotional labor, workplace hierarchy, competitive
  posturing, family expectations, alliance, gatekeeping, or two participants
  transferring responsibility to the third;
- all-woman or all-man groups of other sizes: friendship, family, household,
  peer, workplace, status, care, and credit dynamics suited to the exact cast;
  and
- larger mixed groups: one coherent relationship network with a single
  readable division of labor, attention, access, responsibility, or credit.

When the Story Description explicitly names a relationship or situation,
honor it instead of selecting another when it is compatible with the requested
content level and the safety rules below. At erotic and hardcore levels, use
solo self-relationship or consenting adult spouses, established partners,
dates, lovers, or peer-status intimate partners. Never sexualize relatives,
workplace authority, mentorship, caregiving dependency, financial dependency,
housing dependency, or another power relationship in which refusal could carry
a penalty. Do not default to jealousy, romance, gender conflict, or women
competing for a man. Give women and men agency, flaws, strategies, and
reactions according to the specific behavior being satirized rather than a
gender stereotype.

CORE SATIRICAL EQUATION

Build every Theme from one indivisible visual equation:

    one ordinary relationship friction
    + one unequal desire or false performance
    + one content-level-specific shared physical struggle
    + one dominant metaphor mechanism made from an object or body arrangement
    + one opposed pair of English text phrases
    = one instantly readable social truth

The contradiction must be visible without explanation. Examples include work
moving downward while credit moves upward, one partner demanding care while
pretending not to need it, a tiny domestic object carrying an enormous
emotional argument, a self-important person manufacturing rivalry, everybody
waiting for somebody else to act, or generosity performed only when observed.
Invent original equivalents rather than copying these examples by default.

Privately complete this sentence before accepting a concept:

    Because [person] wants [unequal desire], they [content-level-specific
    physical action], causing [shared object or body arrangement] to [visible
    mechanical change], which forces every other person to [specific
    exaggerated response] and reveals [social truth].

Do not print that planning sentence. Reject a concept if removing any person,
the shared object, or the physical action leaves the same joke intact.

ONE CLOSED INTERACTION

Every requested adult must participate in one closed action-and-reaction
circuit. Favor one strong verb opposition such as pull/resist, give/take,
push/block, speak/listen, claim/carry, inflate/deflate, display/conceal,
delegate/complete, approach/withdraw, or accuse/defend.

Show the action at its most revealing instant. State:

- what the initiating person is doing;
- the exact hand, foot, torso, or gaze involved;
- the direction of force;
- what another participant does in response;
- how the selected object or body-position system visibly bends, stretches,
  tilts, compresses, inflates, deflates, slides, redirects weight, or changes
  state; and
- how that change returns pressure or consequence to the initiator.

Every participant must touch, pull, push, brace, carry, block, redirect, or
visibly react to the same interaction. A gaze, facial reaction, recoil,
observation, or symbolic presence alone is insufficient: every adult must make
direct hand, foot, or supported torso contact with the selected metaphor object
or with another participant who is transmitting force through the body-position
system. Do not call a disconnected observer "integral." Do not pose disconnected
people beside separate symbols. Expressions must correspond to the current
force and must not look like generic smiling, surprise, or anger.

At erotic and hardcore levels, intimate body contact and sexual positioning
may transmit the force directly, so the bodies themselves can be the primary
metaphor mechanism. In that case, do not add an unrelated symbolic object to
explain what the body arrangement already makes visible.

EXTREME FACIAL PERFORMANCE

Give every adult one coherent facial-caricature cluster that combines two or
three visibly altered features. Suitable changes include:

- enlarged or narrowed eyes with an exact gaze target;
- asymmetrically raised, sharply lowered, or hook-shaped brows;
- a widened megaphone mouth, compressed line mouth, triangular shout, smug
  crescent, or tiny guilty O-shaped mouth;
- inflated cheeks, tightened cheek muscles, or a widened jaw;
- a raised or compressed nose with visibly flared nostrils; and
- hair volume or direction that reinforces the reaction.

Make the emotional contrast unmistakable. Productive pairings include smug
versus furious, performatively cheerful versus exhausted, dismissive versus
wounded, triumphant versus trapped, self-important versus unconvinced, or
manipulative versus newly allied.

Name the altered features and their relationship to the action. Do not write
only "an exaggerated expression." Preserve one coherent face with two eyes,
one nose, and one mouth. Do not add facial organs, detach features, melt skin,
create a mask, or make an adult look childlike.

BODY-SILHOUETTE CARICATURE

Give every adult one or more coordinated nonfacial proportion changes that
expose their role in the relationship. Choose one or combine compatible
changes:

- one attached arm elongated to two or three times normal length;
- both attached hands enlarged to two or three times normal size;
- the complete head and hair enlarged to one and a half times normal size;
- the continuous upper torso widened to twice normal width;
- the complete torso compressed to one-half normal width;
- the whole body stretched to one and a half times normal height;
- the whole body compressed to three-fifths normal height; or
- one garment volume inflated to three times normal width around an otherwise
  complete body.

Each altered part must actively perform the satire by gripping, pulling,
pointing, carrying, blocking, claiming, shielding, inflating, or redirecting
the central interaction. State the numeric scale explicitly in every Frame.
Keep adjacent joints attached and readable. Never enlarge breasts, buttocks,
genitals, a pregnant abdomen, or another sexual characteristic for humor.

Layered changes are allowed: for example, one existing arm may be elongated
while its attached hand is enlarged, or an enlarged head may accompany a
widened torso. Every change must affect existing anatomy only. Never add,
remove, duplicate, detach, fuse, or replace a head, torso, arm, hand, leg,
foot, joint, or finger. Preserve a continuous shoulder-elbow-wrist-hand chain
for each arm and a continuous hip-knee-ankle-foot chain for each leg. Keep both
legs and both feet visible, anatomically attached, and physically supported;
they need not bear weight when a support or another consenting adult safely
carries that weight. Avoid broken joints, melted anatomy, accidental body
horror, or a change that exists only because of lens perspective.

In every Theme premise and Frame, serialize the body changes once in this exact
dynamic form:

    Body exaggerations: [PERSON] - [FEATURE] at [NUMERIC SCALE], [OPTIONAL
    ADDITIONAL FEATURE] at [NUMERIC SCALE]; [PERSON] - [FEATURE] at [NUMERIC
    SCALE]; all unlisted body parts remain at normal scale.

Include every requested adult exactly once and list every changed feature for
that person in the same entry. Coordinated layered changes may share one entry.
After this sentence, never introduce a body change that was not declared in
the person's entry.

In every Frame this must be the second sentence, immediately after the dynamic
cast declaration and before wardrobe, face, action, or setting. If later prose
uses a size word for a body feature - including enlarged, elongated, widened,
compressed, stretched, reduced, giant, tiny, massive, or oversized - that
feature and scale must already appear in the person's body entry. Ordinary
action words such as grips, braces, points, or pulls must not imply extra,
missing, detached, or anatomically discontinuous limbs.

In every Theme premise and Frame, serialize physical participation once,
immediately after the body sentence. Use the one form matching the requested
content level:

    Contact map: force chain - [EACH ADULT'S BODY PART, TARGET, AND FORCE].

    Contact map: sensual contact - [CURRENT NON-EXPLICIT SELF-CONTACT OR
    RECIPROCAL CONTACT]; force and support - [EACH ADULT'S BODY PART, TARGET,
    AND FORCE].

    Contact map: defining sexual contact - [CURRENT EXPLICIT ANATOMICAL
    CONTACT]; force and support - [EACH ADULT'S BODY PART, TARGET, AND FORCE].

Use the first form for aesthetic, the second for erotic, and the third for
hardcore. Account for every requested adult at least once; an identifier may
repeat when that person has both defining and support contacts. Every contact
must be sustained in the depicted instant and must transmit, resist, or
redirect force in the one shared interaction. Hovering near, gesturing toward,
pointing at, presenting, watching, recoiling, celebrating, or holding raised
arms does not count as contact. Later action prose must preserve the contact
map exactly.

The contact map must prove the requested content level rather than merely claim
it later. At aesthetic level, map the nonsexual force chain. At erotic level,
map at least one sustained sensual self-contact for a single adult or
reciprocal sensual contact for every adult in a group. At hardcore level, map
the defining current genital, oral, or manual sexual contact for every adult,
plus any hand, foot, limb, or support contact needed to make the geometry safe
and readable. A hardcore contact map containing only hands, feet, posing,
bracing, or nearby body placement is invalid.

CLEAN BACKGROUND AND METAPHOR BUDGET

Use a constructed editorial stage rather than a furnished room. The background
must contain:

- one broad matte color field or very smooth two-color gradient;
- zero environmental depth and no visible wall-floor corner;
- at most two simple geometric location cues;
- exactly one primary metaphor mechanism shared by the whole cast, built from
  either one object or the cast's own body-position and force system; and
- at most one small supporting object from the same situation.

When an object carries the primary metaphor, it occupies roughly 25 to 45
percent of the canvas and participates mechanically in the action. Suitable
object families include one document, rope, scale, chair, sofa, table, door,
balloon, phone, toothpaste cap, alarm clock, paper stack, umbrella, conveyor,
trophy, mirror, or another ordinary object specific to the chosen
contradiction. When the cast's body arrangement carries the primary metaphor,
use no separate symbolic object; one simple support such as a bed, sofa, chair,
or platform may establish safe contact and must not compete as a second
metaphor.

Do not include room inventory, scattered household objects, shelves, plants,
wall art, windows, screens, incidental furniture, distant architecture,
debris, decorative signage, crowds, or multiple competing metaphors. Preserve
at least 25 percent clean negative space around the central interaction.

Every visible surface must be blank except for the two locked English phrases.
Do not decorate paper, books, reports, boards, screens, clothing, walls, or
props with diagrams, formulas, handwriting, charts, icons, interface marks,
fake fine print, or ornamental lettering.

If a selected object normally contains information - including a schedule,
report, chart, clipboard, phone, book, control panel, calendar, form, or
packaging - render its entire surface blank except for the two locked phrases.
Do not mention dense formatting, lines of copy, list entries, data, controls,
page contents, or marks that the image model could interpret as additional
writing.

ENGLISH-ONLY IMAGE COPY

Every Theme must invent exactly two short opposed English phrases that clarify
the behavior already visible in the image. The phrases are not explanations
of an invisible joke; they label the two forces, claims, excuses, or truths in
the current interaction.

Each phrase must:

- contain one to four words;
- use only uppercase ASCII letters A through Z, spaces, apostrophes, question
  marks, or exclamation marks;
- contain no more than 18 characters including spaces and punctuation;
- be correctly spelled, grammatical, concrete, and immediately readable;
- differ meaningfully from the other phrase; and
- avoid names, brands, hashtags, numbers, abbreviations, fine print, and
  generic words such as GOOD, BAD, YES, or NO by themselves.

Examples of productive opposition include "MY SUCCESS" versus "HER WORK",
"YOU NEVER CARE!" versus "JUST A CAP?", "FIGHT FOR ME" versus "WE OPT OUT",
"NOT DONE YET?" versus "I'LL DO IT", "EASY LIFE" versus "ONE MORE THING",
or "I'M LISTENING" versus "LET ME SPEAK".
These examples show brevity and parallel conflict; invent new phrases suited to
each Theme instead of repeatedly copying them.

Integrate each phrase into a diegetic carrier that already belongs naturally
to the action and environment. Prefer lettering stamped, painted, engraved,
embossed, woven, stitched, printed, or projected onto the broad face of the
primary object, two existing regions of one support, garment panels, bedding,
upholstery, or one simple geometric stage cue. Do not introduce separate props
solely to hold the words. Do not use floating editorial caption blocks,
speech balloons, subtitles, interface overlays, detached placards, or text
hovering in empty space.

The lettering must inherit the carrier's perspective, curvature, folds,
surface texture, material response, lighting direction, shadow, and degree of
wear while remaining fully readable. Its type treatment and color must belong
to the scene's palette rather than looking pasted on afterward. Keep both
phrases in the central 80 percent of the image, away from faces, hands, body
contours, and each other. Use large bold horizontal lettering with enough
material contrast for immediate legibility.

Lock the pair in every Theme premise with this exact English syntax:

    Locked image copy: "FIRST PHRASE" | "SECOND PHRASE".

Every Frame must name each carrier and reproduce both locked phrases verbatim
inside straight double quotation marks. End every Frame with this exact
sentence, replacing only the two bracketed phrases:

    The only visible writing is the exact English copy "[FIRST PHRASE]" and
    "[SECOND PHRASE]"; both phrases are large, correctly spelled, complete,
    horizontal, unobstructed, and clearly legible, with no Chinese characters,
    pinyin, bilingual text, gibberish, random letters, numbers, logos, fine
    print, or watermark.

Do not translate, paraphrase, punctuate differently, pluralize, or restyle the
locked copy between Theme and Frame. Do not place other writing on documents,
phones, clocks, clothing, walls, signs, or props.

CONTENT-LEVEL SATIRE COUPLING

Honor the exact requested content level in the Theme and every Frame. Content
intensity must determine the central action, contact geometry, metaphor
mechanism, expressions, copy, and social contradiction. Never create a generic
relationship satire and add nudity or sex afterward. Conversely, never create
an unrelated erotic tableau beside a symbolic prop. Removing the
content-level-defining interaction must break the visual joke.

At aesthetic level, every adult wears a complete opaque outfit and the shared
interaction is nonsexual. Make ordinary actions such as carrying, cleaning,
waiting, listening, interrupting, claiming, spending, delegating, comforting,
or refusing physically expose unequal labor, attention, responsibility,
credit, access, or emotional effort.

At erotic level, use unmistakably adult but non-explicit sensual contact,
intentional exposure or concealment, reciprocal proximity, gaze, touch,
support, approach, withdrawal, or body orientation as the active metaphor
mechanism. The sensual configuration must reveal who seeks attention, who
withholds it, who performs desirability, who bears physical or emotional
effort, or whether apparent competition becomes alliance. Do not show explicit
sexual acts or pornographic anatomical contact. For one adult, require
intentional sensual self-contact whose effort or presentation carries the
satire; for multiple adults, require reciprocal sensual contact that accounts
for everyone. A glamorous outfit, passive nudity, ordinary embrace, or romantic
mood alone does not satisfy this level.

At hardcore level, show one clearly visible consensual adult sexual interaction
already occurring in every Theme and Frame. Name the current act, participating
anatomy needed for spatial clarity, each person's active role, and the exact
supported contact geometry; do not substitute preparation, implication,
aftermath, or euphemism. For a single adult, a solo act may satirize
self-performance, perfectionism, productivity pressure, consumption, or the
need to appear desirable. For two or more adults, every requested person must
actively give, receive, support, guide, or redirect contact within the same
consensual act.

The explicit act cannot exist only in the content-level proof sentence. Within
the first 80 words after the four mandatory opening sentences, redescribe its
defining current contact and unobstructed spatial relationship as visible image
content. Do not replace this with a nearby kneel, embrace, suggestive pose,
almost-contact, strategically hidden anatomy, or support gesture.

At hardcore level, use position, orientation, rhythm, weight support, reach,
initiative, attention, visible effort, control, reciprocity, or distribution
of pleasure to carry the social metaphor. For example, an above/below
configuration may expose unequal labor or performative dominance only when the
current actions and reactions visibly establish that inequality. Being above,
below, standing, seated, kneeling, penetrating, or receiving is never
inherently superior, inferior, masculine, feminine, selfish, or submissive.
Satirize the participants' specific behavior and negotiated relationship
pattern, never a sexual position, consensual preference, anatomy, gender, or
identity.

Vary the intimacy-based metaphor rather than defaulting to above/below:

- who safely supports another person's weight can expose invisible care or
  unequal physical labor;
- who reaches, turns toward, or turns away can expose pursuit, avoidance, or
  unequal bids for connection;
- who sets or adapts to pace can expose communication, impatience, or
  unilateral decision-making;
- who receives attention while another supplies effort can expose one-sided
  reciprocity or performative generosity;
- who occupies the visual center while another creates the supporting
  structure can expose credit, visibility, or status appropriation;
- synchronized versus mismatched movement can expose listening, negotiation,
  emotional presence, or scorekeeping;
- a requested three-or-more-person cast can expose monopolized attention,
  manufactured rivalry, coalition, mutual aid, or a group refusing one
  participant's status performance, but nobody may become a spectator; and
- a single adult's pose, self-touch, mirrorless self-presentation, or repeated
  effort can expose self-surveillance, perfectionism, loneliness, consumption,
  or pressure to perform desirability.

Select one of these causal dimensions or invent an equally concrete
alternative. Do not combine several messages, and do not reuse the same
position-based joke across Themes.

All erotic and hardcore participants remain unmistakably adult, alert,
willing, actively responsive, physically supported, able to breathe, and able
to stop. Never make sex a workplace demand, payment, punishment, coercive
bargain, proof of loyalty, or condition of status, money, housing, employment,
care, or safety. No content level permits coercion, intoxication,
unconsciousness, injury, restricted breathing, unsafe suspension, or
humiliation based on identity.

Serialize the requested level once in every Theme premise and Frame:

    Content-level proof: aesthetic - [OPAQUE WARDROBE AND NONSEXUAL ACTION
    THAT CARRIES THE SATIRE].

    Content-level proof: erotic - [VISIBLE NON-EXPLICIT SENSUAL SELF-CONTACT
    OR RECIPROCAL CONTACT APPROPRIATE TO THE CAST, AND THE RELATIONSHIP
    OPPOSITION IT PHYSICALLY CARRIES].

    Content-level proof: hardcore - [CURRENT CONSENSUAL EXPLICIT ACT,
    SUPPORTED CONTACT GEOMETRY, ACTIVE ROLE OF EVERY ADULT, AND THE
    RELATIONSHIP OPPOSITION THE POSITION OR EFFORT PHYSICALLY CARRIES].

Use only the line matching the requested content level. This must be the
fourth sentence, immediately after the contact map. Do not mention a rule,
rating, or future action; state only what is visibly present now.

THEME CONTRACT

Every Theme defines one complete caricature system:

- the exact requested cast and each adult's relationship role;
- one ordinary friction and one uncomfortable social truth;
- one initiating action and one closed group reaction circuit;
- one shared primary metaphor mechanism, using either one object or one
  body-position system, and at most one supporting object;
- one facial-caricature cluster per adult;
- all numeric body-proportion changes for every adult;
- one clean matte background and no environmental clutter;
- one locked pair of short English phrases integrated into scene-compatible
  diegetic carriers;
- one content-level proof whose action physically carries the satire;
- one frontal or slightly elevated camera relationship; and
- one high-contrast palette and simple motivated lighting system.

Give each Theme one concise natural title. Its premise must use exactly four
sentences to identify the complete cast, relationship, contradiction,
shared action, metaphor, exaggerations, and locked English copy. Its style must
be one flowing sentence defining the clean stage, camera, crop, palette,
lighting, photographic realism, and caricatural finish.

The Theme premise's first sentence must begin with the dynamic cast
declaration. Its second sentence must begin with "Body exaggerations:", list
each person exactly once, and end with "all unlisted body parts remain at
normal scale." Its third sentence must begin with "Contact map:", list each
person exactly once, and state the force-transmitting contact. Its fourth
sentence must begin with the matching "Content-level proof:" prefix, state the
relationship contradiction, content-level-specific action, dominant metaphor
mechanism, and locked image-copy pair. Do not place an undeclared body change,
alternate contact, or contradictory content-level action elsewhere in the
premise.

Themes must differ substantially in relationship type, unequal desire,
interaction verb, metaphor mechanism, facial contrast, body deformation, copy
pair, palette, and composition. Do not assign concepts according to Theme ID
or reuse one domestic argument with different props.

FRAME CONTRACT

Every Frame is one standalone full-frame editorial photograph, not a
storyboard, collage, split screen, before-and-after image, or multi-panel
layout. Multiple Frames within one Theme are parallel visual alternatives, not
chronological steps.

Write every final Frame in English regardless of the requested output
language. Use one unbroken natural-language paragraph. The first sentence must
be the exact dynamic cast declaration and exclude all additional people. The
second sentence must be the complete "Body exaggerations:" serialization. The
third sentence must be the complete "Contact map:" serialization. The fourth
sentence must be the matching "Content-level proof:" serialization. Do
not introduce a name, wardrobe, face, action, prop, or setting before these
four mandatory sentences are complete.
Naturally include:

- every adult's Chinese identity, age of at least 25, stable appearance,
  content-level-appropriate wardrobe or nudity state, relationship role, and
  full-body placement;
- each person's exact facial alterations, gaze target, expression, and all
  numeric body-proportion changes;
- the initiating action, force direction, every participant's response, and
  the selected object's or body-position system's present consequence;
- the primary object or body-position mechanism and sole optional supporting
  object;
- the social truth made visible by the interaction;
- the matte background, negative space, camera, crop, focus, and lighting;
- the exact two English phrases, their carriers, size, color, and placement;
  and
- the mandatory final English-copy sentence.

Near the end, repeat: "Cast remains exactly [FEMALE PHRASE] and [MALE
PHRASE], with no other people." Omit a zero-count gender phrase and preserve
grammatical English.

Use a frontal, eye-level, or mildly elevated normal-lens composition that keeps
every person fully visible from the top of the head through both feet and keeps
all faces, hands, distortions, contact, and copy readable. The frame edge must
not crop hair, head, shoulders, elbows, hands, hips, knees, shins, ankles, or
feet. Avoid extreme wide angle, fisheye, severe foreshortening, shallow focus,
body-crossing foregrounds, dramatic location depth, or any crop through a
person.

Render real adult skin, pores, hair, eyes, fabric weave, object texture, and
contact deformation inside deliberately impossible caricatural proportions.
The image may resemble a high-end satirical magazine campaign, staged
photomontage, or hyper-real editorial tableau, but must not resemble a generic
lifestyle photograph.

FINAL REJECTION GATE

Reject and rewrite any Theme or Frame containing:

- the wrong number or gender composition;
- a missing, duplicated, hidden, cropped, reflected, or extra person;
- a person disconnected from the shared interaction;
- a generic expression without named altered facial features;
- a body distortion without an exact scale and causal role;
- a Frame whose second sentence is not the complete "Body exaggerations:"
  serialization;
- a Theme premise or Frame whose third sentence is not the complete "Contact
  map:" serialization accounting for every requested adult;
- a Theme premise or Frame whose fourth sentence is not the matching
  "Content-level proof:" serialization;
- a body change described later that was not declared in that person's body
  entry;
- an extra, missing, duplicated, detached, fused, replaced, or untraceable
  head, torso, arm, hand, leg, foot, joint, or finger;
- an interrupted shoulder-elbow-wrist-hand or hip-knee-ankle-foot chain;
- a requested person's second leg or foot hidden so completely that the body
  appears anatomically incomplete;
- more than one primary metaphor mechanism or more than one supporting object;
- diagrams, formulas, charts, handwriting, icons, fake fine print, or any
  unlocked marking on any visible surface;
- a normally text-bearing object described as dense, formatted, filled,
  written, scheduled, diagrammed, marked, or detailed beyond the two locked
  phrases;
- a cluttered room, busy background, signage, or decorative prop collection;
- a phrase shown as a floating caption, subtitle, speech balloon, interface
  overlay, detached placard, or other non-diegetic addition;
- lettering whose perspective, curvature, material, lighting, shadow, wear, or
  palette conflicts with its carrier or environment;
- a new prop introduced only to carry text;
- weak interaction that could be replaced by people merely posing;
- hovering, pointing, presenting, raised arms, or another noncontact gesture
  substituted for sustained physical contact;
- satire aimed at identity rather than conduct;
- aesthetic output containing sexualized contact or exposure;
- erotic output lacking unmistakable intentional sensual contact appropriate
  to the cast, exceeding into an explicit sexual act, or using sensuality only
  as decoration;
- hardcore output that is implicit, preparatory, euphemistic, after-the-fact,
  missing a requested adult from the current consensual act, or spatially
  unclear;
- a hardcore contact map that records only support or pose contacts and omits
  the defining current sexual contact for any requested adult;
- a hardcore Frame that claims an explicit act in its proof but does not
  visibly redescribe the defining contact within the next 80 words;
- erotic or hardcore satire that remains unchanged when its intimate action,
  position, effort, or contact is removed;
- a sexual position or consensual preference treated as inherently superior,
  inferior, masculine, feminine, selfish, or submissive;
- coercion, incapacity, restricted breathing, injury, unsafe support, or a
  sexual bargain tied to work, money, housing, care, or safety;
- copy longer than four words or 18 characters;
- missing, altered, translated, misspelled, obstructed, or unreadable copy;
- a phrase longer than four words or 18 total characters;
- a camera crop that hides any part of a requested person's head-to-foot body;
- Chinese characters, pinyin, bilingual text, random letters, numbers,
  unplanned writing, logos, or watermarks; or
- an image whose social truth requires an external explanation.
