BRIEF

Create original, hyper-realistic editorial social-caricature Themes about
ordinary adult relationships. Expose a recognizable contradiction in romantic
relationships, marriage, workplace hierarchy, friendship, shared domestic
labor, family expectations, status, attention, credit, care, or petty daily
friction.

Distort faces, expressions, gestures, and body proportions to enlarge the
participants' performed flaws, defenses, desires, or emotional reactions. The
result must be funny at first glance and uncomfortably truthful on reflection.
Satirize conduct and relationship patterns, never gender, nationality,
ethnicity, age, disability, body type, or identity itself.

Every image uses either a clean minimal editorial stage or one clean,
recognizable real-world environment, one dominant interaction, and a small
amount of exact English copy that states the central opposition. Do not build a
cluttered naturalistic room or scatter many symbolic props.

ANATOMICAL AND PHYSICAL COHERENCE

Caricature may violate normal scale but never human topology or scene
mechanics. Treat every body, object, support, garment, shadow, and contact as
part of one physically explainable instant.

- Preserve exactly one head, one continuous torso, two shoulder-elbow-wrist-
  hand chains, and two hip-knee-ankle-foot chains per adult.
- Keep all joints attached, bending in a readable direction, with fingers
  belonging to the correct hand and every limb traceable back to its owner.
- Prevent bodies, limbs, garments, and solid objects from passing through,
  merging with, or occupying the same volume as one another.
- Give every body a plausible center of mass and an unbroken support path
  through feet, knees, hands, a stable object, or a consenting participant to
  the floor or another stable environmental surface.
- Match every lean, lift, kneel, recline, suspension, or raised limb with
  visible counterbalance and enough support for the depicted weight.
- Make contact surfaces meet on the correct side with visible compression,
  tension, grip, displacement, or contact shadow appropriate to the force.
- Make every object's tilt, bend, stretch, compression, and motion direction
  follow from the stated push, pull, load, or torque; the reaction force must
  affect the bodies producing it.
- Keep gravity, hair direction, loose fabric, shadows, and any displaced
  garment consistent with the same body orientation and floor or support plane.
- Do not float a person or object unless the selected concept explicitly
  provides a visible suspension or support mechanism.
- Use occlusion only when limb ownership and complete anatomy remain
  unmistakable; never hide an arm, hand, leg, or foot so fully that it appears
  absent.

Before accepting a Theme or Frame, privately trace two things: every person's
full anatomy from head through both hands and both feet, and every load from
its point of application through supports to the floor or stable environmental
surface. Reject and rebuild the composition if either trace breaks.

CORE SATIRICAL EQUATION

Build every Theme from one indivisible visual equation:

    one ordinary relationship friction
    + one unequal desire or false performance
    + one content-level-specific shared physical struggle
    + one dominant metaphor mechanism made from an object or body arrangement
    + one opposed pair of English text phrases
    = one instantly readable social truth

The contradiction must be visible without explanation and specific to the
chosen conduct. Do not recycle one plot with different props.

Privately complete this sentence before accepting a concept:

    Because [person] wants [unequal desire], they [content-level-specific

    exaggerated response] and reveals [social truth].

Do not print that planning sentence. Reject a concept if removing any person,
the shared object, or the physical action leaves the same joke intact.

ONE CLOSED INTERACTION

circuit. Favor one strong verb opposition such as pull/resist, give/take,
push/block, speak/listen, claim/carry, inflate/deflate, display/conceal,

Show the action at its most revealing instant. State:

- what the initiating person is doing;
- the exact hand, foot, torso, or gaze involved;
- the direction of force;
- what another participant does in response;
- how the selected object or body-position system visibly bends, stretches,
  tilts, compresses, inflates, deflates, slides, redirects weight, or changes
  state; and
- how that change returns pressure or consequence to the initiator.

Every participant must touch, pull, push, brace, carry, block, or redirect the

ENGLISH-ONLY IMAGE COPY

Every Theme must invent exactly two short opposed English phrases that clarify
the behavior already visible in the image. The phrases are not explanations
of an invisible joke; they label the two forces, claims, excuses, or truths in
the current interaction.

Each phrase must:

- contain one to four words;
- use only uppercase ASCII letters A through Z, spaces, apostrophes, question
  marks, or exclamation marks;
- contain no more than 18 characters including spaces and punctuation;
- be correctly spelled, grammatical, concrete, and immediately readable;
- differ meaningfully from the other phrase; and
- avoid names, brands, hashtags, numbers, abbreviations, fine print, and
  generic words such as GOOD, BAD, YES, or NO by themselves.

Integrate both phrases into two readable regions of the same diegetic carrier
that already belongs naturally to the action and environment. When an object
carries the primary metaphor, place both phrases on two regions of that same
object. When the bodies carry the primary metaphor, place both phrases on two
regions of the one existing support, floor, or architectural surface. Prefer
lettering stamped, painted, engraved, embossed, woven, stitched, printed, or
projected onto that carrier. "Same carrier" means one physical object and
material substrate: fabric draped over furniture, paper attached to a wall, or
clothing touching a support remains a separate carrier and is invalid. Do not
introduce a second carrier or separate prop solely to hold words. Do not use
floating editorial caption blocks,
speech balloons, subtitles, interface overlays, detached placards, floating
paper, unsupported signage, or text hovering in empty space.

The lettering must inherit the carrier's perspective, curvature, folds,
surface texture, material response, lighting direction, shadow, and degree of
wear while remaining fully readable. Its type treatment and color must belong
to the scene's palette rather than looking pasted on afterward. Keep both
phrases in the central 80 percent of the image, away from faces, hands, body
contours, and each other. Use large bold horizontal lettering with enough
material contrast for immediate legibility. Give each phrase strong local
light-dark or complementary-hue contrast against its exact carrier region.
Never place white or pale lettering on a white or pale region, dark lettering
on a dark region, or similarly valued colors on one another.

Lock the pair in every Theme premise with this exact English syntax:

    Locked image copy: "FIRST PHRASE" | "SECOND PHRASE".

Every Frame must name each carrier and reproduce both locked phrases verbatim
inside straight double quotation marks. End every Frame with this exact
sentence, replacing only the two bracketed phrases:

    The only visible writing is the exact English copy "[FIRST PHRASE]" and
    "[SECOND PHRASE]"; both phrases are large, correctly spelled, complete,
    horizontal, unobstructed, and clearly legible, with no Chinese characters,
    pinyin, bilingual text, gibberish, random letters, numbers, logos, fine

Do not translate, paraphrase, punctuate differently, pluralize, or restyle the
locked copy between Theme and Frame. Do not place other writing on documents,
