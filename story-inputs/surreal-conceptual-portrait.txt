BRIEF

ABSOLUTE OUTPUT PRIORITY

An English Frame is one ASCII-only paragraph of at least 600
whitespace-delimited words. Target 650-900 words of concrete imageable detail.
Preserve cast, action, metaphor, support, negative space, camera, material,
lighting, and safety without padding the paragraph through repetition. This
minimum overrides every later request for brevity.

Create original, museum-caliber surreal conceptual portraits of the exact
requested adult cast. Combine restrained fashion photography, practical
installation art, deadpan theatrical staging, and one impossible but visually
coherent metaphor. The image should feel quiet, precise, uncanny, and
emotionally legible rather than spectacular, whimsical, decorative, or
effects-driven.

Use the attached references only to understand broad principles: monumental
negative space, formal adult figures, suspended everyday objects, identity
concealment, sparse stage architecture, limited color, and practical-looking
surrealism. Do not copy any reference composition, object arrangement,
wardrobe, palette, pose, head treatment, or symbolic premise. Do not name,
imitate, or closely reproduce any artist, photographer, campaign, artwork, or
existing photograph.

VISUAL THESIS

Build each Theme from one concise equation:

    one adult psychological or social condition
    + one ordinary object family
    + one impossible spatial relationship
    = one silent conceptual portrait

The condition may involve memory, work, absence, choice, identity, domestic
order, private ambition, emotional distance, routine, travel, inheritance,
attention, or another adult experience. Express it entirely through visible
pose, object behavior, distance, scale, repetition, suspension, concealment,
and color. No caption or explanation should be needed.

Every image has one dominant metaphor, not a collection of unrelated surreal
gestures. Removing the person must destroy the meaning; replacing the hero
object with an arbitrary object must also destroy the meaning. An adult merely
standing beside strange decor is not enough.

CAST AND IDENTITY

Use exactly the requested number of adult women and adult men. Add no
bystanders, assistants, operators, crowds, reflected strangers, human-shaped
mannequins, portraits of people, screen figures, disembodied hands, extra
shadows, or body fragments that imply another person.

Every final Frame explicitly states that this exact visible cast is alone in
the scene and that no additional person or human body part is present. This
must remain unambiguous when empty garments, frames, screens, shadows, or
person-sized objects appear.

Every person is unmistakably twenty-five or older. Unless the Story
Description explicitly specifies another nationality, every person is
Chinese. Give each Theme original adults with exact ages, mature facial or
body structure, distinct builds, skin tones, hairstyles, and personal
wardrobe. Preserve each identity across the Theme.

When a face is visible, give it a calm, self-possessed expression appropriate
to the metaphor: deadpan attention, measured curiosity, private resolve,
contained grief, poised uncertainty, or restrained defiance. Avoid fashion
smiles, theatrical screaming, vacant mannequin expressions, exaggerated fear,
or melodramatic acting.

Give every requested person an indispensable compositional role. With multiple
people, create a clear relationship through distance, mirrored or opposed
poses, exchanged objects, shared suspension geometry, aligned gaze, or one
cooperative action. Do not reduce anyone to background decoration.

CONCEPT ENGINES

Invent beyond these open-ended directions:

- a canopy or constellation of ordinary possessions suspended above a person;
- an empty garment archive arranged like weather, architecture, or memory;
- one wearable object-head that turns identity into a physical interface;
- frames, cases, containers, tools, or domestic objects forming a controlled
  cloud around one seated or standing adult;
- a bed, chair, desk, doorway, luggage system, or other familiar object placed
  in an emotionally contradictory environment;
- cables or threads translating thought, obligation, memory, or choice into a
  visible spatial map;
- repeated objects stopping at a precise boundary around the body;
- one object enlarged or multiplied only when its ordinary function remains
  essential to the metaphor;
- two adults connected by parallel object systems that never physically merge;
  or
- an original practical illusion based on balance, absence, substitution,
  containment, or impossible order.

These are idea engines, not a menu. Do not repeat the same suspended wardrobe,
covered head, archive cloud, desert bed, black void, or seated profile across
nearby Themes.

SUSPENDED ASSEMBLIES

Suspended garments, luggage, frames, tools, household objects, papers,
furniture parts, or sculptural forms must create one readable silhouette and
one intentional relationship to the cast. Use varied height, scale, spacing,
overlap, and density rather than an even decorative grid.

Show thin but credible support lines rising to an off-frame ceiling grid,
overhead truss, concealed rail, or other stable architectural anchor. Every
heavy object has its own plausible load path and hangs vertically under
gravity. Cables remain taut where loaded, cast consistent shadows, and never
terminate in empty space.

Keep suspended objects above or beside safe body paths. Nothing hangs by a
person's hair, skin, neck, genitals, or clothing. Nothing heavy touches a head
or balances over an unprotected body. Use lightweight theatrical replicas when
the visible scale would otherwise be unsafe.

Empty garments must remain visibly empty on hangers or internal dress forms
that cannot resemble people. Framed images contain landscapes, architecture,
objects, or abstract tonal fields, never recognizable human faces or bodies.
Every final Frame containing an empty garment explicitly names its visible
hanger or flat non-human support. Wardrobe archives contain only adult-sized
garments and no baby clothes, child-sized clothing, school uniforms, or other
youth-coded pieces.

When any suspended object overlaps the vertical area above a person, describe
it as an ultralight hollow theatrical replica and keep an open safety gap above
the complete body silhouette. Real furniture, glass, ceramic, clocks, tools,
luggage, and other rigid or heavy objects remain beside the cast, never
directly overhead.

OBJECT-HEAD AND FACE CONCEALMENT

Face concealment is optional, not mandatory. Use it only when identity,
attention, memory, role, anonymity, or communication is the Theme's central
idea.

An object-head is a wearable sculptural headpiece around one existing person's
real head, never a decapitation, floating replacement, second head, or detached
neck. Keep the neck-to-torso connection anatomically continuous. Give the
headpiece a credible opening, internal clearance, balanced center of mass, and
visible support independent of the neck.

Suitable head treatments include an unbranded blank display, shallow box,
translucent shell, soft wrapped volume, architectural visor, framed object
cluster, or another original form. Keep the nose and mouth physically
uncompressed and breathing unrestricted. Never use tight plastic, adhesive
wrap, strangling cord, sealed fabric, a balaclava, an enclosed hood, a sack,
injury, distress, or restraint.

Use lightweight shatterproof acrylic, foam, fabric, card, thin timber, or
rounded polymer for any structure surrounding the head. Never place real
glass, ceramic, brittle material, sharp metal edge, or heavy component around
or above the face, skull, or neck.

Every final Frame using face concealment must visibly describe both a generous
lower-face breathing gap or ventilated air channel in front of the nose and
mouth and an independent load path that bypasses the neck. Any attached cable,
thread, or object is carried by an overhead support or a broad torso-mounted
frame, never by the headpiece, head, hair, throat, or clothing.

Blank screens show only one unlit or abstract tonal surface with no interface,
face, logo, text, symbol, static pattern, or readable image. Threads and cables
may emerge from a supported headpiece but never pierce skin or load the head.
Mirrors and reflective headpieces may reflect only empty architecture, light,
or abstract color. They never reflect, repeat, fragment, or multiply a person's
face, eyes, head, body, or limbs.

WARDROBE AND HUMAN PRESENCE

Favor formal tailoring, austere dresses, sculptural coats, crisp shirts,
pleated skirts, restrained knitwear, long gloves, polished shoes, or similarly
precise adult clothing. Let silhouette, seam, fold, and material contrast carry
the fashion language. Clothing may be white-on-white, black-on-black,
charcoal, stone, cream, or one saturated accent against a neutral field.

Give every person a complete coherent look appropriate to the premise. Vary
tailoring era, silhouette, hem, shoulder line, fabric weight, footwear, and
accessories across Themes. Avoid generic black suits in every image, costume
fantasy, branded clothing, youth-coded uniforms, or styling copied from the
references.

Preserve photographic skin, hair, fabric pressure, and natural asymmetry.
Surrealism comes from the object relationship and spatial design, not from
melted anatomy, synthetic skin, impossible clothing, or distorted bodies.

SET, SPACE, AND NEGATIVE SPACE

Use one controlled environment per Theme:

- a pale seamless studio or architectural room with subtle floor-wall
  separation;
- a deep black stage or void with a visible ground plane;
- a sparse domestic interior reduced to essential furniture;
- a salt flat, dry lake, fog field, shoreline, or other open landscape;
- a gallery-like chamber, archive, waiting room, office, bedroom, workshop, or
  transit space emptied to symbolic essentials; or
- another original location whose function strengthens the metaphor.

Reserve approximately forty to seventy percent of the frame as calm negative
space. Place density deliberately in one canopy, column, cloud, horizon,
vertical stack, or compact human-object cluster. Keep the floor plane, horizon,
wall junction, or stage edge readable enough to anchor scale.

Do not fill empty space with random props, fog, particles, birds, ornamental
fabric, typography, or secondary symbols. Avoid generic luxury interiors,
ruins, cyberpunk alleys, fantasy landscapes, and busy cinematic production
design.

COLOR AND MATERIAL SYSTEM

Choose one disciplined palette per Theme:

- warm white, chalk, bone, and one amber or rust accent;
- charcoal, ink black, graphite, and a small silver highlight;
- cool grey, off-white, dark timber, and one muted red;
- sand, ivory, pale blue, and one brown leather accent;
- monochrome with one restrained material color; or
- another original system using no more than two dominant hues plus one accent.

Use real material response: pressed wool, wrinkled linen, lacquer, brushed
metal, frosted glass, matte plastic, old paper, timber, leather, cotton cord,
ceramic, or painted plaster. Keep highlights restrained, blacks separated, and
whites textured. Avoid rainbow palettes, neon spectacle, excessive gloss,
fantasy glow, liquid chrome, or indiscriminate haze.

CAMERA, LIGHT, AND COMPOSITION

Favor frontal or near-frontal museum-documentation clarity, strict profile,
measured three-quarter view, centered symmetry, deliberate off-center balance,
or a low-density environmental portrait. Use normal or portrait perspective,
typically 40-105 mm equivalent, with level verticals and little distortion.

Vary full-body, seated full figure, three-quarter portrait, paired tableau,
environmental wide, and controlled close portrait across Themes. Keep the
complete conceptual relationship legible. Do not crop away a required support,
object source, body connection, or the negative space that makes the metaphor
readable.

Use one coherent light system:

- broad cool studio daylight with soft floor shadows;
- one overhead pool of light fading into a black stage;
- hard lateral sun producing long graphic shadows;
- pale overcast landscape light with subtle horizon separation;
- restrained frontal flash against matte surfaces; or
- another motivated design with clear direction and falloff.

Lighting must reveal cable paths, object depth, garment texture, body outline,
and the ground plane. Avoid generic cinematic teal-orange, blown white voids,
featureless crushed black, random rim lights, theatrical colored smoke, and
beauty lighting that erases the installation.

ANATOMY, SUPPORT, AND OBJECT SEPARATION

Keep every body anatomically ordinary and traceable. Each visible limb connects
continuously to one person's torso, follows natural joints, and has one
compatible task or support. Do not create extra, detached, repeated, fused, or
source-less anatomy through object overlap, darkness, mirrors, frames, screens,
shadows, or hanging garments.

Give every standing foot a stable ground contact and every seated body a broad
load-bearing chair, bench, bed, or floor. People do not carry the suspended
installation, serve as structural columns, balance on narrow props, or perform
acrobatic poses. Cords may organize space but never bind wrists, ankles, necks,
airways, or sexual anatomy.

Keep object edges and body silhouettes separated by clean negative space where
possible. When overlap is essential, preserve the source and continuation of
every limb, cable, garment, and object.

CONTENT LEVEL

Keep the same restrained conceptual-portrait language at every requested
content level.

- Aesthetic: use complete opaque adult fashion and nonsexual interaction.
  Every garment remains appropriately fastened, with shoulders, chest, pelvis,
  buttocks, and genitals fully covered by opaque fabric.
- Erotic: allow adult nudity, sheer or open tailoring, intimate non-explicit
  touch, and sensual body-object tension. Show no penetration, explicit
  oral-genital contact, or direct genital stimulation.
- Hardcore: every Frame must show one clearly visible consensual adult sexual
  act already in progress while the conceptual installation remains essential
  to its meaning. Directly name the active anatomy and present contact. Keep
  every requested adult awake, willing, responsive, and visibly able to stop.

At Hardcore level, the installation may frame, echo, count, conceal peripheral
space, or create a metaphor around the act, but it may not penetrate, restrain,
suspend, strike, electrically stimulate, or mechanically operate a body. Use
only body-safe toys designed for the depicted contact. Keep heavy suspended
objects, cords, frames, glass, sharp edges, and powered mechanisms outside all
body and sexual-contact paths.

THEME CONTRACT

Every Theme defines:

- one original adult condition or relationship;
- one ordinary object family with a necessary symbolic function;
- one impossible but physically staged spatial relationship;
- the exact requested cast with stable identities and wardrobe;
- one controlled location, negative-space structure, palette, and material
  system;
- one safe practical installation method;
- one camera and lighting grammar; and
- one concise visual thesis that remains useful across all requested Frames.

Themes must differ through premise, object family, spatial mechanism, cast
relationship, wardrobe silhouette, density location, palette, setting, camera,
and emotional temperature. Do not create variants that merely exchange one
hanging object or accent color.

FRAME CONTRACT

Frames within one Theme are parallel finished portraits, not chronological
steps. Each Frame independently restates the complete cast, present pose,
object system, practical supports, setting, palette, camera, light, and current
content-level action. Never refer to another Frame or imply that an object,
garment, pose, or action changed between images.

Vary at least four of these while preserving the Theme: camera distance,
viewpoint, body orientation, distribution of negative space, object density,
foreground layer, gaze circuit, support visibility, light geometry, and
material emphasis.

BATCH VARIETY

Across a full ten-Theme batch, include at least:

- three pale or high-key environments and three dark or low-key environments;
- three suspended systems, two grounded object arrangements, and two wearable
  head or face treatments;
- four distinct wardrobe silhouette families;
- four different density structures;
- four emotional temperatures; and
- six different object families.

For smaller batches, maximize the same diversity proportionally. No single
object family, head treatment, palette, pose, or location may dominate.

NO-TEXT IMAGE CONTRACT

The rendered image contains no title, caption, label, logo, brand mark,
watermark, interface, subtitle, poster, readable screen, printed sentence,
number, QR code, or decorative pseudo-text. Communicate the concept only
through people, objects, pose, scale, spacing, material, color, shadow, and
light. Every final Frame explicitly restates that the rendered scene contains
no readable text, logo, interface, watermark, or human image.

Papers, letters, envelopes, files, labels, tags, books, screens, and signs must
have blank surfaces or abstract non-linguistic tonal fields. Never describe
them as handwritten, printed, addressed, typed, numbered, labeled, or bearing
faded or illegible writing.

OUTPUT

Write every final Frame as one fluent natural-language paragraph in the
requested language, directly usable as an image-generation prompt. Begin with
the visible cast, pose, object relationship, and place. Then integrate
wardrobe, installation geometry, support paths, negative space, camera, light,
palette, and material response in the order that reads most naturally.

For an English Frame, use approximately twelve to sixteen sentences in this
detailed sequence:

1. cast, age, identity, primary pose, location, wardrobe, expression, and gaze;
2. hero object family and impossible spatial relationship;
3. credible installation support, object separation, body support, limb
   continuity, and current interaction;
4. controlled environment and its forty-to-seventy-percent negative space;
5. camera, light, palette, and essential material response; and
6. exact-cast isolation plus the no-text image contract.

Expand these six areas across the full sentence budget instead of limiting
each area to one sentence. Use direct complete sentences of varied length.
The final sentence must explicitly contain the phrases "Only the specified
cast is present", "negative space", and "no readable text, logo, interface,
watermark, or human image".

Keep an English Frame at least 600 words long, or use equivalent detail in
another requested language. Draft toward a 650-900 word working range. Count
whitespace-delimited words before returning each English Frame; if the count is
below 600, add new imageable spatial, material, wardrobe, lighting, support, or
camera detail until the minimum is met. Use ASCII characters only in an English
Frame, including straight quotation marks and ordinary hyphens instead of
smart punctuation. Before publishing, scan every character in an English Frame
and replace any Chinese character, accented letter, smart punctuation, or other
non-ASCII symbol with plain English ASCII. Output no heading, label, bullet
list, alternate version, implementation note, reference-image discussion,
artist name, or compliance explanation.

End after the concrete visible image description. Do not append a material
inventory, symbolic interpretation, philosophical summary, explanation of the
metaphor, or a second passage that restates details already visible in the
prompt.

SILENT REJECTION CHECK

Before returning, reject and rewrite any Theme or Frame that:

1. copies a reference composition or recognizable artwork;
2. contains more or fewer people than requested;
3. uses several unrelated surreal devices instead of one metaphor;
4. would retain the same meaning after replacing the hero object arbitrarily;
5. turns negative space into decorative clutter;
6. creates an unsupported heavy object or cable with no load path;
7. makes an empty garment, frame, shadow, or screen imply another person;
8. depicts decapitation, suffocation, strangulation, binding, injury, or unsafe
   suspension;
9. creates detached, repeated, fused, or untraceable anatomy;
10. hides the conceptual relationship through darkness, crop, fog, or effects;
11. violates the requested content level;
12. uses a dangerous installation in an intimate contact path;
13. renders readable text or an interface; or
14. uses face concealment without a visible breathing path and neck-independent
    support; or
15. explains the metaphor instead of making it visible;
16. calls a blank paper surface handwritten, printed, addressed, or labeled;
    or
17. contains fewer than 600 English words or appends a redundant interpretation
    passage; or
18. contains any non-ASCII character in an English Frame.
