BRIEF

Create sculptural topographic Themes where contour-like bands are formed by the
environment around the exact requested adult cast. The bands must describe real
height, pressure, depth, or flow while preserving ordinary human anatomy and a
single coherent landscape or interior.

WOMAN CAST GATE

Every Theme and every Frame must include at least one unmistakably adult woman
physically testing or altering one topographic boundary. Give her a specific
adult age, stable appearance, clearly visible face and body, grounded position,
gaze, and necessary action. A contour silhouette, map marker, portrait,
reflection, or off-camera observer does not count. Additional CLI-requested
adults may appear, but she must remain the active scale and action anchor. This
brief is incompatible with a zero-woman cast.

CONTOUR SYSTEM

Choose a different measurable phenomenon for each Theme: stepped terrain,
compressed fabric, layered sediment, water depth, deposited powder, carved
material, crop height, or light intensity across relief. Use five to nine nested
or parallel bands with one readable direction of increase. Never paint arbitrary
contour lines on skin or float graphic lines in space.

THEME CONTRACT

The premise names country, exact site, adult cast, measured phenomenon, band
material, direction of increase, current action, and the local anomaly they must
interpret or alter. The style sentence defines elevated or grazing-angle
photography, palette, texture, scale, and light.

FRAME CONTRACT

Each Frame shows the full band system, the anomaly, and requested cast in one
image. Describe band count range, interval behavior, physical support, highest
or strongest zone, lowest or weakest zone, and how one cast action changes or
tests exactly one boundary.

Bands follow the underlying surface, stack or spread with plausible thickness,
and react correctly to weight, water, wind, or tools. Feet and hands make local
contact without causing impossible global deformation. No map labels, numeric
elevations, projected graphics, body duplication, or detached aerial viewpoint.

Reject decorative stripes, ordinary terraced scenery without a human decision,
or a contour system whose direction and physical meaning cannot be inferred from
the image.
