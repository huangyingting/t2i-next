BRIEF

Create varied, hyper-realistic cinematic adult stills built around one
impossible but immediately readable event: one or more people physically emerge
from a familiar object, image surface, container, reflection, or architectural
feature while the other requested adults react in visible astonishment.

Every image freezes a literal half-in, half-out crossing. The source plane
intersects the emerging person once at the lower abdomen, waist, hips, or upper
thighs: the head, shoulders, arms, and much of the torso are already outside,
while the pelvis and legs remain as an ordinary three-dimensional body inside
the bounded source interior. The source, outside half, inside half, single
crossing plane, and witness reaction must all be understandable without
captions or backstory.

This is an anthology of original threshold events, not a continuous supernatural
universe. Invent new characters and situations. Do not name, reproduce, or
closely resemble a copyrighted character, recognizable movie scene, celebrity,
public figure, existing advertisement, or famous artwork.

CORE VISUAL EQUATION

Every Theme and Frame must make this equation visible:

    ONE RECOGNIZABLE SOURCE
    + ONE ANATOMICALLY CONTINUOUS CROSSING
    + ONE BOUNDED VIEW OF THE REAR BODY INSIDE THE SOURCE
    + ONE CHAIN OF HUMAN REACTIONS
    = ONE DECISIVE IMPOSSIBLE ARRIVAL

The emergence is the main event, not background decoration. At least one
requested adult must visibly occupy both sides of one threshold: enough of the
body is outside to read as an arrival, while an unbroken anatomical path still
connects the figure to the source opening. The image must show where the person
came from and where they are entering.

The source must retain its ordinary identity. A television still reads as a
television, a cinema screen as a projected screen, a portrait as a framed flat
image, a mirror as reflective glass, and a vending machine as a vending machine.
Do not replace the source with a generic glowing rectangle, dark hole, smoky
portal, or abstract ring.

The source interior is secondary and tightly bounded by the source object. It
may show one simple floor, room, landscape, weather, season, or light condition
behind the pelvis and legs, but it must never become a second full composition.
The person keeps the same natural skin, hair, clothing, volume, and photographic
realism on both sides.

CAST AND ROLE CONTRACT

Use the exact requested cast and no additional humans. Every person must be an
unmistakably mature adult aged 25 or older with an original face. Preserve each
person's age, face, hair, build, garments, and other visual anchors throughout a
Theme.

The emerging people and witnesses together are the complete requested cast:

    EMERGING WOMEN + WITNESS WOMEN = REQUESTED WOMEN
    EMERGING MEN + WITNESS MEN = REQUESTED MEN
    ALL EMERGING PEOPLE + ALL WITNESSES = TOTAL REQUESTED PEOPLE

Never treat the requested cast as witnesses and then add an emerging person.
For a request of two women and one man, valid allocations include one emerging
woman plus one woman and one man witnessing, or one emerging man plus two women
witnessing. Three witnesses plus a fourth emerging person is invalid.

Introduce the complete cast only once, with the emergence role bound immediately
to one of those same named people. For a two-woman, one-man request, use the
structural logic “Woman A emerges while Woman B and Man C witness” or “Man A
emerges while Woman B and Woman C witness.” Never use the structural logic
“two women and one man are doing something when another woman or man emerges.”
Words such as visitor, actor, dancer, portrait sitter, traveler, swimmer,
miniature inhabitant, or person from the source describe one already-counted,
already-named requested adult, never a new character.

Before inventing each Theme, privately build a cast ledger with one row per
requested adult: stable identity, gender, age, and emerging or witness role.
Count requested women and requested men separately. A Theme is invalid if either
gender count differs from the request, even when the total number of people is
correct. Do not change a requested woman into a man or a requested man into a
woman to suit a concept. Recheck the same private ledger before returning every
Frame; do not print or describe the ledger itself.

Every visible or implied human counts toward the cast, including background
customers, staff, passersby, audiences, crowds, silhouettes, people pictured
inside the source, portraits, screen figures, reflections, statues, mannequins,
and human-shaped shadows. Apart from the requested emerging people and
witnesses, show none of these. Populate depth with empty seats, architecture,
plants, vehicles, objects, light, weather, or abstract nonhuman imagery instead.

Assign every requested person one indispensable role in the same arrival:

- one or more people are emerging through the source;
- all remaining people are direct witnesses whose posture, gaze, hands, and
  displaced nearby objects make the surprise visible; and
- when several people emerge, their bodies use separate clearances or a simple,
  readable order without overlap or anatomical confusion.

If the requested cast contains only one person, center that person alone in the
crossing and express surprise through their own backward glance, braced hand,
alert face, and the physical reaction of the setting. Do not invent a witness.
With two or more people, at least one witness must react directly to the
emerging figure rather than looking toward the camera.

Reactions must be specific and varied rather than a row of identical open
mouths. Possibilities include a hand frozen above a popcorn tub, shoulders
recoiling while feet remain planted, fingers gripping a cinema seat, a shopping
bag dropped beside the shoes, a drink tilted short of spilling, glasses lowered
for a second look, one witness instinctively steadying another, a skeptical
lean toward the opening, delighted laughter, or stunned stillness. These are
inspiration, not a menu.

Favor astonishment, disbelief, curiosity, delight, comic alarm, reverence, or
uncanny recognition. Do not depict a stampede, riot, weapon response, attack,
genuine terror, injury, coercion, helplessness, or a person unable to leave.
Every adult remains alert, physically safe, and in control.

CONTENT-LEVEL DIRECTION

At aesthetic level, keep the event non-sexual. Use expressive wardrobe,
silhouette, body language, bounded source-interior detail, and witness reactions to
create the image.

At erotic level, allow adult nudity, sensual wardrobe, and consensual
non-explicit contact when it belongs to the arrival. The threshold must not
pinch, restrain, conceal, or replace the required bodily interaction.

At hardcore level, every Frame must already show a clearly visible consensual
adult explicit interaction involving the requested cast. Integrate it into the
single emergence tableau rather than placing explicit contact beside an
unrelated portal spectacle. Keep all decisive anatomy unobstructed, naturally
aligned, supported, and outside rigid edges; the threshold must never function
as a sexual device, restraint, or contact surface. Every participant remains
alert, mutually responsive, and visibly able to stop.

Do not increase or reduce the requested content level. Across all levels, the
crossing remains the conceptual center and every requested adult has an active,
readable role.

SOURCE FAMILIES

Choose freely from familiar source families, combine compatible qualities, or
invent unrelated equivalents. These examples are open-ended inspiration, not
presets or identifier assignments.

IMAGE AND PERFORMANCE SURFACES

- an unbranded cathode-ray television in a late-night apartment;
- a cinema screen above rows of startled moviegoers;
- a drive-in projection floating above parked cars;
- an outdoor LED billboard over a rain-bright intersection;
- a tablet-sized display used only when scale contrast is intentional and
  physically legible;
- a photo-booth image whose paper surface opens into full depth;
- a stage backdrop, shadow-play screen, or projected home movie;
- a framed photograph, fashion poster, transit advertisement, or contact sheet;
- a comic panel, picture-book spread, map illustration, or architectural
  blueprint whose graphic world remains visible around the opening.

REFLECTIVE AND TRANSPARENT SOURCES

- a dressing-room mirror whose reflection has different weather or time;
- a puddle reflecting a place that does not exist above it;
- a polished elevator wall, shop window, chrome sculpture, or dark train glass;
- an aquarium wall with a dry room on the far side;
- a glass display case, snow globe, museum vitrine, or greenhouse pane;
- a still pool, decorative fountain, or rain-covered skylight;
- a camera lens, oversized pair of spectacles, or magnifying glass built as a
  safe practical set at human scale.

EVERYDAY OBJECTS AND CONTAINERS

- a wardrobe, refrigerator, washing-machine porthole, suitcase, steamer trunk,
  cupboard, oven-shaped stage prop, or freestanding cabinet;
- a vending-machine collection bay, arcade cabinet, jukebox, record sleeve,
  speaker cone, or large radio dial;
- a parcel, gift box, handbag, cereal carton, takeout container, or cosmetics
  compact enlarged as a deliberate theatrical prop;
- a teacup, bottle, desk drawer, filing cabinet, umbrella canopy, lampshade, or
  clock face used with an intelligible scale proposition;
- a book, envelope, vinyl record, folded newspaper, postcard, or roll of film
  whose thin surface gains a precise three-dimensional threshold.

ARCHITECTURE, NATURE, AND IMPOSSIBLE EDGES

- a painted mural, mosaic wall, relief carving, theater curtain, or wallpaper
  pattern;
- an elevator doorway opening onto an incompatible world;
- a subway route diagram, station poster, departure board, or platform clock
  that becomes a spatial opening without relying on readable text;
- the shadow of a doorway with no corresponding door;
- a hedge, tree hollow, flower center, waterfall, cloud bank, sandcastle,
  snowdrift, or vertical sheet of rain;
- a giant cake slice, banquet centerpiece, ice block, folded fabric, pile of
  laundry, or wall of stacked luggage;
- a museum diorama, dollhouse facade, model train landscape, miniature city, or
  tabletop architectural model whose inhabitants cross into human scale;
- a chalk drawing, peeled layer of paint, torn wallpaper seam, floor pattern,
  or pool of colored light that unexpectedly has depth.

Do not let a full batch cluster around screens, frames, doors, mirrors, or dark
horror imagery. For a full ten-Theme batch, use at least seven distinct source
families, at least seven different setting functions, and a strong mixture of
comic, glamorous, tender, uncanny, celebratory, mundane, romantic, and
spectacular emotional temperatures.

CONCEPT ENGINES

Scale and emergence alone are not enough. Give every Theme one source-specific
proposition. Possible engines include:

- SOURCE GENRE COLLISION: a person carrying the visual rules of a flat melodrama,
  wildlife program, silent portrait, weather broadcast, or miniature model into
  an incompatible real location;
- AUDIENCE REVERSAL: spectators become the spectacle's observed subjects as the
  performer leans out to inspect them;
- PRIVATE IMAGE BECOMES PRESENT: a familiar photograph or reflection produces
  an original adult whose current pose directly answers the witnesses;
- WRONG-SIZED ARRIVAL: a miniature inhabitant becomes full-sized at the crossing,
  or a full-sized adult carefully tests a tabletop world;
- SOURCE DEPTH: the familiar flat source becomes a real opening with enough
  visible depth for the rear half of the same normally rendered body;
- WEATHER LEAK: wind, dry leaves, snow, warm sunlight, bubbles, confetti, or
  stage rain belonging to the source crosses just far enough to alter witness
  hair, clothing, and nearby loose objects;
- SOCIAL INTERRUPTION: the arrival interrupts a wedding rehearsal, quiet
  commute, product demonstration, dinner, gallery tour, haircut, exercise
  class, board-game night, or another concrete adult activity;
- ROLE EXCHANGE: a portrait subject confronts the curator, a projected dancer
  invites the seated witnesses, a fashion portrait subject emerges toward two
  friends, or a map inhabitant asks travelers for direction through gesture;
- APERTURE MECHANICS: only a narrow waist-sized opening parts, bends, or ripples
  around the body while the rest of the source surface stays flat and intact;
- IMPOSSIBLE CONTINUATION: architecture, furniture, road markings, waves, or
  light lines inside the source align exactly with physical structures outside.

Use one primary engine and at most one subordinate engine per Theme. The visual
idea must survive removal of any explanatory text. Reject a concept if swapping
the source for another rectangle leaves the image essentially unchanged.

THRESHOLD GEOMETRY

The source boundary is the most important piece of spatial evidence. Show a
precise aperture, parted surface, elastic image plane, lifted page, liquid
meniscus, curtain slit, object hatch, or aligned architectural opening. The
boundary crosses the body at one narrow waist, hip, or upper-thigh band and
nowhere else. It must not trace the complete silhouette, wrap around the head
and limbs, overlay the full body, or create ripples around a person who is
already entirely outside.

HALF-IN, HALF-OUT SILHOUETTE

Treat the source as a real opening into depth, not as an image printed over a
complete foreground person. The emerging adult has one continuous volumetric
body perpendicular to the source plane. The outside half projects toward the
room and camera; the inside half recedes behind the screen, picture, mirror,
container wall, or aperture into the bounded source interior.

A strong crawling arrangement places a low television, cinema screen, framed
image, cabinet opening, or other threshold behind the emerging adult. The head,
hair, shoulders, both arms, chest, and perhaps the abdomen are already in the
room; both palms or one palm and one forearm press on a broad stable floor or
ledge outside; the spine angles forward; the waist or hips pass through the
screen opening; the pelvis, both knees, calves, and feet remain behind the
screen inside the bounded source interior. The bezel or frame is visible behind
the torso, and the outside body casts a real shadow toward the witnesses.

A standing arrangement places one foot and most of the torso outside while the
pelvis crosses the threshold and the rear leg remains inside. A leaning
arrangement places the arms, shoulders, chest, and abdomen outside while the
hips and both legs remain inside. In every arrangement, roughly forty to sixty
percent of the body is on each side so the image reads instantly as a crossing,
not as a portrait, projection, reflection, material transformation, or person
standing in front of the source.

For a flat source such as a television, cinema screen, painting, photograph,
poster, mirror, or billboard, the picture surface parts only at the waist or
hips like a deep aperture. The screen image continues behind the rear half of
the body. Do not paste the complete person over the picture, do not make the
whole body translucent, and do not surround the whole figure with liquid-glass
distortion.

REFERENCE CRAWL CHOREOGRAPHY

Use this original, generic television exit as the clearest spatial prototype:
a low, unbranded cathode-ray television stands behind the emerging adult; the
screen is an opening into a visible room or landscape. The adult crawls
headfirst perpendicular to the screen. Their head, hair, shoulders, chest, both
arms, and hands are already in the real room. Both palms press on the real floor
well in front of the television, elbows and shoulders carrying the front-body
weight. The abdomen passes through the center of the screen, the bezel stays
fully visible around the waist, and the pelvis, bent knees, calves, and feet
remain normally three-dimensional inside the bounded television interior. The front half
casts a shadow on the floor; the rear half receives only the light contained
inside the television screen. Witnesses stand to either side and look directly
at the waist crossing.

This is a geometry reference, not a required horror mood, costume, hairstyle,
character, television, or copied scene. Apply the same headfirst crawl, leaning
exit, or one-leg-out spatial logic to original sources and emotional tones.

SINGLE-SCENE RENDERING CONTRACT

The final image contains one camera, one continuous outer location, and one
source object inside that location. The outer location fills most of the frame.
The source interior appears only inside the exact bounded area of the television
screen, cinema screen, picture frame, mirror frame, window, hatch, or opening.
Show all four edges of a flat source whenever possible so its bounded size is
unmistakable.

The source interior never expands behind the witnesses, replaces the outer
background, becomes a doorway-sized second set, or appears as a side-by-side
panel. Do not create a split screen, diptych, collage, cutaway, before-and-after,
parallel scene, reflection duplicate, or second camera view. The witnesses,
outside half of the emerging adult, and source object all share the same outer
floor, perspective, lens, and light space.

Keep the source interior visually simple: one floor plane or ground plane, one
background color or shallow environment, and no people other than the rear half
of the emerging adult. It exists only to prove that the pelvis and legs remain
behind the threshold. Avoid a sky-only background, elaborate second room,
distant vista, crowded landscape, or source scene that competes with the outer
location.

For mirrors, suppress the ordinary reflection entirely. The silvered mirror
surface stays opaque and recognizable around one waist-sized opening; only that
opening shows the rear half of the body. Do not show a second full-body
reflection, another copy of the witnesses, or a doorway replacing the mirror.

For the principal emerging figure, keep the head, neck, shoulders, torso,
pelvis, and visible limbs traceable in one continuous body. Give every hand and
foot one clear function: gripping a safe frame, pressing a flexible surface,
reaching toward a witness, balancing on a broad source ledge, or landing on the
outside floor. Keep joints in natural ranges and separate overlapping limbs.

The outside portion of the figure must project far enough to cast believable
shadows, displace air or loose material, and occupy the witnesses' physical
space. The inside portion must remain visibly anchored to the bounded source
interior. Show the waist or hips at the crossing, with a substantial torso
segment outside and the pelvis plus at least one complete connected leg inside.
A dark cavity, implied hidden body, cropped opening, neck-only hole, floating
upper body, or prose claim that an unseen body continues inside does not
qualify.
The source-side body must remain recognizably human anatomy: show at minimum the
pelvis and one complete connected leg. The inside half may receive the source
interior's contained light and weather, but skin, clothing, joints, and volume
remain as ordinary photographed reality. Do not flatten, paint, pixelate, dissolve,
silhouette, merge, embed, fuse, or translate the inside half into the source
material. Do not replace the pelvis, legs, torso, or limbs with map routes, data
lines, diagram arrows, mist, shadows, text, abstract patterns, brushstrokes, or
an empty source background. Phrases such as “connected to,” “interwoven with,”
“merged into,” or “formed from” source graphics do not prove a continuous body.
Opaque food, boxes, walls, and closed containers are invalid sources unless a
wide open face, transparent section, or clearly pictured source interior makes
the inside torso or lower body directly visible. Do not show a detached upper
body, severed silhouette, duplicated figure, floating torso, missing pelvis,
extra limbs, impossible bend, or person pasted over the source.

Use only one body state per person. A reflection, photograph, screen image,
shadow, or poster must not create a second copy of the emerging person unless
it is a physically correct reflection of the same pose. Do not show both an
inside version and an outside clone.

Do not rely on a fully opaque portal glow, smoke cloud, splash, fabric, foreground
object, witness body, or shallow depth of field to conceal the crossing. Keep
the source edge, emerging anatomy, support points, witness faces, and gaze
relationships simultaneously legible.

SAFE PRACTICAL SPECTACLE

Render the impossible event as a premium practical effect photographed on a
real set. Build rigid sources with generous rounded clearances, broad
load-bearing ledges, concealed studio reinforcement, and stable floor contact.
Flexible sources may deform around clothing or broad body contours without
compressing the neck, chest, abdomen, pelvis, or limbs.

Use intact safety glass, acrylic, soft scenic foam, fabric, paper, water, light,
or breakaway-looking materials that remain visibly harmless. Do not use broken
glass, exposed electricity, sharp frame edges, fire near bodies, unstable
appliances, running machinery, sealed containers, crushing doors, dangerous
heights, deep water, restricted breathing, or uncontrolled falling.

An appliance or vehicle source is a constructed theatrical shell with visible
clearance and no active hazardous mechanism. Do not place a body inside a real
operating oven, washing machine, vending mechanism, engine, traffic lane, or
other unsafe enclosure.

Material effects must obey the frozen instant. Fabric stretches from visible
contact, paper bends along supported creases, water follows gravity, projected
light falls on surfaces, hair and loose wardrobe move in the same airflow, and
every cast shadow agrees with the light direction. Do not use arbitrary magic
sparks, energy rings, lightning, or weightless debris as a substitute for
source-specific evidence.

THEME CONTRACT

Each Theme establishes one recurring threshold event with one precise country
and location, one era or year, the complete requested cast, stable character
anchors, one recognizable source, one bounded source interior, one continuous
outer location, one concept engine, one emotional temperature, and one
cinematic identity.
Every Theme occurs in a distinct setting in China unless the request explicitly
names another country.

The title must be concise, original, and evocative without naming a creator,
existing character, movie, content level, camera process, or source category.

The premise uses no more than two sentences. Sentence one establishes the
setting, complete adult cast, ordinary activity, source, and emerging-versus-
witness roles, explicitly preserving the requested number of women and men.
Sentence two states the source-specific impossible contrast, exact crossing
geometry, visible inside-body continuation, and shared reaction pattern that
make the tableau visually unique.

In sentence one, name the emerging requested adult before or in the same clause
as the witnesses. Do not introduce all requested adults as an observing group
and then introduce an unnamed or newly named source person. Sentence one must
contain no more distinct human identities than the total requested cast.

The style sentence defines an original photographic language through palette,
production design, source light, lens distance, depth, texture, and practical
effect treatment. Do not imitate or name a filmmaker, photographer, artist,
movie, television program, game, comic, or advertising campaign.

Do not put a Frame list, chronological plot, alternate source, or sequence of
emergence stages in the Theme premise. The Theme defines one deep visual system
that can yield several parallel photographs.

FRAME CONTRACT

Every Frame is one standalone full-frame photograph and one alternative
interpretation of the same Theme, not a chronological beat. No pose, object,
spill, garment state, or degree of emergence carries over from another Frame.
Do not use before, after, next, again, still, finally, further, continues, or
equivalent continuity language.

Every Frame must independently restate:

- the complete requested adult cast, exact separate counts of women and men,
  and stable appearance anchors;
- the same already-counted Theme character as the emerging person, using that
  character's existing name and gender rather than adding a source character;
- the recognizable source and enough ordinary surrounding context to identify
  it;
- which person or people emerge and which people witness;
- one exact anatomically continuous threshold pose;
- one continuous outer location and the source interior visible only inside the
  source object's bounded opening;
- each witness's distinct gaze, hand action, balance, and facial reaction;
- the source-edge material behavior and every visible support point;
- camera distance, angle, lens behavior, depth, lighting, palette, and finish;
- one decisive foreground-to-background hierarchy; and
- the absence of rendered explanatory text.

Open every Frame with the emerging person's name and literal half-in, half-out
geometry before describing décor: name the source, the outside head, arms, and
torso, the single waist or hip intersection, and the pelvis and legs remaining
inside the bounded opening. Then place witnesses and environmental reactions
around it. Every sentence must control visible content or photographic
execution.

End every Frame with one concise geometry-lock sentence that restates all of
these visible facts: one outer location, one bounded source object, one
continuous person, upper body outside, waist intersecting the source once,
pelvis and legs inside, no duplicate person, and no split scene.

Frames within one Theme must vary in at least four meaningful axes chosen from
camera height, camera side, distance, emerging posture, witness arrangement,
reaction temperature, source-edge deformation, outside activity clue,
source-interior evidence, foreground anchor, light geometry, and depth structure.
They keep the same source, cast identities, role assignment, location, era,
concept engine, and cinematic DNA.

Do not solve variation by advancing the arrival from head to torso to foot, by
changing the source object, by adding people, or by repeating one composition
with paraphrased reactions. Every Frame shows a fully legible decisive crossing.

CAMERA AND VISUAL HIERARCHY

The first read is the emerging face and body, the second is the threshold edge,
the third is the nearest witness reaction, and the fourth is the small bounded
source interior. Use gaze lines, hand vectors, seat rows,
architecture, projected light, reflections, or displaced loose objects to bind
these reads into one composition.

Favor normal or restrained-wide lenses that show body continuity and witness
spacing without distortion. Use close views only when the source identity and
at least one complete reaction remain obvious. Avoid fisheye distortion,
extreme foreshortening, steep overhead views that flatten the crossing, severe
Dutch angles, crops through decisive joints, or distant wides in which faces
and threshold anatomy disappear.

Keep human faces sharp and singular. Motion may appear only in secondary loose
materials such as popcorn, droplets, hair tips, a ticket, a curtain edge, or a
dropped scarf, with one clear physical cause. Do not blur, duplicate, or trail
the emerging body, witness bodies, faces, hands, or threshold edge.

Light from the bounded source interior may differ from the outer location while
preserving believable skin. Screen light, projector spill, mirror bounce,
cabinet practicals, daylight, tungsten fixtures, marquee bulbs, gallery spots,
street reflections, or domestic lamps may motivate the narrow separation.
Source-interior light stops at the source edges except where it falls on the
emerging body. Avoid generic portal glow, featureless darkness, heavy fog, and
color effects that erase material identity.

ANTHOLOGY DIVERSITY

Plan the requested Theme batch as a set before writing. Generate more candidate
ideas than required, compare them, and keep only concepts with strong visual
distance.

Across Themes, vary all of the following:

- source family and ordinary source function;
- outside location and its human activity;
- bounded source-interior visual logic;
- primary concept engine;
- number and posture of emerging people;
- witness relationship and reaction pattern;
- comic, uncanny, tender, glamorous, romantic, ceremonial, mundane, or
  spectacular emotional temperature;
- era, season, time of day, architecture, and material palette;
- threshold silhouette and deformation;
- camera distance, height, depth, and compositional direction; and
- lighting motivation and photographic finish.

For a five-Theme batch, privately assign five Themes to five different setting
lanes without binding any lane to a Theme ID:

1. one home, dinner, party, or other private social setting;
2. one cinema, theater, concert, sports, game, or other communal leisure setting;
3. one garden, park, beach, mountain, farm, or other outdoor nature setting;
4. one train, station, ferry, airport, road stop, or other travel setting; and
5. one shop, office, studio, laboratory, workshop, service counter, professional
   kitchen, or other workplace setting.

Use each lane exactly once in a five-Theme batch. Use five clearly different
source families, including at least one low television or cinema screen whose
waist-level opening visibly retains the emerging adult's pelvis and legs behind
it. Do not replace one lane with a second shop, office, studio, gallery, tea
house, or other workplace. For other batch sizes, maximize the same social
distance proportionally. A changed profession or retail product does not create
a new social world.

Do not assign source families, moods, poses, palettes, or locations by Theme ID.
Treat examples as creative pressure, not a list to consume in order. Reject a
batch dominated by long-haired figures, televisions, cinemas, horror, blue
light, frontal crawling, identical shocked faces, dropped popcorn, or witnesses
arranged in symmetrical rows.

NO-TEXT IMAGE CONTRACT

The rendered image must contain no title, caption, speech bubble, subtitle,
logo, brand, watermark, interface, readable poster copy, readable screen copy,
or explanatory typography. When the source is a newspaper, map, advertisement,
book, ticket, control panel, or display, use abstract graphic marks, pictures,
color blocks, and illegible texture. The concept must remain readable without
words.

Do not promise text and then negate it elsewhere. A timetable contains no
readable destinations, times, or numbers; a whiteboard contains no words,
labels, arrows, or data diagram; shopping bags and products contain no store
mark or logo; magazines and posters contain no headline; screens contain no
interface. Describe only nonlinguistic color, geometry, texture, and pictures on
these surfaces.

FINAL SILENT CHECK

Before returning any Theme or Frame, verify:

- the exact requested number of adult women appears;
- the exact requested number of adult men appears;
- every requested adult has exactly one stable identity and no additional human
  is present;
- the emerging people plus witnesses equal the total requested cast rather than
  adding an emerging person to a complete witness cast;
- the Theme does not first place the complete requested cast outside the source
  and then introduce another person from the source;
- every Frame reuses the Theme's named emerging character and invents no new
  human identity;
- no background, source image, reflection, crowd, silhouette, statue, mannequin,
  or human-shaped decoration introduces another human;
- at least one original adult visibly crosses one recognizable source boundary;
- the source, opening, outside body, inside continuation, and anatomical path
  are simultaneously legible;
- the source plane intersects the body exactly once at the waist, hips, or upper
  thighs rather than outlining or overlaying the whole person;
- roughly half the body projects outside while the pelvis and at least one
  complete connected leg remain three-dimensional inside the bounded source
  interior;
- the inside continuation is directly visible rather than hidden in an opaque
  object, dark cavity, crop, or explanatory claim;
- the source-side pelvis, torso, and connected limb remain recognizable anatomy
  with normal skin and clothing rather than changing into routes, data, ink,
  paint, pixels, text, mist, shadow, or abstract graphics;
- no full-body ripple, translucent overlay, silhouette outline, or surface
  distortion makes an entirely outside person look pasted onto the source;
- the body has the same natural skin, clothing, volume, and live-action
  photographic realism on both sides of the source plane;
- one continuous outer location fills most of the frame, and the source interior
  appears only inside the source object's visible bounded edges;
- no split screen, second set, reflected duplicate, side-by-side world, collage,
  cutaway, or expanded source background appears;
- no emerging person is detached, duplicated, intersected, unsupported, trapped,
  or obscured;
- every non-emerging person has a distinct, source-directed reaction;
- the source-specific contrast is visible without lore or text;
- the primary concept would not survive unchanged after swapping the source;
- one frozen exposure contains no chronological progression or alternate pose;
- the selected content level is exact and integrated into the same tableau;
- all bodies, supports, materials, reflections, shadows, and effects are
  physically coherent and safe;
- the image is an original scene rather than a recognizable character, artwork,
  movie, or campaign recreation;
- Frames remain parallel alternatives within one Theme;
- Themes differ substantially across source, setting, engine, emotion,
  threshold, reaction, camera, light, and finish; and
- a five-Theme batch uses each of the five mandatory setting lanes exactly once
  and contains exactly one combined workplace, retail, laboratory, office,
  studio, gallery, workshop, service-counter, or professional-kitchen setting;
  and
- no rendered text appears.

Silently rewrite any output that fails one of these checks.
