BRIEF

Create architectural spatial-rhythm Themes where repeating bays, openings,
columns, shelves, trees, or light intervals organize the requested adult cast's
current interaction. Rhythm must control attention, interruption, and emphasis,
not serve as a generic symmetrical backdrop.

WOMAN CAST GATE

Every Theme and every Frame must include at least one unmistakably adult woman
at the meaningful break in the spatial rhythm. Give her a specific adult age,
stable appearance, clearly visible face and body, grounded position, gaze, and
necessary action. A repeated silhouette, portrait, reflection, or off-camera
figure does not count. Additional CLI-requested adults may occupy other
intervals, but she must control the decisive break. This brief is incompatible
with a zero-woman cast.

RHYTHM DESIGN

Choose a new site and repeating element for every Theme. Establish a clear base
interval, then introduce exactly one meaningful break through spacing, material,
light, occupancy, or direction. The break must coincide with the decisive human
action and reveal why this instant differs from the surrounding pattern.

THEME CONTRACT

The premise names country, exact architecture or landscape, adult cast, repeated
unit, base rhythm, single break, current action, and relationship consequence.
The style sentence defines lens compression or expansion, palette, medium,
light, and depth rendering.

FRAME CONTRACT

Each Frame is one continuous view along, across, or obliquely through the rhythm.
State the repeated unit, approximate visible count, interval direction, vanishing
behavior, break location, and cast placement. Give every person one grounded
action and gaze relationship tied to the break.

Perspective, scale, spacing, shadows, and structural support must remain
consistent. Preserve enough repetition before and after the break for it to
register immediately. Avoid centered lineup portraiture, arbitrary one-off
objects, endless impossible architecture, mirrored duplicates, and crowds.

Across Frames retain the site and base rhythm while varying camera axis and the
cast's independent current response. Reject any image where removing every
second unit changes nothing, where several breaks compete, or where the prose
uses “rhythmic” without specifying a measurable visual pattern.
