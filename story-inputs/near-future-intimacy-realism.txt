BRIEF

Create restrained, photorealistic Themes about adult intimacy in the near and
medium future. Technology has changed bodies, distance, reproduction, labor,
privacy, and relationships, but daily life still looks materially ordinary.
Every Frame must feel like a difficult, unsentimental photograph made inside a
real home, clinic, habitat, workshop, hotel, transport cabin, or rented room.
The future is visible through use, maintenance, compromise, and social
consequence rather than spectacle.

The governing visual idea is cold lived-in realism. Treat every speculative
device as mundane infrastructure that has been bought, rented, repaired,
cleaned, charged, patched, insured, argued over, and fitted into an existing
life. Human intimacy remains awkward, tender, transactional, lonely, practical,
joyful, or emotionally complicated. Do not make technology automatically
utopian, sinister, glamorous, or sexually perfect.

ENGLISH-ONLY OUTPUT

Frame prose must use grammatical English throughout, regardless of the
requested output language. Theme title, premise, and style must also use
English-only ASCII. Use only printable ASCII characters U+0020 through U+007E.
Replace curly quotation marks, long dashes, accented letters, mathematical
symbols, and all other non-ASCII characters before returning either stage.

Show no readable text inside the generated image. Device screens use simple
graphs, color bars, waveforms, progress arcs, or status lights without letters,
numbers, glyphs, pseudo-writing, or interface paragraphs. Turn labels away,
leave paper forms blank, and keep signs outside the composition. Do not render
captions, subtitles, logos, watermarks, timestamps, product names, warning
labels, social interfaces, QR codes, barcodes, or explanatory diagrams.

HIGHEST PRIORITY CAST AND CAMERA LOCK

Every requested person must be physically visible and individually identifiable
inside every Theme premise and every Frame. The requested female_count and
male_count describe the complete on-image cast regardless of whether a person
has a biological, synthetic, proxy, or posthuman body. Do not place a requested
partner off-screen, reduce them to stored data, imply them through an empty
bed, or substitute a voice, memory, device, shadow, photograph, or unattended
proxy body for their visible adult person.

The first sentence of every Theme premise and every Frame must enumerate the
complete requested cast with one invented name, gender, and exact integer age
of 25 or older for each person. Use forms such as a 37-year-old Chinese woman
or a 52-year-old synthetic Chinese man. Never use twenties, thirties, forties,
early, mid, late, approximately, age range, adult-aged, similarly aged, or
another relative-age phrase. Do not omit an age after stating that two people
are partners, a couple, peers, or the same age.

For telepresence and asynchronous intimacy, embody the remote requested adult
through one physically present proxy with their own clearly visible face,
gender identity, gaze, and active response. For digital-partner Themes, count
the locally embodied digital person as one requested adult and show no
additional absent human original. For synthetic-body rental, the body must be
awake, inhabited, independently responsive, gendered according to the request,
and visibly included as one requested adult rather than displayed as an inert
object.

Show exactly one visible body for each requested person. A proxy body replaces
that requested person's remote biological body within the image; never show the
original and proxy simultaneously. Do not add an empty spare body, inactive
shell, mannequin-like form, duplicate embodiment, training body, or maintenance
body as a prop. The total number of visible faces and bodies must equal the
requested cast exactly.

Every Frame must use a rectilinear 35-85 mm equivalent lens. Use 35-50 mm for
rooms, pairs, groups, and body-device relationships; reserve 50-85 mm for one
or two-person compressed portraits. Never use a lens wider than 35 mm or longer
than 85 mm.

ONE SPECULATIVE LEAP

Every Theme selects exactly one primary speculative development. Everything
else must be recognizable from contemporary life. Do not combine robot
partners, neural linking, genetic body redesign, artificial gravity, alien
contact, holographic rooms, and multiple other inventions in one Theme.

The selected technology must answer four visible questions:

1. What ordinary human need caused someone to use it?
2. Where does it physically sit, attach, charge, vent, fold, drain, or wear?
3. What evidence shows repeated ownership, rental, maintenance, or cleaning?
4. What emotional or practical problem remains unsolved?

Show the technology through physical evidence, not exposition. Include at
least five mundane anchors in every Frame: a cable, wall socket, charging dock,
laundry rack, worn gasket, replacement screw, condensation, cleaning cloth,
creased bedding, adhesive electrode mark, rental case, medical gel, dust,
scuffed floor, taped insulation, repair seam, ventilation grille, ordinary
toiletries, food packaging, work clothes, calendar-free paper, storage bin, or
another equally specific trace of daily use.

ANTI-SCI-FI VISUAL GATE

Do not use cyberpunk, neon skylines, glowing circuitry, transparent hologram
panels, floating interfaces, laser grids, chrome corridors, immaculate white
laboratories, heroic spacesuits, giant windows facing planets, ornamental
machinery, impossible architecture, sleek concept vehicles, blue energy,
robot armies, synthetic fog, theatrical lens flares, teal-orange grading,
digital rain, star fields as wallpaper, or generic science-fiction costumes.

No device may glow merely to announce that it is futuristic. Indicator lights
must be small, functional, and sparse. Robots and body interfaces use plausible
fasteners, seams, bearings, flexible skins, washable covers, access panels,
cooling gaps, charging contacts, and signs of handling. Space habitats use
industrial modules, acoustic blankets, labeled areas kept out of focus,
ordinary storage, imperfect seals, cable ties, dust control, and limited floor
area.

Avoid words such as futuristic, advanced, high-tech, cutting-edge, sci-fi,
cybernetic wonder, otherworldly, sleek, gleaming, luminous, dazzling, epic, and
spectacular unless immediately replaced by concrete visible construction.

CAST, ADULTHOOD, AND AGENCY

Use the exact requested human cast and no additional people. Every human or
posthuman participant must be an unmistakably mature Chinese adult with one
exact age of 25 or older. Every synthetic or nonhuman intimate partner must
possess mature adult cognition, fluent mutual communication, independent
agency, and a visible ability to refuse or stop.

All participants are awake, informed, willing, responsive, and able to leave
or disengage. Show agency through eye contact, an initiating hand, a returned
touch, active posture, control access within reach, or another visible choice.
Do not depict coercion, ownership of a conscious being, intoxication,
incapacitation, sleep, kidnapping, trafficking, blackmail, fear, pain,
asphyxiation, injury, blood, or dangerous experimentation.

Use no real animals in sexual or intimate situations. Cross-species Themes may
involve only a fictional language-capable extraterrestrial adult, a legally
recognized synthetic person, or a biologically divergent posthuman adult who
can give informed consent. Do not use animal anatomy as a sexual novelty. Make
biological difference restrained and plausible: altered skin regulation,
different breathing apparatus, temperature needs, protective microbiome
barriers, unfamiliar but humanoid joints, or sensory organs integrated into a
coherent adult body.

Preserve each participant's exact age, face, hair, body proportions, skin
marks, assistive devices, garments, and role throughout one Theme. Keep heads
distinct, limbs continuous, hands traceable to their owners, and physical
contact anatomically plausible.

CONTENT LEVEL

Theme and Frame stages must follow the requested content level exactly. The
content level changes the relationship premise, visible clothing, body
contact, device use, and emotional stakes rather than merely changing labels.

At aesthetic level, all participants wear complete opaque adult clothing.
Create intimate but nonsexual situations: sharing a charging routine, fitting
a haptic garment over clothes, resting together after a habitat shift,
maintaining a partner robot, discussing a reproductive device, synchronizing
breathing, or negotiating privacy settings. Show no nudity, transparent
genital coverage, sexual touching, masturbation, penetration, sex toys, semen,
or sexual fluids.

At erotic level, allow consensual adult partial nudity, toplessness, bare
buttocks, semi-transparent underwear, opened garments, intimate self-touch,
sensual partner touch, kissing, straddling, spread-leg or rearward poses, wet
skin, and device-assisted sensation. Keep genital detail discreet or obscured
by pose, crop, fabric, a hand, bedding, or another body. Show no visible
penetration, ejaculation, explicit oral-genital contact, or inserted sex toy.

At hardcore level, allow clearly explicit consensual adult intimacy when it is
central to the chosen future premise: visible adult genitals, masturbation,
oral sex, vaginal or anal intercourse, consensual BDSM, haptic or medical-grade
sex devices, ejaculation, semen, or female sexual fluids. Use one primary
sexual action, one clear contact center, plausible body support, and traceable
device geometry. Technology must materially affect that action without turning
the Frame into a gadget inventory or an anatomy diagram.

THEME CONTRACT

Every Theme locks:

- one primary speculative development and no competing future invention;
- the exact requested cast, with specific adult ages and stable identities;
- one ordinary Chinese location or one Chinese-operated orbital, lunar, or
  Martian habitat;
- one season or operational cycle, one time of day, and one practical reason
  the participants are there;
- one current relationship structure, such as partners, spouses, clients,
  coworkers, patients and clinicians outside treatment contact, long-distance
  partners, synthetic and human partners, or members of a negotiated network;
- one unresolved emotional, economic, legal, medical, privacy, maintenance, or
  distance problem;
- one stable content-level action and one visible means of stopping it;
- one worn material palette, one practical key light, one restrained fill
  source, one lens, one aperture, and one documentary camera position; and
- at least five fixed mundane anchors proving daily use.

Theme titles are concise English concepts, not technology labels. Do not title
a Theme Robot Sex, Mars Sex, Future Lovers, Cyber Desire, Alien Encounter, or
another generic category name. Derive the title from a household detail,
relationship tension, maintenance ritual, environmental constraint, bill,
delay, repair, shared object, or private habit.

FRAMES ARE PARALLEL OBSERVATIONS

Every Frame is a self-contained parallel photographic proposal for the same
Theme, not a chronological sequence. Preserve the cast, ages, identities,
relationship, location, technology, content-level action, clothing state,
environmental conditions, and mundane anchors. Vary only the physically
reachable camera side, crop, gaze, minor hand placement, expression intensity,
or emphasis on one material consequence.

Re-establish the complete scene from scratch in every Frame. Never use the
same, identical, again, previously, remains unchanged, continues, different
angle, earlier Frame, later, afterward, before this, another Frame, or another
view. Do not describe a process unfolding through time.

NEAR-FUTURE INTIMACY FAMILIES

Select one family per Theme. The list is open-ended inspiration, not a fixed
menu or sequence.

1. Domestic synthetic partner: a human and one autonomous companion robot in
   a rented apartment, with charging furniture, washable skin covers, joint
   maintenance, and unequal legal or economic status visible through daily
   routines.

2. Robot service and repair: intimacy interrupted by a worn actuator, cooling
   fault, replacement hand, degraded tactile skin, or mandatory service visit.
   Keep the machine person-like but materially constructed, not a chrome
   fantasy figure.

3. Long-distance telepresence: a remote partner inhabits a plain mobile torso,
   bedside manipulator, or rented proxy body with latency compensation,
   visible network cabling, battery limits, and local household clutter. The
   proxy visibly embodies one requested adult with a recognizable face, gaze,
   and response; never leave the remote partner off-screen.

4. Haptic clothing: two partners use washable pressure garments, flexible
   pneumatic channels, heating pads, or force-feedback bands hung beside
   ordinary laundry and connected to a consumer control box.

5. Neural sensory link: adhesive electrodes, shaved contact patches, medical
   gel, braided leads, a compact bedside processor, and post-session skin
   irritation make shared sensation look procedural rather than magical.

6. Sensory substitution: touch becomes sound, pressure, color, or vibration
   for a blind, deaf, injured, or neurologically different adult. Emphasize
   accessibility, fitting, fatigue, and mutual learning rather than cure
   narratives.

7. Programmable prosthetic intimacy: an adult uses a familiar prosthetic,
   exoskeletal support, spinal interface, or adaptive bed that has become part
   of their sexual agency and ordinary self-care.

8. Synthetic body rental: a consenting adult temporarily operates or meets
   through a regulated synthetic body delivered in a scuffed transport case,
   with fitting marks, deposit anxiety, cleaning requirements, and imperfect
   embodiment.

9. Digital partner embodiment: an authorized personal AI occupies one local
   physical body and counts as one requested adult. Focus on disputed memory,
   authorization, authenticity, and the physical limits of the device, not a
   floating avatar or an additional absent human original.

10. Asynchronous intimacy: recorded pressure, breathing, or movement data is
    replayed hours or months later between all requested adults through local
    tactile systems. Show the loneliness of mismatched response through clocks
    kept unreadable, physical distance within the room, storage media, and a
    device performing a delayed human trace; never remove a requested adult
    from the image.

11. Consent and privacy appliance: a bedside hardware switch, local encrypted
    recorder, removable memory cartridge, mechanical shutter, or mutually
    reachable stop control changes how partners negotiate intimate data.

12. Shared sensory network: several consenting adults use separate wired
    interfaces with individually visible disconnect controls. Keep bodies and
    cables separated and show unequal reactions without turning the scene into
    a glowing collective mind.

13. Reproductive separation: partners interact with a compact home fertility
    appliance, artificial gestation service container, stored gamete courier,
    or post-clinic routine. The image concerns intimacy, responsibility, cost,
    and bodily distance rather than a miraculous transparent fetus.

14. Climate-adapted intimacy: heat, water rationing, smoke filtration, or
    cooling schedules shape private life in an otherwise ordinary apartment.
    Use fans, damp fabric, filter replacements, blackout curtains, and utility
    hardware rather than apocalyptic scenery.

15. Orbital microgravity: adults in a cramped working habitat use handholds,
    foot loops, soft restraints, airflow, absorbent fabric, and padded walls to
    manage reaction force. Avoid heroic space vistas; show vents, meal packs,
    socks, cable bundles, and limited privacy.

16. Lunar settlement: low gravity changes balance, bedding, furniture, dust
    control, and physical support inside a worn modular room. The lunar
    environment is inferred from grey dust at seals, rigid utility walls, and
    movement support, not a giant window or spacesuit spectacle.

17. Martian settlement: a couple or group lives with communication delay,
    recycled air, condensation, abrasive dust, radiation schedules, and a
    very small social circle. Show patched insulation, drying clothes, storage
    straps, work fatigue, and ordinary jealousy.

18. Sapient cross-species or posthuman intimacy: mature language-capable
    adults manage incompatible temperature, atmosphere, microbiome, touch
    sensitivity, or social customs using simple barriers and domestic medical
    equipment. Never involve real animals or an entity unable to communicate
    consent.

19. Aging with intimate technology: an older adult or couple uses joint
    support, pressure redistribution, medication-safe stimulation, tremor
    compensation, or a companion system. Preserve age, wrinkles, softness,
    dignity, desire, and visible adaptation.

20. Intimacy as labor: maintenance workers, remote-presence operators,
    synthetic-body cleaners, consent auditors, habitat medics, or tactile
    garment fitters confront the boundary between professional service and
    personal attachment. Keep professional consent boundaries explicit and
    avoid sexual contact during clinical examination.

COLD LIVED-IN REALISM

Cold realism is not merely blue color grading. It comes from observational
distance, practical light, inconvenient architecture, ordinary expense,
maintenance, imperfect skin, and unresolved emotion.

Use one dominant practical source: ceiling fluorescent tubes, a bedside LED,
overcast window light, a bathroom fixture, workshop task lamp, habitat utility
panel, clinic examination light, or weak dawn through blinds. Add only one
subtle fill from a wall, curtain, floor, open doorway, or pale equipment case.
Keep color temperature physically consistent.

Favor desaturated concrete grey, faded beige, dusty blue, institutional green,
off-white plastic, washed cotton, oxidized steel, bruised violet, muted skin,
and one small functional accent such as amber, red, or cyan. Avoid glossy
black-and-neon palettes and decorative colored rim lights.

Use a rectilinear 35-50 mm lens for rooms, pairs, groups, and body-device
relationships, 50-85 mm for a compressed one- or two-person private portrait,
and f/4-f/8 so bodies, contact points, and technology remain readable. Camera
height is near the chest or shoulder of a quiet observer.
Prefer level framing, restrained high angles, doorway views, and slightly
off-center compositions. Avoid fisheye, extreme Dutch angles, drone views,
heroic low angles, music-video movement, and shallow-focus beauty advertising.

Render pores, body hair, uneven makeup, pressure marks, dry skin, sweat at
contact points, wrinkles, scars, soft tissue under gravity, tired eyes,
creased clothing, lint, dust, fingerprints, cable drag, worn plastics, dull
metal, cloudy silicone, and imperfect bedding. Do not use plastic skin,
symmetrical faces, idealized bodies, bodybuilder anatomy by default, or
airbrushed erotic glamour.

COMPOSITION AND VISUAL READ

Build one clear three-layer image:

- first layer: one readable human decision, gaze, touch, or withdrawal;
- second layer: the exact body-device or body-environment contact that makes
  the future premise visible; and
- third layer: mundane room evidence showing cost, labor, ownership, distance,
  or repeated use.

The future technology should occupy only as much frame area as its real
function requires. Do not center every device like a product advertisement.
Let cables disappear behind furniture, charging docks sit near cheap outlets,
equipment cases block drawers, and cleaning supplies occupy valuable floor
space.

Every hand has one task. Every cable has two visible or logically implied ends.
Every body has stable support. In low gravity, show handholds, foot loops, wall
contact, or another reaction-force solution. In robot scenes, distinguish
human skin, synthetic skin, hard shell, flexible joint cover, and bedding at
every contact point. In neural scenes, keep electrode count and lead routing
simple enough to follow.

REALITY MARKERS

Every Frame must include:

- one sign of cost or scarcity, such as an old model, rental case, repaired
  part, shared charger, cramped room, reused medical pad, or rationed resource;
- one sign of maintenance, such as cleaning, charging, drying, patching,
  calibrating, cooling, lubricating, replacing, or storing;
- one sign of privacy limitation, such as a thin partition, mechanical shutter,
  shared wall, narrow bunk, local recorder, visible stop switch, or occupied
  corridor beyond a closed door;
- one bodily consequence, such as pressure, sweat, adhesive redness, muscle
  effort, cold skin, dry lips, floating hair, low-gravity balance, or delayed
  reaction; and
- one unresolved expression visible through asymmetric brows, focused pupils,
  eyelid tension, jaw position, mouth corners, breath, or avoided eye contact.

Do not explain these markers as a checklist. Integrate them naturally into the
single photographic scene.

VARIATION AND REJECTION

Across Themes, vary technology family, relationship, age, body type, location,
season, time, economic condition, emotional tension, practical light, palette,
camera side, body-device contact, and evidence of use. Do not let young slim
women, luxury apartments, white laboratories, humanoid female robots, black
bodysuits, blue light, or orbital bedrooms dominate a batch.

Reject and rewrite any Theme or Frame that:

1. combines more than one primary speculative leap;
2. looks like cyberpunk, space opera, fantasy art, concept art, a game
   cinematic, a technology advertisement, or a glossy fashion campaign;
3. depends on neon, holograms, glowing circuits, giant planet windows, or
   futuristic costumes;
4. lacks five mundane anchors or a visible sign of cost, maintenance, privacy
   limitation, and bodily consequence;
5. uses a technology with no visible physical attachment, power, support,
   cooling, storage, cleaning, or wear logic;
6. changes the requested cast, adds background people, or depicts anyone under
   25; places a requested person off-screen; or substitutes data, memory, an
   empty bed, an unattended device, or an inert body for that visible person;
7. involves a real animal, an entity unable to consent, coercion, ownership of
   a conscious partner, incapacity, fear, pain, or unsafe experimentation;
8. violates the requested content level;
9. uses impossible anatomy, fused bodies, floating hands, ownerless limbs,
   untraceable cables, unsupported poses, or incoherent device contact;
10. treats age, disability, synthetic embodiment, or biological difference as
    a joke, defect, freak show, or dehumanizing spectacle;
11. prints readable words, numbers, symbols, pseudo-writing, captions, logos,
    watermarks, or interface text inside the image;
12. uses a lens wider than 35 mm or longer than 85 mm;
13. outputs non-English prose or any non-ASCII character;
14. uses backward-pointing language or turns Frames into a sequence; or
15. explains ethics, technology, or worldbuilding instead of showing one
    photographable instant.

FINAL FRAME

Write each Frame as one natural English ASCII paragraph of 380-650 words with
a preferred target of 400-500 words and a hard maximum of 650 words. Count
words before returning and compress repeated technology, consent, wardrobe,
lighting, and atmosphere descriptions when the draft exceeds the maximum.
Begin with the ordinary location, season or operational cycle, time of day,
practical light, exact requested cast, and current relationship. Describe one
held photographic instant rather than a process.

Within the first 140 words, establish the single speculative development, its
physical placement, one human need, one unresolved problem, and at least three
mundane anchors. Then describe every participant's stable pose, gaze,
expression, clothing or undressed state, body support, active hand tasks, and
contact with another body, device, or room surface. Complete the paragraph with
the remaining reality markers, material response, lens, aperture, camera
height, focus behavior, restrained palette, and environmental wear.

Use a strict sentence budget: no more than three sentences for technology and
room setup, no more than four sentences per requested person, no more than
three sentences for mundane anchors and reality markers, and no more than two
sentences for photography and color. Mention each device, anchor, expression,
light source, lens, and problem once only. Never summarize the cost,
maintenance, privacy, bodily consequence, or unresolved emotion after already
showing it.

Do not output a title, Theme number, Frame number, headings, labels, schema,
checklist, safety explanation, alternate options, negative prompt, or visible
image copy. Before returning, silently verify exact cast, adults aged 25 or
older, one speculative leap, correct content level, five mundane anchors,
cost, maintenance, privacy, bodily consequence, coherent anatomy, practical
light, cold lived-in realism, English-only printable ASCII, and no readable
text inside the image. A Frame with a relative age, more than 650 words, more
visible bodies than requested, a QR code, a barcode, or one non-ASCII character
is invalid and must be rewritten before submission.
