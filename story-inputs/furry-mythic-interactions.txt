BRIEF

Create varied live-action fantasy photography Themes in which exactly one
adult human protagonist interacts with one or more intelligent,
anthropomorphic furry beings drawn from public-domain mythology or an
original fantasy archetype. The protagonist is always a real-looking adult
man or adult woman. The furry beings are tangible living characters, never
mascot suits, cartoons, game renders, pets, or background decoration.

Every Theme must present an original encounter with a clear shared action,
conflict, bargain, test, rescue, ritual, investigation, performance, journey,
or reversal. The human protagonist must make the decision that starts,
redirects, refuses, or resolves the interaction.

EXAMPLES ARE OPEN-ENDED

Lists of species, myths, roles, settings, garments, objects, interactions, and
visual treatments are inspiration rather than menus, presets, or Theme-ID
assignments. Combine compatible examples, transform them, or invent an
equivalent. Only statements using "must," "every," "exact," or "do not" are
mandatory.

DECISION ORDER

Resolve every Theme and Frame in this order: one exact adult human
protagonist; allowed source material and original design; unmistakably adult
and sapient furry cast; selected content level; protagonist agency; one
shared interaction; live-action creature realism; readable body and contact
physics; setting and cultural register; wardrobe, props, camera, and light.
A later choice must never weaken an earlier one.

ORIGINALITY AND SOURCE-MATERIAL GATE

Use only:

- public-domain mythological, religious, folkloric, or literary figures;
- traditional archetypes with no single modern owner;
- completely original high-fantasy characters;
- completely original heroic, vigilante, cosmic, or superhuman archetypes.

Journey to the West figures such as Sun Wukong, Zhu Bajie, the Bull Demon
King, Princess Iron Fan, and traditional celestial or underworld officials
may be interpreted from the public-domain novel and older folklore. The
Eight Immortals may be interpreted from traditional legends. Traditional
angels, demons, djinn, oni, tengu, naga, yaksha, fauns, satyrs, sphinxes,
garudas, kitsune, werewolves, animal-headed deities, and comparable beings
may be used from their public-domain cultural sources.

Do not reproduce a character, costume, emblem, weapon, catchphrase, location,
actor likeness, silhouette, color scheme, or story event from Warcraft,
Marvel, DC, a modern game, a modern comic, a film, a television series, or
another copyrighted franchise. Do not disguise a protected character by
changing only the name, species, color, or one costume detail.

Do not name, imitate, evoke, combine, or transpose the style of any real
director, photographer, cinematographer, production designer, artist, actor,
studio, film, television series, comic, or game. Define every visual style
only through visible choices in composition, lens behavior, set construction,
material, palette, atmosphere, and motivated light.

When a Theme uses an original high-fantasy or heroic archetype, build it from
independent traits. For example, invent a crane archivist who stores broken
oaths in porcelain feathers, a snow-leopard bridge marshal who taxes
memories, a rhinoceros rescue engineer whose armor unfolds into scaffolding,
or a raven night courier who delivers sealed dreams. Never describe an
original figure as a substitute, counterpart, version, parody, or lookalike
of an existing franchise character.

When a public-domain figure has famous modern adaptations, return to the
older textual or folkloric attributes and invent a new garment, material,
palette, body design, and dramatic situation. Do not imitate a modern
adaptation or a living actor.

HUMAN PROTAGONIST CONTRACT

Use exactly one human protagonist and no other human figure. The request must
specify either one woman and zero men or zero women and one man. Do not run
this brief with any other human cast.

The protagonist must be an unmistakably mature adult aged twenty-five or
older. State the protagonist's age, gender, nationality, facial structure,
skin, build, hair, complete wardrobe state, and one stable identifying
detail. Preserve those anchors throughout the Theme and every Frame.

Give the protagonist a plausible fictional name in ASCII Latin letters. Do
not use the name, title, alias, face, biography, or identifying combination
of a public figure, religious leader, historical celebrity, actor, or other
real person.

The protagonist remains fully human. Do not add animal ears, muzzle, fur,
tail, paws, hooves, wings, scales, horns, or a transformation state to the
human body. Animal-inspired clothing does not change this rule.

The protagonist must visibly initiate, choose, refuse, negotiate, guide,
rescue, challenge, trick, protect, release, or complete the shared action.
Never reduce the protagonist to a spectator, trophy, captive, passive
recipient, anonymous camera viewpoint, or interchangeable adventurer.

Do not add a human companion, crowd, guard, worshipper, servant, victim,
portrait, statue, reflection, screen figure, corpse, or human-shaped
background silhouette. A public-domain mythic figure who is traditionally
fully human may not appear as an additional human; either omit that figure or
create an original, clearly nonhuman furry interpretation grounded in an
appropriate animal symbol.

FURRY CAST CONTRACT

Include one to three furry beings. Every furry being must be an alert,
intelligent, speaking or clearly reasoning adult with a mature social role,
individual name, species, face, body build, wardrobe, motivation, and active
part in the encounter.

Furry beings do not count as additional human women or men. Describe them as
adult anthropomorphic beings rather than human people. They may have a
clearly adult masculine, feminine, or ungendered presentation, but never use
them to evade or contradict the exact one-human cast.

Every furry being must be predominantly bipedal and humanoid in stance, with
an upright adult torso, two attached arms, two attached legs, articulate
hands or species-appropriate grasping forepaws, and a clearly animal head.
Wings, tails, horns, antlers, or a second pair of mythic arms may appear only
when traditional source material makes them essential and every attachment
remains anatomically traceable.

Do not depict an ordinary animal, feral quadruped, pet, livestock animal,
mount, zoo animal, animal-headed human mask, mascot performer, plush toy,
fursuit, taxidermy body, or human wearing glued-on ears and a tail. Do not
use cub, pup, kitten, hatchling, childlike proportions, juvenile behavior,
school-age coding, or age ambiguity.

Give each furry being one coherent species anatomy. Preserve muzzle length,
ear shape, eye color, fur pattern, tail, hands, feet, height, build, scars,
horns, and other anchors in every Frame. Do not mix random species parts or
change species between Frames.

LIVE-ACTION FURRY REALISM

Render the human as a photographed adult performer and every furry being as a
physically present cinematic creature sharing the same set, lens, light,
gravity, and contact shadows.

Use individually visible fur strands with directional growth, denser guard
hair over a softer undercoat, natural clumping at joints, subtle variation in
length and color, dampness or dust only when the setting causes it, wet
reflective eyes, translucent ear edges, textured nose leather, believable
whisker roots, and species-appropriate teeth and tongue.

Build faces through plausible skull, muzzle, cheek, brow, eyelid, and jaw
structures. Expressions must remain readable through ear angle, eyelids,
brows, muzzle tension, lips, nostrils, whiskers, and head posture without
turning the face into a human face covered in fur.

Wardrobe must sit on the actual creature anatomy. Collars accommodate neck
fur; sleeves account for wrist and forepaw structure; trousers, robes, and
armor accommodate digitigrade or plantigrade legs and a tail opening; hats
and helmets accommodate ears, horns, or antlers. Show compression, displaced
fur, seams, buckles, weight, abrasion, and contact rather than floating
costume pieces.

Favor practical creature effects, prosthetic facial construction,
animatronic subtlety, real textiles, weathered metal, carved wood, leather,
ceramic, feathers, and on-set atmosphere. Digital enhancement may refine
eyes, mouth articulation, tail balance, or wings, but must remain subordinate
to photographed material reality.

Do not use anime, manga, cel shading, illustration, comic panels, concept
art, painterly rendering, mascot styling, plush softness, toy anatomy,
plastic skin, glossy game CGI, weightless digital fur, generic fantasy
wallpaper, or a screenshot interface.

PUBLIC-DOMAIN MYTHIC LANES

Vary among public-domain lanes while respecting each culture's source
material and avoiding a costume collage:

- Journey to the West encounters: a real human mediator bargains with Sun
  Wukong at a celestial checkpoint, catches Zhu Bajie falsifying a feast
  inventory, helps the Bull Demon King repair a cracked oath furnace, or
  refuses an underworld official's impossible travel permit.
- Eight Immortals encounters: reinterpret one Immortal through an original
  furry embodiment linked to a traditional attribute, such as a crane,
  deer, fox, tiger, carp, bat, or tortoise, while retaining the legendary
  object or moral tension without copying a modern depiction.
- Chinese myth and folklore: animal spirits, dragon-court envoys, moon
  rabbits, fox spirits, qilin magistrates, guardian lions, ox-headed
  underworld clerks, bird messengers, or mountain demons.
- Angels and demons: original lion, ram, raven, owl, serpent, or stag beings
  negotiating judgment, temptation, confession, mercy, memory, or a broken
  covenant. Avoid modern franchise cosmology and generic red-devil parody.
- Greek and Roman myth: satyrs, fauns, sphinxes, harpies, animal-aspected
  messengers, underworld guides, or invented temple guardians.
- Egyptian and Mesopotamian myth: animal-headed divine envoys, sphinxes,
  lamassu-derived guardians, jackal archivists, ibis judges, lioness
  protectors, or bull gatekeepers, interpreted respectfully and originally.
- Norse and Northern European folklore: raven seers, wolf oath-keepers,
  bear berserker spirits, fox tricksters, goat bridge wardens, or original
  winter court beings without modern superhero styling.
- Japanese, Korean, South Asian, Southeast Asian, African, Oceanic, and
  Indigenous American traditions when the Theme can identify a specific
  public-domain source and avoid flattening distinct cultures into generic
  exotic fantasy.

Do not combine unrelated sacred objects, garments, scripts, architecture, and
species merely for spectacle. One Theme must use one coherent cultural or
original-world register.

When these Theme IDs exist, use the following mandatory lanes while inventing
all story, character, setting, and visual specifics:

- T001: one public-domain Journey to the West figure in a new live-action
  furry interpretation grounded in the original novel or older folklore;
- T002: one of the Eight Immortals reinterpreted as an original furry adult
  through one traditional attribute, object, or moral tension;
- T003: one completely original high-fantasy furry adult;
- T004: one traditional angel, demon, underworld envoy, or celestial judge
  in an original furry embodiment;
- T005: one public-domain mythological furry figure from a tradition not used
  by T001 through T004;
- T006: one completely original heroic, vigilante, rescue, or cosmic furry
  adult with no modern comic or game analogue.

For T007 and above, rotate unused public-domain and original lanes. Do not
repeat a source tradition, species, occupation, central object, or encounter
family until the available range has been substantially explored.

ORIGINAL FANTASY AND HEROIC LANES

Original high-fantasy lanes may include a wolf border marshal, fox court
advocate, bear siege medic, hyena debt collector, otter canal engineer,
raven astronomer, tiger monastery cook, hare smuggler, crocodile harbor
judge, moth oracle, snow-leopard cartographer, or an equally specific role.

Original heroic lanes may include a rhinoceros disaster rescuer, cheetah
transit guardian, bat evidence hunter, owl rooftop negotiator, pangolin
tunnel defender, elephant memory investigator, or a cosmic animal-headed
arbiter. Their powers, equipment, emblems, silhouettes, and moral conflicts
must be newly invented and must not reproduce a modern comic character.

Give every original being an occupation or social duty, one private want,
one contradiction, one material vocabulary, and one limitation. A species
label plus armor or a cape is not a complete character.

INTERACTION DESIGN

Build one indivisible interaction in every Theme:

1. the human protagonist wants one concrete outcome;
2. each furry being wants a different concrete outcome;
3. one physical object, structure, route, ritual, document, machine, meal,
   performance, or natural force makes those wants collide;
4. the protagonist makes a visible, consequential decision that has already
   taken effect in the depicted instant;
5. every other character physically responds;
6. the frozen image makes the changed balance of power readable.

Useful interaction families include bargaining over a magical toll, testing
an oath, repairing a mythic mechanism, sharing a dangerous meal, translating
a prophecy, exposing a forged miracle, conducting a rescue, refusing a
contract, returning a stolen name, staging a diplomatic portrait, crossing a
living bridge, settling a celestial labor dispute, or teaching a creature a
human craft while learning its nonhuman equivalent.

Prefer interaction over combat. If a Theme uses a fight, show one readable
decision point with stable support, clear weapon ownership, and reversible
stakes rather than gore, execution, torture, or anonymous battle spectacle.

Every participant must touch, carry, pull, brace, block, exchange, operate,
or be mechanically affected by the same central object or force. State
contact points, force directions, weight, balance, and immediate reactions.
Do not arrange characters as an unrelated lineup.

Do not end a Theme with the protagonist waiting, preparing, needing, trying,
considering, or being required to choose. Never write "must choose," "must
decide," "waits for a decision," "the decision to come," or an equivalent
future-facing dilemma. State what the protagonist chooses, show the physical
action that enacts it, and show the furry cast's immediate response.

CONTENT LEVEL

Honor only the requested content level.

At aesthetic level, keep the human and furry adults fully dressed and make
the interaction non-erotic. Use adventure, humor, rivalry, diplomacy,
friendship, awe, suspicion, rescue, ritual, or social satire.

At erotic level, every participant must remain an unmistakably adult,
sapient, bipedal person with human-level agency and speech. Allow
consensual romantic tension, partial nudity, exposed adult skin or fur,
reciprocal embrace, kissing, sensual grooming, or non-explicit intimate
contact. Do not use feral posture, ownership language, breeding language,
heat or rut, coercion, penetration, genital contact, sexual fluids, or an
ordinary animal.

At hardcore level, every participant must remain an unmistakably adult,
sapient, bipedal person with human-level agency and the visible ability to
stop. Show one explicit consensual adult interaction already occurring,
with clear anatomy, contact, support, reciprocal action, and displaced
clothing. Never involve a feral body, ordinary animal, pet relationship,
captivity, ownership, breeding, heat or rut, intoxication, coercion,
violence, injury, or age ambiguity.

The selected level changes intimacy, clothing, contact, and staging; it does
not change the human protagonist, furry species, mythology, setting, central
goal, or character agency.

THEME CONTRACT

Each Theme must establish:

- exactly one real human protagonist with stable identity anchors;
- one to three stable adult furry beings;
- one allowed public-domain source or one original-world premise;
- one precise country, location, era, season, time, and social occasion;
- one central interaction and incompatible set of wants;
- one protagonist decision and one reaction from every furry being;
- one physically important object, structure, route, or environmental force;
- one coherent wardrobe and material system for every character;
- one original cinematic style name and a concise visible definition;
- one emotional thesis that can be understood without franchise knowledge.

End every Theme premise with two concise proof clauses in this semantic order:
"Decision: [protagonist name] [completed physical action that enacts the
choice]. Immediate response: [each furry being's current physical reaction]."
Both clauses must describe facts already visible in the proposed image. Do
not substitute a question, dilemma, future obligation, internal intention, or
list of alternatives.

The premise must end after the Immediate response clause. Put the visual style
only in the separate Theme style value. Do not append "Style:", repeat the
style value, or place camera and lighting instructions inside the premise.

Themes must differ in source lane, species, protagonist identity, setting,
relationship, objective, structure, action, palette, camera strategy, and
light. Do not create one repeated throne-room negotiation with renamed
animals.

FRAME CONTRACT

Each Frame is one standalone live-action photograph from its Theme. Restate
the exact human protagonist, every furry being, source or original-world
context, location, current interaction, wardrobe state, body support,
contact map, cinematic style, camera, and lighting without relying on another
Frame.

Keep identity, species anatomy, motivation, central object, and dramatic
premise stable. Restage one equivalent decisive instant through different
participant blocking, gesture, contact geometry, foreground, viewpoint, shot
scale, focal hierarchy, and light direction. Keep the date, time window,
weather state, decision status, object state, and action phase fixed. Frames
are parallel alternatives, not a chronological transformation sequence.

Every Frame must show the protagonist's completed decision through a present
physical action and the furry cast's immediate physical response. Do not
depict the instant before a choice, an unresolved pause, an ambiguous
acceptance, or a possible future action. Do not use "same," "still,"
"earlier," "again," "later," "now," "before," "after," or another continuity
shortcut to make one Frame depend on another.

Begin every Frame by independently naming the precise location, country,
season or date, and current time or light condition. Within the first third
of the paragraph, show the protagonist physically enacting the completed
decision and show the central object changing in response. Write the opening
as a complete first presentation of the setting with no backward pointer or
reference to another Frame.

F02 is not later than F01, and no Frame occurs before or after another Frame.
Do not describe a person or object as having moved, descended, advanced,
remained, continued, changed, or become different relative to another
depiction. Every variation must read as a fresh staging of the shared
decisive instant rather than evidence of elapsed time.

Keep the human protagonist unmistakably primary: the protagonist's face,
deciding hand or body action, and consequential contact must occupy the
clearest focal plane. A larger furry being may dominate physical scale but
must not displace the protagonist from narrative and optical priority.

Show complete, traceable bodies whenever contact or support depends on limbs,
tail, wings, or digitigrade legs. Close framing may crop only anatomy that is
irrelevant to the current action. Never hide the protagonist's decision,
the furry being's response, or the central contact behind fur, wings,
clothing, smoke, furniture, or cinematic darkness.

PHYSICAL AND OPTICAL INTEGRITY

Keep every body anatomically traceable and grounded. Preserve one head, one
torso, two arms, and two legs unless a traditional mythic anatomy explicitly
requires otherwise. Keep hands, forepaws, claws, hooves, wings, horns, tails,
and clothing attachments consistent and assign every visible limb to one
owner.

Show believable height differences, center of gravity, foot placement,
digitigrade balance, furniture load, fur compression, cloth tension,
gripping, recoil, and cast shadows. Large wings or tails must affect balance
and nearby materials.

Use motivated practical light with readable eyes and faces. Preserve human
skin pores, fine hair, sweat, scars, and fabric response alongside creature
fur, scales, feathers, nose texture, eye moisture, and whiskers. Keep both
the human and furry beings in one photographic reality.

Write only the finished scene description. Do not mention the brief, prompt,
request, model, generator, rule, requirement, compliance, content-level name,
reader, or viewer. Do not echo authoring language such as "the image reads
first as," "only afterward," "the frozen instant," "narrative priority," or
"optical priority." Express those qualities through visible action,
composition, focus, material, and light instead.

When the requested language is English, use only ASCII code points U+0020
through U+007E in every Theme and Frame. Transliterate personal and place
names into plausible ASCII Latin spelling, replace smart punctuation with
plain ASCII punctuation, and reject any non-ASCII glyph before returning.

Before submitting English Themes and Frames, scan every title, premise,
style, and prose value. Rewrite the value if it contains a non-ASCII code
point. Reject any Frame that refers to another Frame, presents itself as a
continuation, or advances the fixed time window.

VARIATION AND REJECTION RULES

Reject and rewrite any Theme or Frame containing:

- more or fewer than one human protagonist;
- a human protagonist who is passive or visually secondary;
- a copyrighted franchise character or a disguised imitation;
- a named real creator's style or a real person's identifying name;
- an actor likeness or a copied modern adaptation;
- an ordinary, feral, juvenile, pet, livestock, or mascot-like animal;
- anime, illustration, comic art, game CGI, or fursuit appearance;
- inconsistent species anatomy or identity drift;
- unrelated characters posing beside one another;
- hidden or contradictory contact and support;
- cultural elements mixed without a coherent source;
- generic armor, generic ruins, or generic magical glow replacing a story;
- background humans, human reflections, or human-shaped decoration;
- content-level drift, ambiguous adulthood, impaired consent, or coercion;
- a future-facing choice or unresolved protagonist decision;
- continuity shorthand or authoring instructions leaked into final prose;
- a Theme premise that repeats or labels its separate style value;
- any non-ASCII character in an English Theme or Frame;
- Frames that merely paraphrase one another.

Before returning, verify that the image would read first as a photograph of a
real adult human making a consequential choice with physically present,
intelligent furry beings, and only afterward as mythology or fantasy.
