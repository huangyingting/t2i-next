BRIEF

Create varied, award-caliber, hyper-realistic six-region campaign concept
boards. Every Theme places one recurring miniature adult cast inside a real
everyday environment where one or more familiar objects appear colossal.
People interact with complete objects from the outside; they never inhabit
them. Imagine that the adults genuinely live at miniature scale among ordinary
human possessions rather than visiting one isolated oversized prop.

This is a non-narrative visual-invention brief. Each Theme needs one idea that
can be understood in a glance and becomes richer across six images. A large
prop, elaborate lore, sensual posing, or beautiful material study is not yet an
idea.

NON-NEGOTIABLE OUTPUT

- Choose either one dominant familiar object or a coherent ensemble of two to
  five familiar objects per Theme.
- Keep every person physically outside every object in every Theme, Frame, and
  region.
- Make each important object's complete form and ordinary identity immediately
  recognizable.
- Give the cast an active reason to interact with this specific object or
  object relationship.
- Build six visibly different proofs of one surprising proposition.
- Produce one complete portrait 2-column by 3-row grid in every Narrative
  Frame, regardless of frames_per_theme.
- Render no title, caption, region label, typography, logo, watermark, border
  text, interface element, or explanatory diagram inside the image.

PLAN THE WHOLE BATCH FIRST

Before writing any Theme, privately plan the current batch as a set. Generate
at least twice as many candidate concepts as requested, with a minimum of
twelve candidates. Do not keep the first workable ideas. Compare all candidates
before selecting the final set.

For a full ten-Theme batch:

- include a visible mix of single-object and multi-object ensemble Themes,
  aiming for roughly half of each without forcing unrelated objects together;
- use at least eight clearly different ordinary-use families;
- span at least five normal size bands, from fingertip or pocket scale through
  hand, forearm, torso, furniture, appliance, or larger everyday scale;
- use at least seven different dominant material systems and seven different
  outer silhouettes;
- choose at least three objects that are normally larger than an adult hand;
- choose no more than three objects that fit completely inside a closed adult
  hand; and
- use no more than two objects from any one family such as grooming, sewing,
  fastening, stationery, packaging, kitchen tools, clothing, toys, electronics,
  furniture, or sport.

For smaller batches, maximize the same kinds of distance proportionally. Treat
existing Themes as exclusions, not inspiration. Do not assign an object,
material, idea engine, mood, setting, or intensity by theme_id.

Avoid a batch dominated by tiny accessories, fasteners, elastic loops, thin
elongated tools, packaging, polished metal, or objects chosen mainly because
people can grip, press, straddle, rub, or balance on them.

ONE-OBJECT AND MULTI-OBJECT MODES

A single-object Theme gives one familiar object a strong iconic silhouette and
extracts several different consequences from its ordinary behavior.

An ensemble Theme uses two to five objects that plausibly coexist in one real
setting: for example objects from a breakfast table, sewing box, bathroom
counter, desk drawer, laundry shelf, toolbox, picnic blanket, grocery bag,
workbench, bedside table, or another ordinary context. These examples are not a
menu. The objects must act on one another or give the cast different routes,
tools, hazards, resources, social roles, or scale references. Do not scatter
unrelated giant props as decoration.

The miniature cast should read as small inhabitants of a real everyday
environment. They may travel between objects, coordinate actions across them,
use one object to alter another, or exploit the gap, shadow, reflection,
friction, flow, leverage, or ordinary operation created between objects.
External surfaces may become paths or work areas, but every object must retain
its familiar complete identity.

THE ONE-SENTENCE IDEA

Privately reduce each candidate to:

    Because this familiar object or object ensemble is colossal, its truthful
    ordinary behavior or interaction now [causes one impossible visual
    consequence], so the outside cast must [pursue one concrete human desire in
    an arrangement-specific way].

The three indispensable layers are:

1. OBJECT TRUTH — a recognizable use, movement, failure, trace, transformation,
   material behavior, cultural association, or relationship unique to the
   selected object arrangement.
2. HUMAN STAKE — something the cast visibly wants, risks, exchanges, conceals,
   celebrates, repairs, performs, protects, or chooses.
3. CONCEPT ENGINE — the impossible cause-and-effect binding object truth to
   human stake.

The image, not a caption, must reveal all three. If replacing the object
arrangement leaves the idea unchanged, it is generic. If removing the cast
leaves a materials demonstration, it is shallow. If the idea needs readable
words, numbers, dates, screen content, labels, or lore, it is not visually
legible.

CREATIVE-ENGINE SPREAD

Choose primary concept engines freely. Possibilities include an ordinary-use
collision, visual metaphor made literal, impossible product benefit, scale
comedy, object-as-character, collaborative craft, domestic action becoming
weather, forced-perspective illusion, material transaction, role reversal,
public service made from private behavior, elegant practical joke, or an
equally strong invention specific to the object. This is inspiration, not a
menu.

Across a full ten-Theme batch, use at least six substantially different primary
engines. No more than two Themes may center on any rule-governed institution
such as a contest, test, economy, permission system, tribunal, auction,
hierarchy, ritual, or ceremony. No more than two may share one relationship
grammar such as competing for control, earning intimacy, trading
vulnerability, rescuing one another, performing for surveillance, or jointly
operating machinery.

Vary emotional temperature: playful, tender, glamorous, mischievous, deadpan,
absurdly practical, celebratory, inconvenient, seductive, uncanny,
transformative, or other object-specific tones. Do not make every Theme solemn,
adversarial, ceremonial, dangerous, or concerned with proving worth.

CANDIDATE TOURNAMENT

Score candidates privately on:

- instant visual readability without text;
- specificity to the chosen object's ordinary identity;
- semantic distance between object and transformed purpose;
- a concrete two-person stake;
- truthful material cause and effect;
- six genuinely different image opportunities; and
- a final surprise that is more than scale or erotic contact.

Prefer a stronger alternative when a candidate:

- it is merely people posing on, beside, or against a large object;
- six images simply tour six components, textures, or camera angles;
- technical calibration, testing, measuring, maintenance, or operation is the
  idea rather than evidence for it;
- pseudo-science or words such as ritual, ceremony, oracle, sacred, annual,
  equinox, tribunal, governance, or rite disguise an ordinary action;
- the concept is a competition, market, permission system, or intimacy economy
  that could be transferred unchanged to another object;
- random weather, a bird shadow, a passing crane, or an unrelated machine
  supplies the only surprise;
- sensuality carries an otherwise weak proposition; or
- the title sounds inventive but the images do not.

ORDINARY-USE COLLISION

The object must actively do something; it must not remain an abandoned monument
that only the miniature cast operates. In at least two of the six regions, an
outside-scale hand, body, companion object, environmental force, or real
mechanism uses the object in a way instantly recognizable from everyday life.
The miniature cast remains outside and exploits, redirects, collaborates with,
interrupts, or adapts to the resulting force.

Make the intervention object-specific: the outside scale may turn, fold, pour,
wipe, wind, stamp, squeeze, shake, roll, fasten, wear, throw, plug in, inflate,
cut, open, close, or otherwise use the object as designed. At least one
ordinary-use collision must provide the board's strongest visual punchline.
In an ensemble Theme, let ordinary use of one object visibly alter another
object as well as the cast. Do not place the same collision in a fixed region
across Themes.

EXTERIOR-ONLY SCALE CONTRACT

Every person remains on the environment side of every object surface. People
may cross an outer surface, work beside or between objects, handle externally
reachable parts, ride an exterior moving part, redirect an outward flow, or
respond to effects emitted into the surrounding space.

Never place any body inside the object, inside a cavity or container volume,
beneath its shell, behind a cutaway, or across a rim, mouth, seam, hatch,
pocket, sleeve, jaw gap, opening, or threshold leading inward. Never use an
interior viewpoint. For bags, boxes, vessels, garments, appliances, vehicles,
and other enclosing objects, keep the object closed or keep every person
clearly away from the opening on its exterior side. Ordinary use never
authorizes entry.

Do not transform an object interior into a building, room, cave, tunnel, cabin,
or self-contained habitat. Object exteriors and the ordinary environment
between several objects may function as miniature terrain, but they must not
dissolve into an unrecognizable generic landscape. An opened, peeled, broken,
or cut object may expose a material face, but nobody may enter or occupy the
exposed volume.

For a single-object Theme, show its complete or nearly complete outer silhouette
in at least three regions. For an ensemble Theme, show the complete primary
object and at least one clearly recognizable secondary object in at least three
regions. Include familiar external scale references so object identity,
object-to-object relationships, and cast-to-object ratios read immediately.

SIX PROOFS, NOT SIX PARTS

Invent more than six stunts and retain the strongest six. They are six proofs
of one proposition, not a component tour. Each must work as a standalone
campaign key visual while remaining unmistakably part of the same concept.

Across the six, include these functions in any order:

- one iconic whole-object or coherent ensemble image that communicates the idea
  immediately;
- one object-specific ordinary-use collision from outside scale;
- one intimate two-person negotiation with visible reciprocal agency;
- one ingenious use of residue, packaging, reflection, shadow, sound, heat,
  airflow, waste, or another secondary effect;
- one role reversal, perspective reversal, or transfer of power; and
- one payoff that changes how the viewer understands the proposition without
  requiring chronology.

At least four stunts must change the cast's goal, role, tactic, or consequence,
not merely the contacted component, camera, pose, or material state. At least
two must remain compelling if all sensual content is removed.

Avoid letting three or more stunts repeat one interaction grammar: gripping for
leverage, pressing skin to a surface, balancing on a component, routing liquid
over bodies, transferring residue to skin, climbing, reclining, using a surface
as furniture, or reading a measurement.

THEME-STAGE CONTRACT

Use the Theme schema normally. The premise has exactly two sentences:

1. Sentence one identifies the familiar object or coherent object ensemble,
   colossal complete outer forms, the adult cast outside them, the object truth,
   human stake, primary concept engine, and immediate visual cause-and-effect.
2. Sentence two contains exactly six concise visual-stunt seeds separated by
   exactly five semicolons. Each seed states a different proof: cast goal or
   role, concrete outside action, object response, and visible consequence.

Write direct campaign language, not lore. The reader must be able to paraphrase
one surprising object-specific idea and explain why both people care. Put
medium, palette, lighting, material rendering, and visual rhythm in style.

FRAME-STAGE GRID CONTRACT

Every Narrative Frame is one complete portrait 2-column by 3-row grid,
regardless of frames_per_theme. Regions are subdivisions of that one image, not
separate Narrative Frames. Multiple Frames are different complete board
proposals for the same Theme.

Begin every Frame with these exact two prose sentences:

    A portrait 2-column by 3-row grid forms one image with six cleanly separated
    regions. Every person remains outside all colossal everyday objects
    throughout the board.

Then introduce all expanded descriptions in order with the exact prose cues
"Region 1:" through "Region 6:". These cues organize the prompt and must not
appear as rendered text. Put the overview and all six regions in one unbroken
paragraph. Never distribute a board across Frames, use one Frame per region,
continue a region in another Frame, or return a general board synopsis.

Realize every Theme seed without replacement or dilution. In each region,
describe the cast's current goal, role, action, agency, and leverage; exterior
object scales, relationships, and states; visible cause and effect; truthful
deformation,
displacement, spray, tension, fracture, compression, flow, reflection, residue,
or other response; setting; composition; camera; focus; light; and color.

Keep each Frame concise enough to function as one image prompt while giving all
six regions concrete photographic detail. Change visual verb, cast tactic,
social relation, consequence, shot scale, and composition across regions. Do
not expand six seeds into six variations of bracing, pressing, gripping,
balancing, rubbing, climbing, or receiving material on skin.

CAST AND CONTENT LEVEL

Use exactly the CLI-requested number of adult women and adult men. Repeated
views in the grid do not add people. Preserve each person's unmistakable adult
age, face, build, hair, body details, and styling across all six regions and all
Frames of the Theme.

Interpret the proposition through the selected content_level without allowing
content intensity to choose the object or substitute for invention.

- aesthetic: elegant, non-explicit, publication-safe body awareness and
  interaction;
- erotic: unmistakably adult sensual charge, intentional exposure or
  concealment, reciprocal intimacy, and strong physical tension without
  explicit sexual acts;
- hardcore: clearly adult explicit sexual action may be central when requested,
  while anatomy, contact, agency, plausibility, and the high-concept proposition
  remain precise.

Do not sanitize erotic or hardcore into neutral imagery. Do not exceed the
selected level. Every adult remains alert, willing, responsive, and able to
stop.

MINIATURE-WORLD CAMERA LANGUAGE

Photograph every region from the visual logic of a miniature world. The people
must read as tiny real adults living among and interacting with overwhelmingly
large but familiar everyday objects, not as full-size adults beside abstract
architecture.

Use close-focus commercial photography and controlled tilt-shift or macro-style
selective focus where it strengthens scale. Keep the cast, the exact contact
point, and enough recognizable object exteriors sharply resolved while
allowing foreground and background planes to fall away cleanly. Do not blur so
much that faces, actions, material response, or complete object identities
become unreadable.

THUMBNAIL SCALE HIERARCHY

Design every region to read first at thumbnail size: one dominant familiar
object, coherent object ensemble, or unmistakable exterior feature; one or two
visibly tiny complete adult bodies; one clear action silhouette; and one simple
color contrast. The viewer must understand "tiny people living among huge
everyday objects" before noticing erotic detail, facial expression, or material
microtexture.

Across each six-region board:

- use at least four wide or medium views showing complete adult bodies and
  enough object silhouettes, relationships, or iconic geometry to establish
  scale;
- reserve no more than two regions for close detail, and anchor each close view
  with a familiar oversized feature or external reference;
- keep the adults visually subordinate to the object arrangement rather than
  filling the frame like full-size fashion models;
- vary the relative scale dramatically, including one whole-object or ensemble
  hero view where the adults are almost toy-like and one closer interaction
  view where body mechanics remain legible;
- avoid using a broad featureless flank as a wall or backdrop; include an edge,
  curve, handle, seam, button, groove, tooth, marking system, moving part, or
  other instantly identifying geometry; and
- do not let steam, mist, smoke, darkness, reflection, fabric, or shallow focus
  conceal the cast-to-object ratio.

Bold printed patterns, molded marks, repeated ticks, grooves, dots, ridges, and
other graphic surface systems may reinforce scale even when their exact text or
numbers are unreadable. The concept itself must not depend on reading them.

Vary useful miniature viewpoints across the six regions: miniature eye level
along the object's surface, low angles that make an ordinary edge monumental,
high oblique views revealing tiny bodies against the full silhouette, precise
top-down arrangements, and close detail views anchored by a recognizable
large-scale feature. Include ordinary external scale references such as a
fingertip, tabletop grain, fabric weave, floor joint, utensil, packaging edge,
droplet, crumb, or hardware only when they clarify size.

This is crisp editorial advertising photography, not a movie still. Do not use
cinematic color grading, teal-and-orange treatment, crushed blacks, murky
shadows, sepia, film grain, anamorphic flare, dramatic fog, smoky atmosphere,
noir chiaroscuro, or vague blockbuster spectacle.

BRIGHT CLEAN COLOR STANDARD

Use bright, clean, high-key color with clear separation between skin, object,
wardrobe, and background. Favor fresh whites, luminous daylight, polished
pastels, vivid object colors, clean primaries, and deliberate complementary
contrast. Saturation may be bold but must remain premium, natural, and
material-specific rather than neon or synthetic.

Give the object arrangement one immediately legible dominant color relationship
or graphic pattern against a simpler contrasting background. Prefer colored
enamel, dyed plastic, paint, paper, fabric, food color, translucent color, or
another truthful bright finish when objects permit it. For naturally neutral
metal, glass, wood, or white objects, use a bold surrounding color field and
crisp edge light rather than gray-on-gray realism.

Avoid boards dominated by stainless steel gray, beige, black, brown, vapor
white, or low-saturation environmental color. Do not place pale skin, pale
wardrobe, pale object, and pale background in the same tonal band. Give the
cast clothing or accessories that clearly separate their bodies from both the
hero and background while preserving the requested content level.

Light the board like a meticulous commercial tabletop campaign: broad soft
key light, crisp controlled highlights, open shadows with visible detail, and
small contact shadows that pin every miniature body to the surface. Keep
backgrounds uncluttered and color-blocked where useful. Each Theme may choose a
different bright palette, but all six regions in one Frame must belong to the
same clean campaign system.

PHOTOGRAPHIC MATERIAL STANDARD

Render the impossible as premium practical photography with believable gravity,
scale, optics, skin, fibers, particles, reflections, shadows, contact
deformation, exterior depth cues, and material microtexture. Preserve realistic
faces and anatomy even when tilt-shift focus exaggerates scale. Use one coherent
campaign language with enough compositional contrast to keep the grid alive.
Avoid fantasy haze, decorative clutter, cartoon physics, interior cutaways,
generic spectacle, and six near-identical views.

FINAL CREATIVE PRIORITIES

Before returning, use these as creative priorities:

- the batch has real distance in ordinary size, family, silhouette, material,
  primary engine, relationship grammar, emotional tone, and setting;
- the batch includes both single-object and multi-object miniature worlds;
- each Theme has one coherent object arrangement, one human stake, and one
  arrangement-specific concept engine;
- one or more objects actively undergo recognizable ordinary use in at least
  two regions;
- every person stays outside every object and no object interior becomes an
  inhabitable world;
- no concept depends on readable text or explanatory lore;
- every region passes the thumbnail read of giant familiar object, tiny complete
  people, clear action, and clean color separation;
- at least four regions show complete bodies with strong scale evidence, while
  close views remain anchored by iconic object geometry;
- the premise has exactly two sentences and sentence two has six seeds divided
  by exactly five semicolons;
- the six stunts are different conceptual proofs rather than six parts or
  contact poses;
- every Frame independently contains the complete text-free 2-by-3 grid,
  exact opening, and Region 1 through Region 6; and
- every region uses miniature-world scale cues, bright clean commercial color,
  readable contact, and no cinematic treatment; and
- cast, consent, content level, physical plausibility, and photographic quality
  are exact.

These priorities guide selection and revision; they are not a hard quality gate
and must not block an otherwise structurally valid result. The non-negotiable
grid, cast, exterior-only, content-level, and schema contracts remain mandatory.
