BRIEF

Create varied, award-caliber, hyper-realistic six-panel campaign concept boards.
Each Theme must be a fresh visual invention, not a variation on a prescribed
subject. Every board contains one recurring miniature adult cast and one
familiar everyday object enlarged to colossal scale. The cast always remains
outside that object and uses its recognizable exterior properties to make six
impossible but instantly legible images possible.

This is explicitly a non-narrative high-concept campaign board. Conceptual
invention replaces conventional plot, relationship conflict, or a person merely
exploring a setting. The colossal object remains a complete freestanding object,
not architecture, a habitable interior, or a miniature world.

OPEN SUBJECT SELECTION

Choose one familiar everyday physical object freely and unpredictably for each
Theme. It must be recognizable from ordinary human life before enlargement:
for example a household item, food, tool, garment, accessory, package, desk
object, grooming item, toy, sport object, or similarly common thing. These
examples are open-ended, not a menu. Do not assign categories, moods, objects,
materials, or intensities by theme_id. Theme IDs identify outputs only.

Across requested Themes, maximize distance in subject, world logic, material
behavior, human activity, palette, and visual rhythm. Do not choose several
subjects that all become caves, forests, mazes, baths, beds, shelters, or
landscapes.

THE CONCEPT EQUATION

Build each concept with this equation:

    familiar everyday object enlarged to colossal scale
    + distant system or ritual + truthful exterior material mechanism
    = one surprising campaign proposition

The distant system or ritual must change what the object's exterior does and
what the outside cast can do with it. Scale by itself is not an idea.

The hero remains one complete, recognizable object rather than dissolving into
an environment. Build an external hero system from several of its real surface
features, attached components, operating states, detachable parts, byproducts,
packaging elements, or outward effects. They may acquire useful new roles, but
the object's familiar outer silhouette, construction, scale, and original
identity must remain legible.

A strong proposition completes this sentence with concrete, visible nouns and
verbs:

    This is not merely a giant [everyday object]. Its external [real property
    or component] becomes [unexpected function], so the miniature cast outside
    it can [specific desire, ritual, service, game, contradiction, or
    transformation].

Reject the proposition if the final clause is only "explore it," "feel it,"
"connect with it," "be immersed in it," or another vague sensory treatment.

EXTERIOR-ONLY SCALE CONTRACT

Every person remains physically outside the colossal object in every Theme,
every Narrative Frame, and every grid region. People may work beside it, cross
or brace against its outer surface, handle an externally reachable component,
ride an exterior moving part, redirect an outward flow, or respond to an effect
emitted into the surrounding space.

Never place a person inside the object, inside one of its cavities, beneath its
outer shell, within a container volume, behind a cutaway wall, or across an
opening, rim, seam, hatch, mouth, pocket, sleeve, or other threshold leading
into it. Never use an interior viewpoint. An opened, split, peeled, or cut
object may expose a surface to the outside, but every person must remain on the
environment side of that exposed surface without entering the object's volume.

Do not turn the object into a building, room, cave, tunnel, vehicle cabin,
arena, landscape, planet, or self-contained habitat. Surround it with a
credible external setting and familiar scale references. In at least three
regions, show enough of the complete or nearly complete outer silhouette for
the original everyday object and the cast-to-object scale ratio to read
immediately.

SECOND-ORDER LEAP

Before writing, generate multiple unrelated candidates and select the one with
the clearest second-order leap. The final idea must combine at least three
compatible creative operations, chosen for the selected subject rather than by
quota:

- function transfer: an exterior component becomes a transport path, wearable,
  outdoor furnishing, exposed machine, service tool, stage device, sport
  apparatus, or another unexpected working role;
- state drama: different physical states create different actions and images;
- miniature logistics: the outside cast builds a working tool, route, rig,
  workshop, delivery system, or public service around or across the exterior;
- scale crossing: a hand, tool, flow, mechanism, shadow, or force from another
  scale intervenes from outside and changes the visible interaction;
- character shift: the complete object or an external component acts as host,
  companion, rival, audience, performer, guide, or creature without turning
  cartoonish;
- material weather: a truthful byproduct travels outward and becomes climate,
  light, atmosphere, precipitation, or environmental force around the object;
- ritual or game: an ordinary use becomes a ceremony, courtship rule, sport,
  feast, performance, challenge, or elegant practical joke;
- hero reveal: exterior components resolve into the iconic full silhouette,
  service moment, presentation, or other visible payoff.

These are open creative operations, not Theme assignments or fixed panel roles.
Invent other operations when they serve the proposition better.

THE SIX-STUNT TEST

Invent more than six visual stunts, then retain the six strongest. Every retained
stunt must:

- work as a standalone campaign key visual;
- use a different exterior role, state, process, component, scale, or physical
  force;
- show the adult cast doing something necessary outside the object;
- contain clear contact, force, response, and material consequence when bodies
  or tools interact with the object's exterior;
- remain understandable without a caption; and
- belong unmistakably to the same complete everyday object.

Most stunts must change function or state, not merely change camera angle or
body pose. At least one stunt must contain a category-breaking event such as a
working miniature system, an outside-scale intervention, material weather, a
component with a social role, or an equally strong external invention arising
naturally from the object.

HARD REJECTION GATE

Discard and replace the entire Theme if any stunt puts a person inside the
object or treats its interior, cavity, opening, or hollow volume as a location.
Also discard it if the object stops reading as one familiar enlarged object and
becomes generic architecture, terrain, or an immersive micro-world.

Discard and replace the entire Theme when four or more stunts can be summarized
with passive bodily verbs such as walk through, stand beside, gaze at, touch,
stroke, lean on, lie on, bathe in, embrace, or climb.

The following are beginnings, not finished concepts:

- repeated long things becoming a forest;
- lines or fabric becoming a maze;
- liquid becoming a bath;
- a curved surface becoming a bed or slide;
- a mushroom, shell, leaf, or cloth becoming shelter;
- a large device merely being operated at scale.

One of these forms may appear only if another idea overturns it through changed
function, ritual, law, social meaning, logistics, or scale. Sensual photography
cannot compensate for a weak proposition.

THEME-STAGE OUTPUT CONTRACT

Use the Theme schema normally, but make the two-sentence premise carry an
auditable creative plan:

1. Sentence one names the familiar everyday object, its colossal complete outer
   form, the cast outside it, the distant system or ritual, and the external
   physical mechanism that creates the second-order conceptual leap.
2. Sentence two contains exactly six concise visual-stunt seeds separated by
   semicolons. Each seed names the transformed exterior component or state, its
   new function, and a concrete cast action outside the object.

Do not spend the premise on generic mood, intimacy, exploration, or backstory.
The style field carries medium, palette, light, material rendering, and visual
rhythm. The six seeds may use any order; no panel position has a permanently
assigned narrative function.

FRAME-STAGE OUTPUT CONTRACT

Every Narrative Frame is one complete 2-column by 3-row grid, regardless of
frames_per_theme. It is one portrait image containing six cleanly separated
regions with consistent gutters and no visible title, caption, label,
typography, logo, watermark, border text, or interface element. Regions are
spatial subdivisions inside that one image; they are not Narrative Frames.

When multiple Frames are requested, every Frame must independently redescribe a
different complete six-region board for the same Theme. Never distribute one
board across multiple Frames, never use one Frame per region, and never
continue a board or region in the next Frame. Put the board overview and all six
region descriptions inside each Frame's single unbroken prose paragraph.

Realize all six stunt seeds from the Theme premise, one per region. Do not
replace, merge, or dilute them. Explicitly describe Region 1 through Region 6;
a general board synopsis without six expanded region descriptions is invalid.
Each complete Narrative Frame should use roughly 450 to 650 words, or equivalent
detail in the requested language. Expand each seed into a photographic scene
with:

- the recurring cast's exact action and leverage;
- explicit evidence that the cast remains outside the object;
- the external component's scale, role, and physical state;
- visible cause and effect;
- tactile deformation, displacement, spray, tension, fracture, compression,
  flow, reflection, residue, or another truthful material response;
- composition, camera distance and angle, lens character, focus, lighting, and
  color; and
- enough surrounding context to make both scale and concept immediately clear.

The six regions form a flexible visual rhythm, not six equal poses and not a
mandatory beginning-middle-climax sequence. Any region may be quiet, comic,
intimate, kinetic, monumental, or revelatory when the chosen concept calls for
it.

CAST AND CONTINUITY

Use exactly the requested number of adult women and adult men. All depicted
people are clearly adults. The same cast recurs across the six regions; repeated
views do not add people. If multiple Narrative Frames are requested, that same
cast also recurs in every complete board. Give each person a stable compact
identity: apparent age, ancestry or complexion, body build, face structure, and
hair.

Preserve identity, body details, styling logic, and any transformed wardrobe or
body marks across all regions unless the proposition visibly changes them.
Supporting tools must be built from or materially linked to the hero ecology,
not added as arbitrary spectacle.

CONTENT LEVEL

Interpret the chosen concept specifically through the requested content_level.
Do not let content intensity choose or limit the subject.

- aesthetic: elegant, non-explicit, publication-safe body awareness and
  interaction;
- erotic: clearly adult sensual charge, intentional exposure or concealment,
  intimate self-touch where conceptually useful, and strong physical tension
  without explicit sexual acts;
- hardcore: clearly adult explicit sexual action may be central when requested,
  while anatomy, contact, agency, physical plausibility, and the high-concept
  campaign proposition remain precise.

Do not sanitize erotic or hardcore into neutral wellness imagery. Do not add
sexual content beyond the selected level.

PHOTOGRAPHIC STANDARD

Render the impossible as premium practical photography: believable gravity,
scale, optics, material microtexture, skin texture, moisture, fibers, particles,
reflections, shadows, contact deformation, exterior depth cues, and a readable
cast-to-object size ratio. Favor one controlled visual language with enough
contrast between regions to keep the grid alive. Avoid generic fantasy haze,
decorative clutter, cartoon physics, interior cutaways, empty spectacle, and six
near-identical camera views.

FINAL SILENT CHECK

Before returning output, verify:

- the subject was freely invented and not inferred from theme_id;
- the subject is one familiar everyday object enlarged to colossal scale;
- every person remains outside it in all six regions;
- at least three regions reveal its complete or nearly complete outer silhouette;
- the premise follows the exact two-sentence contract;
- sentence two contains six semicolon-separated, visibly different stunts;
- at least three creative operations combine into one coherent proposition;
- the object appears through multiple truthful exterior components, states, or
  effects without becoming an interior world;
- most cast actions are active and consequential rather than passive contact;
- all six stunt seeds are realized in the Frame;
- every Narrative Frame is independently one complete six-region grid;
- the requested cast count and content level are exact; and
- the final grid contains no rendered text.

If any check fails, revise before returning.
