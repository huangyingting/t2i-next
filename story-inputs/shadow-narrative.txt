BRIEF

Create cinematic shadow-narrative Themes in which the requested adult cast's
ordinary present action casts a second concrete event onto a receiving surface.
The shadow event must arise from real blockers and one light source, not from
supernatural autonomous silhouettes.

WOMAN CAST GATE

Every Theme and every Frame must include at least one unmistakably adult woman
as the clearly visible real-world shadow caster. Give her a specific adult age,
stable appearance, grounded body, position, gaze, and useful present action.
Her shadow alone, a portrait, reflection, silhouette without a visible source
body, or off-camera caster does not count. Additional CLI-requested adults may
appear, but she must remain integral to both readings. This brief is incompatible
with a zero-woman cast.

DUAL READING

For every Theme invent a new relationship between object-level action and shadow
event: agreement versus doubt, repair versus fracture, arrival versus departure,
protection versus exposure, or another visually specific contrast. Arrange the
cast and one principal prop with straight rigid edges so their combined shadows
form one simple triangle, diamond, or sail from one exact light position. Use a
small floor spotlight below waist height, fixed behind and to one side of the
cast so the source remains visible, a light-toned vertical wall in front of the
cast, and an eye-level camera
at a side three-quarter angle that visibly includes source, blockers, and wall.
The receiving surface must be light-toned and at least
four exposure stops brighter than the cast shadows; never use blackboards, dark
walls, patterned surfaces, or transparent screens.

THEME CONTRACT

The cast must never touch, adjust, or operate the light source.

The premise names country, exact site, adult cast, real action, the sole practical source,
receiving surface, resolved shadow event, and the meaning created by their
contrast. The style sentence defines contrast ratio, palette, medium, surface texture,
and an eye-level side three-quarter camera axis.

FRAME CONTRACT

Each Frame shows cast, principal prop, source logic, and complete simple shadow
on one continuous wall. Keep one dominant shadow-casting practical; weak ambient
room light may reveal the source and cast but must not create a competing shadow.
Describe every person's real pose and support independently, then identify the
body and prop contours responsible for the shadow. Keep
the actual cast recognizable and the shadow event readable at the same time.

All shadows share perspective and direction appropriate to the named sole finite
source, whose unobstructed position relative to cast and receiving surface must
be explicit. Do not use low-angle, overhead, frontal, or wall-parallel cameras.
Contact shadows attach at feet and props; distance softens edges plausibly.
Shadows may merge into the simple shape but may not contain details unsupported by
blockers. No projected images, cutout puppets hidden off-camera, duplicated
people, extra shadow figures, text, or multiple suns.

Reject generic long shadows, silhouettes merely repeating bodies, or a secondary
event that exists only because the prose names it.
