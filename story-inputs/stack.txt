THEME AND FRAME CONTRACT

Every Theme must define one complete portrait system:
- one original collective geometric proposition;
- one private closed studio, stage, or constructed interior;
- one coherent wardrobe or artistic-nudity logic;
- one safe support method;
- one dominant camera relationship;
- one palette and motivated lighting system; and
- one playful, tender, theatrical, or commanding group dynamic.

Give each Theme one concise title. Its premise must describe the whole cast,
current geometric arrangement, visible support logic, and why the arrangement
is visually surprising in no more than two sentences. Its style must be one
flowing sentence defining composition, lens behavior, crop, palette, light,
surface rendering, and finish.

Frames within a Theme are parallel alternatives of the same broad visual
proposition, not chronological steps. No pose, garment state, crop, prop
position, or support state carries between Frames. Every Frame must stand alone,
fully redescribe the exact cast and arrangement, and use a different compatible
camera strategy, silhouette rhythm, or expression pattern.

Write each final Frame as one unbroken natural-language paragraph in the
requested language. Do not output a screenplay, shot list, tag list,
instructional explanation, visible title, caption, label, logo, watermark, or
interface element.

SAFE STRUCTURAL ILLUSION GATE

The cast may appear stacked, tiered, suspended, or interlocked, but no adult may
bear another adult's weight on the head, neck, spine, ribs, abdomen, pelvis,
lower back, or unsupported joints. Do not place knees, feet, elbows, or hard
props on another person's back or torso. Do not use human bodies as steps,
platforms, bridges, stools, or load-bearing architecture.

Build elevated or impossible-looking arrangements with visible safe engineering:
transparent acrylic tiers, hidden-height padded plinths, broad upholstered
platforms, floor-mounted posing blocks, overhead body-safe harnesses, individual
waist supports, rigid scenic armatures kept outside body paths, or compositing
that gives every adult independent support. Support hardware must have soft
edges, broad contact, quick releases, and no pressure on the neck or chest.

Every adult must support only their own body or be independently supported.
Hands may touch shoulders, arms, waists, outer hips, or nearby safe structures
for expressive connection, but must not pull hair, compress airways, pin joints,
or create painful leverage. Reject and rewrite any arrangement that depends on
unexplained balance, dangerous compression, hidden crushing force, or impossible
human strength.

CAST COUNT AND ANATOMY GATE

Every requested adult must be visibly present exactly once in the image. Make
the full cast count auditable through separate heads, hairstyles, faces or
clearly traceable profiles, torsos, and limb paths. Cropping may hide part of a
person, but it must not make cast count or ownership ambiguous.

Show ordinary anatomy, natural joints, and correct digits. Give every visible
hand, foot, and limb one compatible task. Keep each limb traceable from shoulder
or hip to its endpoint. Do not create detached anatomy, merged torsos, shared
limbs, extra hands, repeated heads, duplicated bodies, impossible bends, or
cross-person skin continuity.

When bodies overlap, separate them with distinct hair, skin tone variation,
garment color, edge light, focus, pose direction, or a visible gap. Keep every
recognizable body fragment attached unambiguously to its owner.
