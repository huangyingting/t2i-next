BRIEF

Create tense but nonviolent evidence-table Themes built around the requested
adult cast arranging ordinary objects to solve a concrete visual question. The
table is a physical reasoning surface, not a crime cliché, conspiracy wall, or
forensic gore scene.

WOMAN CAST GATE

Every Theme and every Frame must include at least one unmistakably adult woman
actively testing the evidence arrangement. Give her a specific adult age,
stable appearance, clearly owned hands, position, gaze, and necessary reasoning
action. A silhouette, portrait, photograph, reflection, or off-camera analyst
does not count. Additional CLI-requested adults may collaborate, but she must
remain visually central to the test. This brief is incompatible with a
zero-woman cast.

EVIDENCE DESIGN

Choose a different benign inquiry for each Theme: restoring an object, tracing a
journey, identifying a craft process, comparing weather damage, reconstructing a
meal, planning a conservation action, or another materially grounded puzzle.
Use a small, readable set of evidence items from varied material families. Every
item must contribute a different inference; omit anything merely decorative.

THEME CONTRACT

The premise states country, exact room, adult cast, the question, and the current
interpretation being tested. Name the most decisive two
objects; leave the remaining supporting objects to the Frame.
The style sentence defines overhead or oblique photography, surface palette,
light, and tactile detail.

FRAME CONTRACT

Each Frame shows one complete arrangement on a single table and the entire
requested cast actively interpreting it. Include the two decisive objects from
the premise and only as many supporting items as remain legible. Name their positions,
the spatial grouping that expresses the current hypothesis, and one cast action
that changes or tests a relationship. Hands may enter the evidence area only
when their owner and task remain unambiguous.

Use physical adjacency, overlap, orientation, scale, residue, wear, matching
edges, or material transfer as the logic. Do not use strings, pins, labels,
captions, digital interfaces, police tape, weapons, blood, fingerprints, or
anonymous photographs of people. Any visible writing must be indispensable,
brief, explicitly quoted, and attached to a named object.

Reject decorative flat lays, interchangeable curios, mysteries explained
only by prose, or scenes where the cast merely looks down without testing the
arrangement.
