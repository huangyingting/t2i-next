ONE NECESSARY MOTION SYSTEM

Choose exactly one motion mode and one carrier system per Theme. Natural fit
outranks variety; repeating a credible mode is better than forcing another.
Privately complete: "Because this visible current activity is happening, this
carrier must move this way." If deleting the carrier leaves the activity
unchanged, it is decorative; rewrite the premise.

Do not combine carrier classes. No crowd plus rain; no train plus flying
garment; no garment plus liquid; no pan plus independently moving crowd; no
water plus flying cloth; no moving practical lights added to another carrier.
Hair, hems, droplets, and reflections count with the carrier only when they are
direct physical or optical consequences of that same event.

SCENE AND MECHANICS

Close two physical loops:

- Scene loop: location, operating state, time, weather, population, clothing,
  props, and activity commonly coexist. A closed room stays dry; rain outside
  glass stays outside; wind-blown rain reaches a covered floor only through a
  visible opening and leaves matching wetness.
- Mechanics loop: force has a visible source; mass suits the force; limbs
  reach through normal joints; weight has enough support; released objects
  start at the releasing hand and follow gravity after the apex; paths do not
  cross faces or decisive contact.

Do not call walking, cleanup, conversation, equipment handling, or garment
adjustment sharp during a long exposure. Those adults either hold one explicit
still pose or their movement is the selected carrier.

CAMERA, EXPOSURE, AND RECORDED RESULT

Close the imaging loop: camera support or pan, focal length, camera distance,
aperture, focus distance, shutter time, flash duration, curtain timing,
ambient-to-flash ratio, carrier speed, and light placement must produce the
claimed final sharpness and blur.

Use plausible ranges:

- panned subject: about 1/15 to 1/4 second;
- flash-frozen action plus trail: about 1/15 to 1/2 second;
- still anchor with moving world: about 1/4 to 1 second.

Longer exposure requires an unusually slow carrier and a pose that can truly
remain still. Estimate displacement from speed and shutter time. Walking for
one second covers about one to one-and-a-half meters. Do not pair two seconds
with a three-centimeter trail or 1/30 second with a meter-long slow-walking
smear without a valid distance and speed explanation.

A tripod prevents camera shake; it does not freeze people. At shutter times
slower than 1/4 second, do not call faces critically sharp merely because the
camera is locked. Use a short flash with subdued ambient exposure on people,
shorten the shutter, or allow slight human motion.
At Hardcore level, a still-anchor shutter slower than 1/4 second must use a
short flash on every primary adult with continuous ambient on them at least
three stops lower. Without that flash, cap the shutter at 1/4 second and do not
claim pore-level or critically sharp human detail.

Shutter time and flash duration are different. Shutter controls ambient
accumulation. Flash duration freezes the flash-lit instant. For fast cloth,
droplets, hair, or limbs, use a plausible t.1 flash duration around 1/2000 to
1/10000 second. Never call 1/200 or 1/250 second a flash pulse.

Flash freezes only surfaces it illuminates strongly. If a moving primary adult
must appear as one crisp flash image during a slow shutter, continuous ambient
exposure on their skin and clothing stays at least three stops below flash
exposure; otherwise a ghost or smear must appear. Do not place a moving cast in
bright sun, skylight, or strong practical light and claim flash alone removes
ambient ghosting. Camera flash cannot freeze distant rain or crowds beyond its
useful range; light a distant carrier locally or record it as ambient blur.

First-curtain flash puts the crisp image at the beginning of a trail that
continues in the travel direction. Rear-curtain flash puts the crisp image at
the end of the preceding trail. Use one sharp position, not sharp copies at
both ends. Match release point, trail, frozen position, direction, and
destination to curtain timing.

For fixed-camera modes, architecture and stationary props remain sharp. Blur
belongs only to the selected carrier and its direct reflection. A stationary
lamp reflected on a stationary floor does not streak merely because the
shutter is slow. Depth softness, bokeh, fog, bloom, and shallow focus are not
motion blur.

For panning, the camera is not locked or fixed. The tracked subject translates
in the pan direction; stationary surroundings streak opposite it. Do not pan,
zoom, rotate, shake, and orbit together.

Claim sharpness only inside plausible depth of field for the stated lens,
aperture, focus distance, subject spacing, and camera distance. Do not call
near eyes, a distant crowd, and far architecture all critically sharp at a
wide aperture.
Describe displacement as a percentage of frame width or height, or in pixels
for a declared output; never claim a trail is centimeters long on a full-frame
sensor whose entire width is about 36 millimeters.
