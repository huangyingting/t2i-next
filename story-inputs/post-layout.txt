BRIEF

Create varied, copy-ready image prompts for finished portrait poster designs in the visual language of a tactile mid-century cinematic photomontage. Each result may promote a fictional film, person, product, event, service, or cultural subject, but it must transform that subject into one resolved analog collage poster rather than a clean advertisement, moodboard, storyboard, process sheet, or collection of alternatives.

Use Subject, Scene, Composition, Style, Text, Details, and Output as seven internal planning concerns, not as a literal response format. Cover all seven concerns in that conceptual order while integrating them into one compact, natural, directly imageable prompt. Do not print those concern names, field labels, prefixes, headings, a template, planning notes, explanations, or optional branches.

Every readable word inside the generated image must be grammatical English written with ASCII Latin letters. This includes the headline, subtitle, labels, credits, quotations, dates, badges, annotations, newspapers, documents, tickets, packaging, signs, clothing, film strips, and environmental graphics. The descriptive prompt follows the CLI output language, but that language must never leak into the rendered image.

ENGLISH-ONLY IMAGE TEXT GATE

The dedicated image-text passage inside the natural prose is the sole source of visible image copy. For every visible string, first state its exact physical carrier and location in the poster, then write the string exactly as it must appear between literal ASCII straight double-quote characters `"`, never curly quotation marks. A quoted string without a concrete location is invalid. Outside that passage, use no quoted strings and do not spell out, repeat, translate, or paraphrase image copy; refer only to generic roles such as the headline, subtitle, or credit block.

Use only unaccented English letters A-Z and a-z, Arabic numerals 0-9, spaces, and standard ASCII punctuation in visible image copy. Render zero Chinese characters, Japanese kana, Korean hangul, Cyrillic, accented Latin letters, non-English scripts, pseudo-letters, lorem ipsum, or invented glyphs.

Chinese people, places, eras, and objects do not authorize Chinese writing in the image. Do not place a Chinese name, title, slogan, handwriting sample, newspaper masthead, factory badge, archive label, street sign, or document wording in quotation marks anywhere in the descriptive prompt. When an authentic object would normally contain local-language text, either leave its printed area blank, crop it out, turn it away, reduce it to non-readable paper texture, or replace it with short exact English copy already quoted with its location in the image-text passage.

Newspaper and archival fragments must not contain dense generated paragraphs. Use blank column texture and halftone rules, or only short exact English snippets quoted with their locations in the image-text passage. If a word is not explicitly quoted in that passage, it must not be readable anywhere in the image.

EXAMPLES ARE OPEN-ENDED

Examples below are inspiration, not presets, quotas, or a fixed sequence. Combine compatible ideas or invent an equivalent. Only statements using "must," "every," "exact," or "do not" are mandatory.

REFERENCE EFFECT AND POSTER DNA

Every Frame must visibly belong to one analog editorial poster family:

- one dominant monochrome photographic hero whose crop is wide enough to carry the selected content-level subject, relationship, and defining physical interaction rather than reducing them to a face alone;
- two to four smaller documentary, environmental, object, silhouette, or film-strip image fragments that enrich the subject without competing with the hero;
- one oversized condensed headline assembled on a torn paper slab and integrated into the middle or lower visual hierarchy;
- a few short English editorial fragments, credits, quotation blocks, date or venue details, or review-like pull quotes arranged as authentic print matter, each quoted verbatim with its exact physical location;
- overlapping torn paper with irregular deckled edges, pasted seams, slight curl, glue shadows, and visible layer depth;
- warm aged cream paper, dense black ink, and a restrained set of one to three saturated spot colors such as vermilion, cobalt, or mustard;
- halftone dots, photocopy grain, coarse newsprint, ink spread, slight registration error, fold wear, scuffs, and occasional film perforations; and
- an asymmetric but controlled reading path that feels assembled by a decisive print designer, not scattered randomly.

The reference establishes this design grammar only. Do not reproduce its people, title, quotations, language, film, exact placements, or recognizable artwork. Invent original content for every Theme.

CAST AND ADULTHOOD

When people are requested, use the exact CLI-requested human cast and no additional people. Every depicted person must be an unmistakably mature Chinese adult aged 25 or older, alert, willing, responsive, and able to stop. Preserve identity, age, face, hair, body proportions, garments, and other anchors throughout a Theme.

CONTENT-LEVEL VISIBILITY CONTRACT

Apply only the branch matching the CLI-selected content level. The selected
branch is a hard visual contract for the photographic content, not a mood,
theme name, text tone, wardrobe suggestion, or optional supporting detail.

AESTHETIC

At aesthetic level, the dominant hero photograph must remain unmistakably
non-explicit while directly showing the Theme's primary subject and action.
Use conventional coverage and non-sexual interaction unless the source story
explicitly requires adult nudity or intimacy; when it does, keep the treatment
non-explicit and subordinate to the stated subject. Do not add explicit sexual
activity merely because the collage language is provocative.

EROTIC

At erotic level, every Frame must make non-explicit adult intimacy
unmistakably visible in the dominant hero photograph. Show adult nudity,
intimate contact, or unmistakable bodily tension as the current physical
subject rather than an ordinary clothed portrait, separate posing,
preparation, suggestive typography, or atmosphere alone. With multiple
participants, keep every requested adult visibly willing, responsive, and
engaged through gaze, posture, or active contact while keeping genital contact
and explicit sexual activity out of view.

HARDCORE

At hardcore level, every Frame must place the direct explicit adult interaction
in the dominant hero photograph. Show the interaction already occurring, name
the involved anatomy and exact current contact, and keep every requested adult
visibly active, willing, responsive, and able to stop. Do not substitute a
portrait, nudity alone, separate bodies, preparation, alignment, implication,
text, or a tiny secondary fragment.

For erotic and hardcore Frames, at least one smaller photographic fragment
must reinforce the same selected-level interaction through a different
meaningful crop or viewpoint without inventing a second event. Every other
fragment containing a participant must preserve the same identity, clothing
state, relationship, and current interaction context; it must not contradict
the dominant hero with a neutral or fully disconnected depiction.

Typography, quoted copy, nudity by itself, facial expression, color, and a
minor supporting fragment cannot satisfy the selected content level. No paper
slab, photograph crop, torn edge, headline, shadow, or overlapping fragment may
hide the defining contact, required anatomy, active participation, or
content-level visibility anchor. Preserve enough facial and bodily information
to show adulthood, identity, willingness, and response at the same time.

THEMES AND FRAMES

Generate exactly the Theme count and Frames-per-Theme count requested by the CLI. Each Theme establishes one design problem: a specific subject, purpose, context, exact headline, palette family, and visual identity.

Use the Theme schema deliberately: `title` is an original grammatical English headline of one to six words that every Frame must reproduce exactly; `premise` defines the shared subject, context, and one content-level visibility anchor that states the required current interaction or non-explicit presentation for every Frame; `style` locks the palette family, material language, and visual identity in one concise sentence. Do not translate the title. Never replace the visibility anchor with a mood, slogan, future action, preparation, or typography.

Across Themes, vary subject category, location, audience, emotional temperature, era, material language, palette, and layout potential. Before returning a Theme batch, compare every pair and rewrite any pair that shares the same premise family, setting function, era, or style system. Do not produce several campaigns for one fictional story.

Frames are parallel finished poster proposals for the same Theme, never chronological stages. Preserve the Theme's exact subject, headline, content-level visibility anchor, spot-color family, print material language, and identity anchors. Keep a portrait poster format while giving each Frame a substantially different collage architecture, hero crop, headline geometry, fragment placement, overlap rhythm, focal hierarchy, and image-text relationship. Changing only color, camera angle, or decoration does not count as a new layout. Restate each Frame independently; do not use "same," "still," "again," or another continuity shortcut.

During Theme generation, return only the shared design concept, its mandatory
content-level visibility anchor, and one concise style sentence. Do not include
Frame drafts, field templates, count values, alternate layouts, or
instructions for later stages.

SEVEN-PART CONTENT STRUCTURE

Before drafting the final prose, organize the visible design through these seven
concerns in this conceptual order. Their names are internal authoring cues and
must not appear as labels, headings, or prefixes in the response:

1. Establish the primary subject, defining appearance, selected content-level
   visibility anchor, current action or function, and the one feature that
   must dominate recognition.
2. Establish the concrete scene, intended audience, use case, and narrative or
   informational context.
3. Define crop or camera distance, collage architecture, focal hierarchy,
   placement, reading path, and protected negative space without restating an
   aspect ratio.
4. Define one coherent visual language through material, lighting, color, era,
   texture, and editorial tone.
5. Place the exact English Theme title and every other visible string on a
   specific physical paper layer or object in a specific poster zone, then
   write each string verbatim between literal ASCII straight double quotes.
   If no supporting copy is needed, include only the title. Nothing outside
   this image-text passage may quote, repeat, or add visible copy.
6. Add only the few supporting objects, callouts, paper layers, or decorative
   elements that strengthen the main idea.
7. Finish with image quality, edge treatment, text fidelity, print finish, and
   the final readability requirement without specifying output dimensions or
   an aspect ratio.

Write one compact, fluent paragraph rather than seven labeled lines. Let the
seven concerns shape the paragraph's information flow without exposing the
template. Do not leave brackets, placeholders, implementation notes, or
multiple-choice wording in the final prompt. Apart from quoted English image
copy, the prose must remain entirely in the requested output language.

COLLAGE LAYOUT AND FOCAL HIERARCHY

Build each Frame as a portrait 4:5 poster around one dominant collage architecture. Vary the architecture freely: the hero may be top-heavy, centrally cropped, pushed to an edge, interrupted by a headline slab, revealed through a paper tear, or crossed by a diagonal band; supporting fragments may anchor corners, form a film strip, create a documentary base, or counterweight the headline. These are compositional moves, not fixed slots.

The dominant hero photograph should command roughly half the poster without becoming a clean full-bleed portrait. Use one primary focal point, one forceful headline zone, subordinate image fragments, and a clear reading path. Let tears and overlaps create deliberate occlusion only outside the selected content-level visibility anchor. Preserve the defining face, gesture, physical contact, required anatomy, product feature, or event clue. When the selected level depends on interaction, use a sufficiently wide crop to show every participant's active role and the complete defining contact in one immediately readable fragment. Make the hero and title legible at thumbnail size.

Deliberate collage is mandatory, but it must resolve into one poster proposition. Repeated photographic crops of the same protagonist are permitted as printed source fragments and do not add cast members. Do not present alternate layouts inside the poster, use six equal tiles, distribute fragments with equal visual weight, float elements without paper support, or place the headline as a clean digital overlay.

TEXT AND GRAPHIC DISCIPLINE

Treat text as physical printed matter inside the collage, not as a caption added afterward. Lock the exact English headline across a Theme. Keep supporting copy minimal, purposeful, fully specified, and English-only; never invite the model to invent filler paragraphs. For every quoted string, identify whether it is printed on a headline slab, pasted scrap, ticket, photograph edge, credit strip, badge, sign, or another concrete carrier and state its exact poster location. Text may support the selected content level but must never carry or replace its required physical evidence. Repeat no visible string outside the dedicated image-text passage and add no environmental copy by implication. Prohibit Chinese or other writing systems, gibberish, pseudo-letters, fake logos, watermarks, lorem ipsum, and unreadable microtext.

Specify type scale, weight, alignment, spacing, contrast, physical paper substrate, and relation to adjacent tears and photographs without naming a copyrighted identity. The headline must use oversized condensed block lettering with convincing ink texture; secondary copy may use compact grotesque, slab-serif, or typewriter-like print. Keep the headline immediately readable and use no more than two additional text levels.

STYLE AND MATERIAL COHERENCE

Keep every Theme inside the analog cinematic photomontage family while deriving its specific edition from the subject, audience, and purpose. Choose a restrained cream-black foundation, one to three spot colors, one dominant paper stock relationship, one photographic treatment, and one print-era attitude. Avoid generic terms such as "premium," "cinematic," "modern," or "luxury" unless visible choices define them.

Vary the treatment through documentary newsprint, screen-printed color blocking, letterpress severity, archival cinema ephemera, photocopied counterculture, contact-sheet photography, or another compatible analog print direction. Do not drift into clean corporate minimalism, glossy 3D advertising, polished interface design, generic digital gradients, or unrelated futuristic graphics. Do not imitate named artists, existing campaigns, copyrighted characters, or recognizable brands.

Keep every artifact physically located: halftone belongs to reproduced photography, ink spread to printed letters, registration error to spot-color layers, fold wear to exposed paper ridges, glue shadows to pasted edges, and film perforations to photographic strips. Do not apply one uniform vintage filter over the entire image or stack unrelated damage merely to create age.

PHYSICS AND RENDERABILITY

Treat every Frame as one final physical composite assembled from distinct printed photographs, paper shapes, and type elements. Within each photographic fragment, give each person or object one coherent location, scale, orientation, support, and compatible function. Keep anatomy natural, product geometry intact, print-layer perspective consistent, pasted-edge shadows plausible, and repeated crops recognizably tied to the same source identity.

Protect all required text with adequate contrast and uncluttered space. Do not place important copy across complex faces, hands, anatomy, reflective highlights, deep folds, or perspective surfaces unless the surrounding composition description explicitly makes that integration legible.

OUTPUT

Write one compact, fluent paragraph. Target 450–650 Chinese characters and never exceed 800; target 220–320 English words and never exceed 400. Every phrase must control visible content, collage hierarchy, exact text, paper layer, print artifact, or finish.

Before returning, verify that the prose is one natural paragraph with no seven-part labels or prefixes; all seven content concerns are covered in their conceptual order; only the CLI-selected content-level branch was applied; the Theme's content-level visibility anchor appears directly in the dominant hero photograph rather than in text, mood, preparation, nudity alone, or a minor fragment; every required participant, action, contact, and anatomy detail for that branch is visible and unobscured; erotic or hardcore content is reinforced by at least one smaller photographic fragment without creating another event; the exact English Theme title, subject, and spot colors are unchanged; composition and final output-quality language contain no redundant aspect-ratio or dimension requirement; every visible string appears verbatim in the dedicated image-text passage between ASCII straight double quotes and has an exact physical carrier and poster location; no quotation mark of any kind appears outside that passage; every quoted string uses only permitted ASCII English characters; no other passage spells out or introduces readable copy; zero non-English glyphs appear anywhere in the rendered poster; the dominant monochrome hero, torn headline slab, subordinate photographic fragments, editorial scraps, physical paper depth, and analog print artifacts are all visible; the primary visual and title hierarchy survive thumbnail viewing; every promised word is readable; and the result describes one finished poster only. Rewrite any Frame that fails this check. Remove repeated adjectives, backstory, symbolism explanations, audience reactions, and details that do not improve hierarchy or renderability.
