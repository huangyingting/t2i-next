friend-held portrait, attention to the phone lens or preview for a front-camera

photographer, a second phone, impossible floating camera hardware, or an extra

An arm's-length front-camera selfie must never claim full-body framing: normal
arm reach without a wide-angle lens cannot include the complete body. Use a
mirror selfie, timer camera, fixed camera, or nearby friend whenever the full
body is required.

For a batch of three or more Themes, include at least one front-camera selfie
or mirror selfie, at least one nearby-friend portrait, and at least one timer

non-selfie method. Do not satisfy the whole batch with ordinary photographer

Every Theme premise must explicitly name its framing family and shooting

large-half-body front-camera selfie," or "a full-body portrait photographed by

Include enough pose to understand the activity and preserve a readable slice

neck, bust center, hands, active prop, hips, or required framing boundary.

Make the face and eyes visually dominant even when the full upper body is

shoulders, aggressive forward lean, and a face pushed into shadow.

Compose three physical depth planes: one near prop, hand, table edge, doorway,
or vehicle surface; the complete requested cast in the dominant middle plane;
and recognizable location details in a softly separated background. Use
moderate depth of field. Background bokeh may soften distant detail but must
not erase the location or blur a requested companion.

LIGHT AND COLOR

Motivate light from a real source: hard morning sun through a doorway, broad
window light, warm cafe light mixed with daylight, cool overcast courtyard
light, late-afternoon market backlight, candlelight mixed with blue window
light, or soft garage skylight. Preserve natural exposure, consistent shadow
direction, realistic highlight roll-off, and catchlights that agree with the
source.

Keep the face high-key and readable. Use broad frontal or three-quarter fill
from open sky, pale walls, tabletops, or windows so both eyes remain luminous
and shadows under the brows, nose, and chin stay soft. Hard sun may create
sparkling rim light or patterned background light, but it must not cut the
face into severe bright and dark halves. Avoid low-key lighting, heavy
chiaroscuro, deep eye sockets, muddy greens, brown-dominant grading, dramatic
underlighting, and moody cinematic contrast.

Give every Theme one dreamy but physically photographable atmosphere. Rotate
through distinct optical and environmental treatments before repeating:

- soft golden-hour backlight with a translucent rim around hair and shoulders;
- broad window light diffused through sheer curtains into a luminous cream
  veil;
- small prism refractions casting restrained pastel rainbow fragments on a
  pale wall, table, or garment edge without crossing the eyes;
- dappled flower or leaf shadows drifting softly across the background;
- bright rain droplets, condensation, or misted glass turning distant daylight
  into pearl-like bokeh;
- candlelight or warm table lamps balanced by cool blue-hour window fill;
- sunlit pollen, steam, or fine dust visible only inside one believable light
  beam;
- reflected water caustics from a pool, fountain, glass bowl, or rain puddle
  moving faintly across a background surface; or
- delicate practical fairy lights, cafe bulbs, or city lights rendered as
  soft pastel bokeh behind the subject.

The dreamy atmosphere must remain real-location photography with a visible
physical source. Keep optical effects restrained around the background,
hairline, garments, and props so the face retains natural skin color, both
eyes stay crisp, catchlights remain coherent, and anatomy is never obscured.
Favor romantic cream, blush, peach, powder blue, lavender, celadon, pale gold,
soft coral, and fresh botanical green. Do not use magical particles,
supernatural auras, fantasy scenery, heavy bloom, fog that hides the body, or
random sparkles painted directly on skin.

Favor warm skin against a restrained environment palette, with one or two
clear wardrobe accents such as black and cream, turquoise and denim, floral
red and porcelain blue, emerald and ivory, or coral and charcoal. Avoid
fantasy glow, neon cyberpunk grading, teal-orange blockbuster grading, heavy
HDR, crushed blacks, blown skin, artificial bloom, and random sparkles.

FRAME CONTRACT

Every Frame is one self-contained prose prompt for one location photograph.
Restate the exact parameter-controlled cast and every requested person's
complete visible identity, makeup or grooming, wardrobe and coverage, exact
pose, hand tasks, agency, and body support, plus the hero prop or explicit
contact, location evidence, framing family, camera position, depth planes,
light, and material realism. For every person, describe the gaze target, eye
mechanics, four-signal micro-expression, contact, and immediate material
consequence explicitly. State the concrete emotional trigger and show the
primary and secondary emotion through visible facial and physical evidence
rather than emotion labels alone. Do not rely on another Frame for identity or
context.

Every Frame must begin with these two exact sentences in this exact order:
"live-action photorealistic location portrait with natural undistorted
perspective. bright high-key East Asian lifestyle beauty portrait, soft
feminine social-media glamour, luminous ivory skin, clear large almond eyes,
clean modern digital-camera realism." Do not shorten, reorder, paraphrase,
capitalize differently, or replace either sentence.

After those two opening sentences, organize the descriptive body into these
four consecutive information blocks:

1. IDENTITY AND LOOK — exact cast; each adult's identity, face, hair, makeup or
   grooming, body, exact wardrobe and coverage; location; and one clean
   environment cue.
2. EYES AND EMOTION — each person's gaze target, coherent eye mechanics,
   source-matched catchlights, four subtle facial signals, concrete trigger,
   primary emotion, and secondary emotion.
3. POSE AND ACTION — each person's body support, upper-body gesture, both hand
   tasks, and agency; small hero prop or explicit contact; decisive action
   instant; and one immediate material consequence.
4. CAMERA AND LIGHT — flattering camera position, subject occupancy, three
   depth planes, high-key motivated light, restrained palette, and the few
   most important skin, hair, garment, prop, and location details.

Spend most of the budget on the face, eyes, micro-expression, hairstyle,
upper-body gesture, wardrobe, action contact, and flattering light. Keep
setting construction, backstory, numerical eye measurements, clinical muscle
terminology, and surface defects concise. Completeness and image-defining
detail matter more than an arbitrary word count, but do not repeat a fact after
it is visually established.

Frames within one Theme preserve the exact cast and every requested person's
identity, body, hairstyle, complete makeup or grooming design, wardrobe pieces,
coverage state, activity, pose, gaze, eye mechanics, micro-expression,
emotional trigger, primary and secondary emotion, hand contacts, and agency.
They also preserve the location, hero prop or explicit contact, time of day,
action instant, material consequence, and light. Vary only camera height,
camera side, foreground overlap, depth-plane emphasis, or which stable
location detail anchors the background.

Every Frame must stand alone. Never use same, identical, still, again, now,
before, after, previous, next, compared with, continues, remains, rather than,
or from this angle to refer to another Frame. Restate every stable fact as a
present visible fact and describe only the current emotional and physical
instant.

Before publishing, compare paired Frames field by field and rewrite any
identity, count, makeup, accessory position, clothing, framing, shooting
method, pose, hand, gaze, contact, action, consequence, location, time, or
light difference. Then scan out every cross-Frame comparison word above.
Delete negative checklist phrases before publishing.

Honor the requested output language exclusively. When the requested language
is English, do not emit Chinese characters, untranslated fragments, or mixed
language inside a Frame.

   studio-seamless, dark, gritty, low-key, brooding, or heavily cinematic.
2. Anyone seems under 25, resembles a real person, changes identity, or looks
   synthetic.
3. The cast differs from exactly `female_count` women and `male_count` men, or
   adds a person, reflection, portrait, mannequin, screen face, or body part.

   cropped away, or used as a prop.
5. Location, outfit, coverage, activity, or explicit contact is generic,
   incoherent, level-inappropriate, or changes between paired Frames.
6. A pose is impossible or unsupported; hands lack tasks; fingers, limbs,
   breasts, hair, fabric, bodies, or props merge, duplicate, intersect, float,

7. A final Frame uses wide-angle, ultra-wide, fisheye, 0.5x, action-camera,

8. Skin is plastic, waxy, airbrushed, glittered, over-sharpened, or flawless;

9. Light lacks a real source, coherent shadows and catchlights, high-key facial

   opaque sunglasses, fog, or hard hat shadow hides eyes or anatomy.
10. Themes repeat location, activity, outfit, prop, pose, face/body direction,

11. The two exact opening sentences or four information blocks are missing,

12. Eyes are crossed, misaligned, unfocused, glassy, duplicated, shadowed, or

13. Gaze, expression, or emotion lacks a target, trigger, two-emotion causal
    chain, and four visible signals, or becomes generic, contradictory,
    childlike, distressed, intoxicated, vacant, or discontinuous.
14. Action lacks grip/contact pressure, support, direction, decisive instant,
    agency, or an immediate material consequence.
15. Any immutable cast, identity, anatomy, makeup, grooming, accessory,
    wardrobe, pose, gaze, emotion, hand, contact, action, location, or light

16. A Frame uses continuity shorthand, references another Frame or time step,

    three accessories, or full intimate-area coverage; makeup lacks exact

    makeup/accessories; or a requested man's grooming/accessories are omitted.
